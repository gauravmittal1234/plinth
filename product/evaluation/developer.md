# Plinth R1 through a developer's eyes: the pitch-meeting test

Evaluators: a real-estate GTM strategist who has sold proptech to Indian developers, and a B2B product designer · 18 Oct 2026
Scope: the completed R1 design (PRD, DESIGN.md, artboards and reviews), judged from the developer's side of the table. **D1 (no developer money, ever)** is fixed.
Data: fictional only (DESIGN.md §5). Meridian Habitat stands in for the "large listed brand" (the set has none; with 11 finished projects it is closest). Persona names are invented. **[J]** marks our judgement; quoted copy is from the artboards.

---

## 1. Verdict in 5 lines

1. **Orbis Land (champion, Kestrel Park = Low):** will claim the profile at once. It will *actively* list and keep data current only if it gets a live showcase it can print on hoardings, and consented enquiries and visits delivered into its CRM. Today it gets neither. [J]
2. **Meridian Habitat (large brand, Sapphire Grove = Moderate):** will claim the profile defensively and route it to compliance. Sales won't engage: there is no volume, no CRM route, a 15-day dispute clock, and complaint counts that aren't adjusted for its size. Its answer is "we'll monitor, not list". [J]
3. **Vantage Buildcon (opponent, Coral Towers = High):** says no. Expect a legal notice and quiet pressure on CPs. The design gives it fair process but no path back and no reason to cooperate. [J]
4. **Net:** R1 is a strong due-process console and a weak developer product. It answers "can you hurt me unfairly?" well. It barely answers "what do I get?" [J]
5. **The fix, all within D1:** turn the portal into a free *Developer workspace* with four parts: record, buyers, project facts and showcase. Give developers a heads-up on *every* band change and a 2-day fast lane for clerical errors. Make three value levers real: proof they can show, commission-free direct buyers, and a fair, fast hearing.

## 2. The current pitch, and how it lands

### 2.1 The pitch the founder can give today, using only what is designed

> "Plinth rates every RERA project in Gurugram for delivery risk from public records. We never take developer money, so nobody can buy, hide or preview a score. Claim your profile free in our Evidence & Reply portal. If a fact is wrong, dispute it: we acknowledge in 48 hours and decide in 15 working days. Your reply appears next to the score. Before any downgrade into High or Severe you get 7 days' notice. Buyers can book site visits on WhatsApp and ask you for your price list, free of charge. To quote your score, use our live link."

**Screens the founder would show:** WebProject (Aurelia Heights Ph 2), WebDeveloper (Northfield's promised-vs-actual timeline), WebDevPortal (notice, dispute flow with live buyer-card preview), WebMethodology, and the WaAlerts visit flow.

**What the room hears [J]:** "You will be rated whether you like it or not; here is the complaints window." Every developer-facing feature on WebDevPortal concerns the record. The navigation reads *Overview · Projects · Notices · Dispute a fact · Public reply · Dispute log*. Nothing in it is about buyers. The pitch's only "give" is the visit and enquiry line, and no screen shows what the developer receives.

### 2.2 Where D1 helps the pitch and where it limits it

| D1 helps | D1 limits |
|---|---|
| **It can't be bought, so it's worth showing.** A Low from a payee is an ad; a Low from Plinth is evidence. Archetype-B developers have no neutral proof today [SS §2.3] | **No volume lever:** no featured slot, boost, launch banner or guaranteed leads, and early traffic is small (M12 target 80k visitors) |
| **No procurement.** No PO, IT review or contract: the sales head can say yes in the room | **No contract, no enforceable SLA.** Our promises stay one-sided unless published as a Charter with a public SLA board |
| **Protection both ways.** "Rank order is never for sale": a bigger rival can't outbid you either | **No revenue for developer success.** No account manager; a solo founder runs the dispute queue |
| **Direct buyers owe no brokerage.** Brokerage runs ~2.8–4.6% of bookings at listed NCR developers [SS §2.2] | **"Not a customer" was read as "no tools".** F-65 (CRM integration) was struck on that logic, starving the data-current loop [J] |
| **Strongest defamation defence:** no commercial motive (PRD §16) | **No carrot for weak developers**, only scrutiny, so litigation risk concentrates in archetype C |

### 2.3 Persona (a): Kavya, Head of Sales & Marketing, Orbis Land (champion)

Kestrel Park is **Low (high confidence)**: 63% built against 58% of time used, and 1 of 9 finished projects late. In Compare it shows ₹17,400/sq ft carpet all-in, against ₹18,900 for Aurelia.

**Pitch emphasis:** "You are the developer this was built for."

**Objections, verbatim style:**
- "Kestrel is Low. Can I put that on my hoardings and in my Meta ads tomorrow? With what, a copied link?"
- "A buyer books a visit through you and my site office gets a first name and a four-digit code. Who calls them to confirm? Who follows up after?"
- "If it isn't in LeadSquared, it doesn't exist for my team."
- "Your project page has none of my product. No floor plans, no specs, no brochure. You took my price list off my website without asking."
- "If my CA signs the QPR a week late, do I drop a band with no warning, and do my followers hear first?"
- "How many buyers do you actually have? Don't tell me 'trust'; tell me numbers."
- "My CPs will ask why buyers are going around them."

**What helps:** WebDeveloper's promised-vs-actual timeline (the proof Kavya can't produce herself). WebSearch's default sort, "Lowest delivery risk", with "No paid placement". WebCompare's all-in per carpet sq ft. The WebFollowing alert "QPR for Jul–Sep 2026 filed on time".

**What hurts:** WebDevPortal offers only "Quote your score only through this live link". WebProject's actions are Follow, Ask and Share, with no "Contact Orbis" or "Book a visit". WaAlerts: "the site team sees only your first name". WebPrivacy: the relay "stops working… after 30 days", though primary sales cycles run longer [J]. WebFollowing: the price list comes from "its site", not from Orbis.

**What she needs to say "yes, list and keep it current":** a live showcase (QR, link, later an embed); an enquiry and visit inbox that forwards into the CRM; a price-list and plans editor; phone-based site capture; a heads-up before any band move; monthly buyer signals.

### 2.4 Persona (b): Rohit, VP Sales, Meridian Habitat (large brand, indifferent)

Sapphire Grove Ph 1 is **Moderate (high confidence)**: 48% built against 55% of time used, and 2 of 11 finished projects late. Its price list moved from ₹1.89 Cr to ₹1.95 Cr on 16 Oct 2026, and Plinth alerted followers.

**Pitch emphasis:** "Independent proof protects premium brands from being lumped in with NCR's legacy."

**Objections:**
- "Moderate, with high confidence? Both late projects waited on authority approvals. Your method nets out COVID but not a fire NOC stuck at the department."
- "You told our followers about a price revision before my team had briefed our CPs."
- "You show raw complaint counts. We have ten times the units of a mid-size developer. Show it per 100 units or don't show it."
- "Fifteen working days to fix a wrong fact during a launch weekend? And six disputes a quarter across 20-plus phases?"
- "Our legal team reviews your methodology and terms before anyone here logs in. Who signs off at Plinth? One founder?"
- "We have thousands of CPs and Sell.Do. What does this add?"
- "Your AI answers questions about our projects. Who checks it?"

**What helps:** WebMethodology's published weights, versioning and "a methodology committee with one external member". The evidence drawer's snapshot hashes. WebDevPortal's notice "on hold while we check" and its live buyer-card preview. Replies published unedited.

**What hurts:** WebDeveloper's "Are buyers going to HARERA? 7 decided against the group" is an absolute count, though the PRD input is "per 100 units" (§6.2). The dispute quota "N of 6 disputes used" is per developer, not per phase. "Developer says · not yet verified" sits on its own head of customer relations' statement. Every phase card carries the group "Track record" reason. Nothing lets Meridian *publish* its price list; Plinth reads it.

**What he needs:** a legal pack (methodology PDF, terms, a Developer Charter, named governance); complaints normalised by size; a clerical fast lane and a quota scaled to phases; multi-user roles; a heads-up on price-list publication; a read-only feed of Meridian's own records. His realistic yes is "claim and correct", not "promote". [J]

### 2.5 Persona (c): Mr. Chaudhary, MD, Vantage Buildcon (opponent)

Coral Towers is **High (low confidence)**: 22% built against 70% of time used, and 2 extensions (Feb 2029, then Aug 2029). It is quoted at ₹1.62 Cr, ₹1.90 Cr all-in. Vantage plans a new phase in Sector 89, not yet registered.

**Pitch emphasis (realistic):** "We'll score you either way. Here is how to be heard, and how to improve."

**Objections:**
- "You're not a regulator. Who authorised you to grade us?"
- "HARERA granted those extensions. An extension is legal, and you punish us for using the law."
- "If you show 'High', bookings stop, and then the project stalls because you said it would."
- "Our new phase has a new funding partner and escrow discipline. Your model can't see that."
- "Take it down until our lawyers have reviewed it. Expect a notice."
- "We'll tell our CPs not to touch Plinth customers."
- "What exactly do I have to do to get off High?"

**What helps:** due process exists (notice, hold during the check, published reply). Every card has a "What would change this" line, and the Stall flag stays separate. AppRisk's "Only public-record events move a band. Nobody can pay to change it" makes an arbitrariness charge hard to sustain.

**What hurts:** WebDevPortal's "This is not a negotiation of our opinion." WaAlerts sends band moves to followers as they happen, and AppRisk's 1080×1350 share card spreads the band on WhatsApp. WebSearch lists Coral under "hidden by your filters". There is no turnaround evidence type (new funding, completed rescues), no legal-notice intake and no designed facts-only mode.

**What would move him from "sue" to "file on time":** a deterministic path back ("two quarters with construction ahead of sales"), a turnaround evidence lane, a fair-sounding notice, and a public SLA for Plinth itself. He will not promote Plinth. Success means no injunction, timely QPRs and a published reply. [J]

*(The new-developer edge case: Halcyon Homes (Juniper Enclave, Limited history) fears that "limited" reads as "risky". The portal has no way to add positive evidence (escrow certificates, approvals, funding tie-ups) outside a dispute.)*

## 4. The developer value proposition within D1

### 4.1 The one-page pitch (what the founder should be able to say by R1 launch)

> **Plinth: where good delivery gets believed.**
>
> Gurugram buyers no longer believe brochures. They check. Plinth is where they check: every RERA project, read from public records, by a company no developer pays. Ever. That is why a Low on Plinth means something. Nobody can buy one, so nobody can doubt yours.
>
> **What you get, free, forever, with no contract**
> 1. **Proof you can show.** A live Plinth Record for each project and for your group: a QR for hoardings and brochures, a link for WhatsApp, an embed for your site. It updates itself. Buyers trust it because you can't edit it.
> 2. **Buyers who come to you direct.** When a buyer asks for your price list or books a visit, it lands in your inbox and your CRM with a brief: configuration, all-in budget, loan status and timeline. They have already seen your record and the true all-in cost. We charge nothing per enquiry, and no one owes us brokerage.
> 3. **A fair, fast hearing.** You see every band change before buyers do. We fix clerical errors in 2 working days. Every dispute is logged and every reply is published next to the score. Our own response times are public.
> 4. **A channel for progress.** Your site engineer captures location-verified photos. Followers see them labelled "Captured on site", and the next QPR confirms them.
> 5. **Signals.** Every month: views, follows, enquiries, visits, who you are compared with, and what buyers ask about your projects.
>
> **What we ask:** claim your profile, keep your price list and plans current, file QPRs on time, and reply when you disagree.
>
> **What we will never do:** sell you placement, sell your buyers to anyone, sell a competitor placement over you, show anyone a score early, or take a rupee from you.

### 4.3 The three most important D1-compatible value levers

1. **Live, unbuyable proof (the Live Record kit).** This is the reason D1 is an asset in the pitch, not a handicap. It must be live-only, it must carry the group line and the disclaimer, and it must be identical for every developer.
2. **Commission-free, pre-qualified, consented direct buyers, delivered into their CRM.** The inbox, visit check-in and email/webhook forward cost the developer nothing and carry no brokerage. This speaks to the biggest line in a developer's cost of sale (~2.8–4.6% of bookings [SS §2.2]) without Plinth earning from it.
3. **Symmetric, fast due process with a path forward.** That means a heads-up on every band move, a 2-day clerical lane, a public SLA board for Plinth, Path back, and location-verified site capture. It turns the score from a verdict into an agenda developers can act on, and it is what keeps archetype C at the table rather than in court.

### 4.4 Trade-offs for the founder

1. **Free tools cost engineering with no developer revenue.** The R1 workspace delta is roughly 4–6 weeks for a small team [J]. The payback is buyer-side: fresher price lists, plans and progress make cost sheets, Compare and alerts more accurate.
2. **Reword F-65 without touching D1.** A generic hand-off is a free tool, not a sale, but each one is a DPDP disclosure needing a purpose-specific consent artefact.
3. **A heads-up on every band change delays buyers by up to 72 hours.** That stays inside the existing 72-hour recompute promise; don't stretch it. Red-flag rules stay immediate.
4. **Developer content dilutes "Records show".** Keep it in a labelled section, and out of reasons, ranking and AI answers except as cited "Developer says".
5. **The showcase can read as endorsement, and developers will cherry-pick Low projects.** Every widget links to "All N projects by this group". Answer misuse with a public correction note, not revocation, which would make the widget a lever.
6. **Free analytics carries privacy and "data product" optics.** Commit in a published Developer Charter: free, aggregate (k ≥ 20), identical for all.
7. **Relay extension vs privacy.** The default stays 30 days. Only the buyer can extend it.
8. **Size-fair display and a per-phase quota add dispute load** for a solo founder. Budget the M4 ops hire (PRD §16).
9. **An "On-time Register" brings PR**, but Indian real-estate awards are widely seen as pay-to-play [J]. Make it formula-only: no application, fee or event.
10. **D1 means Plinth bears verification costs.** SS suggested charging for "the cost of verifying facts". Sample-audit site captures rather than checking all of them.
