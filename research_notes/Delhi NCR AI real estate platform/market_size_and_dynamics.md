# Delhi NCR Residential (Apartment/Flat) Market: Size, Structure and Dynamics (as of October 2026, with India context)

Research date: 17 Oct 2026. Every figure is labelled with its period. Sources use different methods and are not interchangeable:
- **ANAROCK** covers primary sales in the top 7 cities, with prices quoted as base selling price (BSP) on super built-up area.
- **Knight Frank (KF)** covers primary sales in 8 cities, using a weighted average price.
- **PropEquity** and **NAR–CRE Matrix** track primary sales value.
- **Square Yards** uses IGR registrations, which include both primary and resale.

Unless stated otherwise, "sales" below means **primary (developer) sales only**.

## 1. Annual NCR residential sales volume and value, 2022 to 2026 YTD, by sub-market

### Takeaway
NCR sells about **52,000 to 62,000 new homes a year** (primary only). The count depends on the source:
- ANAROCK: 61,900 in 2024 and 57,220 in 2025.
- Knight Frank: 52,452 in 2025.

Primary sales value is about **₹1.5 to 1.6 lakh crore a year** (PropEquity: ₹1.53 lakh crore in 2024, ₹1.63 lakh crore in 2025).

In 2026 YTD, volumes are flat to down and value is falling sharply:
- H1 2026 value was ₹68,217 crore, down 24% YoY.
- Knight Frank puts Jan–Sep 2026 at 35,574 units, down 11%.
- ANAROCK's quarterly figures sum to about 42,300 units over 9M 2026, roughly flat to slightly up.

Gurugram is about 42–55% of NCR units and about 60% of value. Noida plus Greater Noida is about 28–35% of units. Delhi NCT is about 2% of primary sales.

### Inferences
- **9M 2026 by ANAROCK quarters** (15,200 + 13,350 + 13,750) comes to about **42,300 units**. I back-calculated 9M 2025 at about 40,700 (Q1 2025 of about 12,560 from the +21% YoY figure, plus 14,255 and 13,900). That makes 9M 2026 up about 4% on ANAROCK's numbers, against Knight Frank's −11%. This methodology gap matters for any sizing deck. Use a range of **about 50,000 to 60,000 primary units a year** for NCR.
- **Run-rate value for 2026** is about ₹1.3 to 1.4 lakh crore of primary sales. This annualises NAR–CRE's H1 figure of ₹68,217 crore, so it is down from about ₹1.6 lakh crore in 2025. The drop is mainly a fall in Gurugram's ultra-luxury ticket sizes, not in units.
- **Gurugram dominates value but is losing unit share** (51% to 42% of KF sales). Volume is moving to Greater Noida, Ghaziabad and Faridabad, which are cheaper end-user markets.
- **Delhi NCT is not a primary apartment market** (about 2% of NCR primary sales). Any Delhi NCT play is a resale and builder-floor play (see section 3).

### Gaps
- I could not verify ANAROCK's or Knight Frank's NCR annual unit figures for **2022 and 2023** in this session; the web search budget ran out. From memory, ANAROCK's figures are roughly 63,700 (2022) and 65,600 (2023), but these are **unverified** and should be checked against ANAROCK's December 2023 press release.
- I found no annual unit split for 2024 or 2025 by sub-market (Gurugram, Noida, Greater Noida, Ghaziabad, Faridabad, Delhi, Sonipat). Only half-year and quarterly shares were found. PropEquity sub-market annual tables were behind blocked pages.
- I found no source quantifying Sonipat, Bhiwadi or Neemrana separately; ANAROCK says Bhiwadi is about 2% of NCR inventory.
- Value by sub-market is only available for Gurugram.

## 2. New launches, unsold inventory, months of inventory, and the shift in ticket sizes

### Takeaway
NCR supply has been cut back:
- ANAROCK launches were 10,900 in Q3 2026 (down 14% YoY). Q2 2026 launches were down 40% YoY.
- Unsold inventory is about 86,000 units (ANAROCK) or about 104,000 (Knight Frank). It is roughly 18 months of sales (ANAROCK) or 7.9 quarters (Knight Frank), and the trend is improving.

The mix has shifted sharply to luxury:
- More than 55% of 2025 NCR launches were priced above ₹2.5 crore.
- In H1 2026, ₹2–5 crore homes were 60% of launches and 43% of sales.
- Stock under ₹1 crore is old and slow to sell. The ₹0–50 lakh band has about 40 quarters of inventory.

### Inferences
- Unsold NCR stock is split two ways:
  - **Sub-₹1 crore stock** is about 51,000 units and old, with QTS of 14–40 quarters. It sits mostly in peripheral Greater Noida, Ghaziabad and Faridabad projects, where an AI matching and discovery tool could help shift stock.
  - **₹2–10 crore stock** clears fast, with QTS of 1.5–4.2 quarters.
- The luxury skew compresses volume while inflating value. A platform charging a percentage of value would benefit from premiumisation. A per-lead or per-transaction model would see shrinking volumes in Gurugram.

### Gaps

- I found no NCR inventory split for ready-to-move vs under-construction.

## 3. Resale (secondary) vs primary market, and what Delhi NCT's housing stock actually is

### Takeaway
Across India's major cities, **resale is about 43% of registered residential transactions (FY2025)**, up from 38% in FY2019.

In Noida and Greater Noida, resale rose from 29% to 40% of about 30,000 registrations (FY2025). That implies roughly **12,000 resale deals a year in Noida and Greater Noida alone**.

Delhi NCT has almost no new apartment supply (about 1–2% of NCR launches and 2% of sales). Its market is mainly:
- resale of old DDA and group-housing (CGHS) flats;
- builder floors from plot redevelopment;
- a large unauthorised-colony segment.

**No reliable public data splits Delhi NCT registrations by these housing types.**

### Cited Findings
**India primary vs secondary (Square Yards, IGR data)**
- Primary was **57% and secondary 43%** of registered residential transactions in FY2025. Secondary was 38% before the pandemic (FY2019).
- Total registrations rose from **3.07 lakh (FY2019) to 5.44 lakh (FY2025)**, up 77%.
- Secondary transactions rose from **1.22 lakh to 2.33 lakh**, an 11% CAGR. Primary grew at a 9% CAGR.
- Cities covered: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Noida & Greater Noida, Pune, Thane. **Delhi and Gurugram are not covered.**
— [Square Yards Primary vs Secondary FY2025](https://static.squareyards.com/PrimaryVsSecondary-UnpackingDemandTrendsinIndia'sResidentialMarket-SquareYards.pdf); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/indias-secondary-real-estate-market-grows-to-43-share-by-fy25-square-yards/2605611/)

**Noida and Greater Noida**
- FY2025: **0.30 lakh registrations, 60% primary and 40% secondary**. FY2019: 0.23 lakh, split 71/29.
- Square Yards attributes the rise in resale to "limited new launches, past concerns over project delays, and rising prices".
- Square Yards also says the traditional resale discount "has reduced significantly".
— [Square Yards](https://static.squareyards.com/PrimaryVsSecondary-UnpackingDemandTrendsinIndia'sResidentialMarket-SquareYards.pdf)

- 2024: **5.77 lakh units worth ₹4.03 lakh crore**.
- Covers primary plus secondary in Pune, Thane, Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, **Noida, Greater Noida, Ghaziabad**. No city split was published.

## 9. Macro factors 2025–2026: rates, home loans, affordability, tax, and the 2027 outlook

### Takeaway
The RBI cut the repo rate by a cumulative **125 bps to 5.25%** in the 2025 easing cycle; the last cut was in December 2025. It then held in February, June and August 2026, and on **7 October 2026 raised the rate 25 bps to 5.50%**, shifting its stance to "calibrated tightening". This was the first hike in about three years. SBI home loans now start at **7.50%** (effective 15 Oct 2026).

Knight Frank describes India housing as **"late-cycle consolidation"**: flat volumes, rising inventory, and prices held up by incentives. ANAROCK sees a Q3 2026 rebound carrying into the festive season.

For NCR in 2027, the outlook is cautious: rate support has reversed, affordability is stretched, and luxury supply is heavy. It is partly offset by infrastructure catalysts (Jewar airport, RRTS, Dwarka Expressway).

### Cited Findings
**Rates and monetary policy**
- Knight Frank:
  - Repo **5.25% through H1 2026**, after **125 bps of cumulative easing**; the last cut took effect in December 2025.
  - The MPC held in February and June 2026, citing West Asia energy risks and a possibly deficient monsoon.
  - FY26 GDP was estimated at **7.6%**. The RBI revised FY27 GDP to **6.6%** and FY27 CPI to **5.1%** (as of mid-2026).
  - Knight Frank says "the monetary tailwind that sustained demand through 2025 has largely run its course".
  — [Knight Frank H1 2026](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf)
- RBI MPC, 5–7 Oct 2026:
  - Repo **raised 25 bps to 5.50%** (unanimous). SDF is 5.25%; MSF and Bank Rate are 5.75%.
  - The stance moved from neutral to "calibrated tightening". The August 2026 meeting had held at 5.25%.
  - CPI rose to **4.8% in August 2026** from 4.5% in July.
  - FY27 GDP is projected at 7.1% per this source, which conflicts with Knight Frank's 6.6% citation; the RBI may have revised it upward.
  — [SCC Online (9 Oct 2026)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/10/09/rbi-hikes-repo-rate-to-5-50-percent-october-2026/); [Kalkine](https://www.kalkine.co.in/article/economic/rbi-repo-rate-hike-policy-rate-reaches-550-as-lending-costs-come-into-focus)
- SBI home loan rate: **"7.50% p.a. onwards"**, effective 15 Oct 2026. — [SBI](https://sbi.bank.in/web/interest-rates/interest-rates/loan-schemes-interest-rates/home-loans-interest-rates-current)

**Affordability and incentives**
- Developers prefer "flexible payment plans, subvention schemes, stamp duty waivers, and enhanced channel partner commissions" to price cuts.
- For mid-ticket and affordable buyers, "price appreciation has outpaced the benefit of lower borrowing costs".
- Buyer caution is attributed to "structural anxiety around AI-driven shifts in white-collar employment", elevated energy prices and West Asia tensions.
— [Knight Frank H1 2026](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf)
- ANAROCK attributes the 2025 decline in top-7 volumes to "hardening property prices, layoffs in the IT sector, geopolitical tensions". — [Telangana Today/ANAROCK](https://telanganatoday.com/housing-sales-volume-down-14-pc-in-2025-in-7-cities-anarock)

**Outlook**
- ANAROCK (Q3 2026): "Festive sentiment, stable borrowing costs, and a healthy launch pipeline should carry this momentum into the closing quarter, although higher prices are likely to keep buyers selective". It also notes affordability "remains the binding constraint".
- ANAROCK's NCR Q1 2026 outlook: "sustained growth through 2026", with Gurugram leading on high-end and luxury and Noida and Greater Noida as "high-growth corridors".
— [ANAROCK Q3 2026](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/Q3_2026_Pan_India_Residential_Market_Viewpoints_9cf1a7e616.pdf); [ANAROCK NCR Q1 2026](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/NCR_Q1_2026_Residential_Market_Viewpoints_fdadfa9e4a.pdf)
- Knight Frank: if launches keep outpacing sales through H2 2026 without a recovery in absorption, "inventory pressure will intensify and developers will face increasing reliance on incentives". — [Knight Frank H1 2026](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Effect of the October 2026 hike.** ANAROCK's "stable borrowing costs" outlook was written before the hike, so it is partly overtaken. Floating-rate EMIs will rise. For NCR in 2027, expect:
  - continued pressure on volume;
  - more developer incentives, such as subvention and price protection;
  - a further shift toward ready and resale homes, where buyers can see what they are buying.
  That shift favours a resale-focused platform.
- **AI-related job anxiety** among white-collar workers matters especially in Gurugram and Noida, where IT and GCC employment anchors demand. Knight Frank explicitly names it as a 2026 buyer-caution factor.
