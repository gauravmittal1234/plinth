# Quantitative pain map for Indian consumers: official grievance data and large surveys (as of Oct 2026)

Method note: the session's web-search budget ran out early (shared across parallel researchers) and the backup search tool was not configured. Most numbers below therefore come from primary documents fetched directly: 40+ Lok Sabha answers (Budget and Monsoon sessions 2026, Winter 2025) pulled from the sansad.in question database, the CIO Annual Report 2024-25 (scanned PDF, read page by page), the India Justice Report's Consumer Justice Report 2026, PIB releases, and LocalCircles survey releases. Each figure carries its year. "FY" means April to March, and "2025" alone means calendar year 2025.

## 1. National Consumer Helpline (NCH): volumes, sector mix, growth, convergence and resolution

### Takeaway
NCH volume is rising sharply: 9.5 lakh complaints in 2022, 13.4 lakh in 2024, 20.5 lakh in FY26, and about 1.97 lakh per month in H1 2026. E-commerce is still the biggest single sector at about 5.1 lakh complaints in 2025, but its share is falling fast (39.8% in FY23, 28.2% in FY26). Most of the new growth comes from other sectors: food, travel and tourism, "agency services", electronics, telecom and consumer durables. Inside e-commerce, the categories growing fastest are non-delivery (+68%) and "refund/replacement not given as per policy" (+83%). NCH reports 100% "disposal", which is a closure metric, not a measure of whether the consumer was satisfied.

### Inferences
- E-commerce grew about 36% from FY25 to FY26 (≈4.24 lakh to ≈5.78 lakh, derived from the dataful totals and shares). Non-e-commerce complaints grew about 65% (≈8.9 lakh to ≈14.7 lakh). NCH's growth is now led by services and offline sectors: food, travel, agency services, electronics and durables, telecom and broadband.
- In Karnataka, travel and tourism nearly doubled in 2025 (+98%) and food rose 56%. Electronics (+40%), telecom (+47%) and agency services (+38%) all outgrew e-commerce (+24%), while banking fell 8% (banking complaints mostly go to the RBI Ombudsman instead).
- E-commerce pain has moved from "refund not paid" (flat or falling) to fulfilment and policy disputes: non-delivery +68%, refund/replacement denied under policy +83%, and fake/counterfeit goods ≈15.8k. Flipkart and Amazon together account for about 44% of e-commerce complaints. Quick commerce (Zepto, Blinkit) entered the top 10 in 2025.
- NCH's 100% "disposal" means a complaint was forwarded or closed, not that it was fixed. The conflicting disposal-time figures (13 to 66 days) suggest the metric is defined inconsistently.

### Gaps
- No national NCH sector-wise breakdown (all sectors, counts) for 2024, 2025 or FY26 was found. The Karnataka table is the best available proxy. The NCH dashboard (consumerhelpline.gov.in) was not accessible.
- No NCH data on consumer satisfaction after closure, or on how often convergence partners actually resolve complaints.
- "Agency Services" and "Food" are not defined in the sources. Agency services may cover LPG/gas agencies or courier services, but this is unverified.

## 2. Consumer commissions (e-Jagriti / e-Daakhil / Confonet): pendency, sector mix and time to resolution

### Takeaway
About 5.74 lakh cases were pending in consumer commissions in January 2026, three-quarters of them at district level, and 40% of bench posts were vacant. By filing volume, insurance, housing and banking dominate litigated consumer disputes. Housing is 44% of National Commission cases, and insurance is about 25% of State and District cases. Average disposal times run to 400+ days, against a statutory target of 3–5 months.

### Cited Findings
- Pending cases (Jan 2026): NCDRC 16,382; State Commissions 1,21,922; District Commissions 4,36,029; total 5,74,333. In 2025, 1,62,474 cases were filed and 1,50,197 disposed. — [Lok Sabha USQ 2069, 11.02.2026](https://fcainfoweb.nic.in/PMS/writereaddata/2026_LS_B_2069.pdf)
- Filings and disposals by year: 2021: 1,49,412 / 1,01,739 (68.1% clearance). 2022: 1,75,680 / 1,82,868 (104%). 2023: 1,74,284 / 1,85,779 (107%). 2024: 1,73,181 / 1,58,321 (91.4%). 2025: 1,62,896 / 1,50,581 (92.4%). At the 2025 disposal rate the backlog would take 3.81 years to clear. Bench vacancies were 916 of 2,286 posts (40.1%) as of 24 Mar 2026, with 48.3% of State Commission posts vacant. — [GetNyay Consumer Justice Tracker, compiling LS Q886, Q2069 and RS Q3571 of 2026](https://getnyay.in/data/consumer-justice)
- Sector shares of cases filed, 2010–2024 (Confonet/e-Jagriti):
  - NCDRC (2.71 lakh cases): housing 44.4%.
  - State Commissions (~4.25 lakh): insurance 25.1%, housing 18.7%, banking 8.7%, electricity 3.9%, medical 3.1%, "others" 26.5%.
  - District Commissions (~16.57 lakh): insurance 24.5%, banking 10.6%, housing 7.4%, electricity 7.0%, finance 3.4%, "others" 26.3%.
  - Source: [IJR Consumer Justice Report 2026 (full report)](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/IJR_Consumer%20Justice%20Report_2026_English.pdf)
- The NCDRC took 448 days and seven hearings on average to dispose of a case. 35% of cases in State Commissions were pending for more than three years, and overall pendency rose 20% in five years. Only 685 commissions serve 775 districts. — [IJR national press release, 18 Mar 2026](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/National%20Consumer%20Report%20Press%20Release_IJR%202026.pdf); [IJR full report](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/IJR_Consumer%20Justice%20Report_2026_English.pdf)
- Sector disposal times:
  - Since 2016, 82.6% of insurance cases disposed by Delhi District Commissions took more than six months.
  - Since 2013, 83% of housing cases in Bengaluru District Commissions took more than six months.
  - Since 2020, only 20% of Maharashtra insurance cases were disposed within six months.
  - More than 25% of cases in the Mumbai, Kolkata and Bengaluru District Commissions are housing cases.
  - Source: [IJR full report](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/IJR_Consumer%20Justice%20Report_2026_English.pdf)
- Andhra Pradesh State Commission, 2010–24: insurance 34%, housing 12%, banking 11%. Average disposal took 416 days, and only 25% of cases were disposed within three months. — [IJR Andhra Pradesh release](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/Andhra%20Pradesh%20Press%20Release_CJR%202026.pdf)
- e-Jagriti launched on 1 Jan 2025, merging OCMS, e-Daakhil, NCDRC CMS and CONFONET. VC hearing equipment is installed at 10 NCDRC and 35 State Commission benches. — [LS USQ 2069](https://fcainfoweb.nic.in/PMS/writereaddata/2026_LS_B_2069.pdf)

### Inferences
- Litigation data and complaint data rank sectors differently. NCH volume is led by e-commerce (low-value tickets). Disputes that consumers escalate to quasi-judicial bodies are led by insurance, housing and banking (high-value tickets). Those three are where unresolved pain is most expensive.
- A 1–1.5 year time to judgment in insurance and housing cases means the formal route fails for time-sensitive needs, such as hospital discharge or possession of a flat.

### Gaps
- No official sector-wise pendency is published (the IJR sector data covers filings, not pending stock). "Others" (about 26%) is an unspecified e-Jagriti category.
- No published sector-wise average disposal time at the all-India level.
- A search-result summary said e-Jagriti had over 2.29 lakh cases filed and 2.07 lakh disposed (a 90.75% rate) by June 2026. The original page was not fetched, so this is unverified.

## 3. RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme: volumes, categories, growth and resolution

### Takeaway
Complaints under the RBI Ombudsman framework reached 13.34 lakh in FY25 (+13.55%). Loans and advances (29.25%) and credit cards (second-highest, +20%) lead. About 85% of complaints received at the central receipt centre were "non-maintainable", which suggests consumers are confused about where to complain. FY26 figures had not been published as of Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
- FY25: 13,34,244 complaints, up 13.55% from 11,75,075 in FY24. The Ombudsman offices (ORBIOs) received 2,96,321 (+0.82%). The Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (CRPC) received 9,11,384, of which 7,76,336 were closed as non-maintainable, 1,08,331 sent to ORBIOs and 10,589 to CEPCs. — [AffairsCloud summary of the RBI Ombudsman Annual Report 2024-25, Dec 2025](https://affairscloud.com/rbis-annual-report-of-ombudsman-scheme-2024-25-complaints-up-13-55-loans-and-credit-cards-lead-grievances/); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/amp/story/banking/rbi-ombudsman-disposal-falls-to-93-in-fy25-when-to-approach-for-complaint-redressal)
- Categories, FY25: loans and advances 29.25% (the largest). Credit card complaints rose 20.04% and became the second-largest category. Mobile/electronic banking was 16.86% of the total, down 12.74%. Banks drew 81.53% of ORBIO complaints (private banks 37.53%, PSBs 34.80%) and NBFCs 14.80% (43,864). Disposal rate was 93.07% (95.10% in FY24). 62.16% of complaints were maintainable, 51.91% were resolved by settlement or mediation, and 43.36% were rejected. — [CAalley](https://www.caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/rbi-ombudsman-complaints-rise-13-6-in-fy25-led-by-loan-and-card-issues)
- ATM/debit card share fell from 14.56% (FY23) to 7.47% (FY25). Complaints per lakh accounts fell from 8.9 to 7.7, 91.22% of ORBIO complaints were filed digitally, and cost per complaint was ₹1,582. — [AffairsCloud](https://affairscloud.com/rbis-annual-report-of-ombudsman-scheme-2024-25-complaints-up-13-55-loans-and-credit-cards-lead-grievances/)
- RBI's 2025-26 actions:
  - RB-IOS 2026 issued on 16 Jan 2026, replacing RB-IOS 2021.
  - Category-specific Internal Ombudsman Directions issued 14 Jan 2026.
  - Central and state co-operative banks brought under RB-IOS from 1 Nov 2025.
  - A two-month campaign from 1 Jan 2026 to clear Ombudsman complaints pending more than a month.
  - Draft rules of 6 Mar 2026 to compensate small-value digital frauds and shorten bank processing of fraud complaints.
  - Source: [RBI Annual Report 2025-26, Ch. VI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1466)
- Digital payment-related frauds reported by banks: 13,319 cases / ₹96.65 crore (FY24) and 10,465 / ₹67.52 crore (FY25). Refunds: ₹12.38 lakh (FY24), ₹214.58 lakh (FY25) and ₹858.72 lakh (FY26). From July 2024 reporting was restricted to certain transaction types, so FY26 counts (20 cases) are not comparable. — [LS USQ 3466, 10.08.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3466_iaS46g.pdf)

### Inferences
- Credit cards and loans (including recovery and mis-selling, which sit inside these categories) are the growth hotspots in regulated finance. Mobile banking and ATM complaints are falling as rails mature.
- 85% of central complaints being "non-maintainable" (≈7.8 lakh people a year) is a large, measurable navigation failure: people do not know which forum or format to use. Complaint routing and drafting is a concrete product wedge.

### Gaps
- The RBI Ombudsman Annual Report 2025-26 (FY26 complaint volumes) had not been published or could not be found. The RBI site blocked automated PDF access with a CAPTCHA.
- No verified FY25 sub-category data on mis-selling or recovery agents. A search-result summary gave credit cards' FY25 share as 17.15% and deposit accounts' as 16.84%, but neither was verified in full text.

## 4. Insurance: Bima Bharosa (IRDAI), claim repudiation and the Insurance Ombudsman

### Takeaway
Health insurance is the most acute regulated-sector pain point by growth, severity and resolution failure combined:
- 61.5% of Insurance Ombudsman complaints (FY25).
- About 1.18 lakh Bima Bharosa health complaints (FY26).
- Health-claim complaints up 35% (FY25) and about 25% (FY26 annualised).
- Ombudsman complaints about claim repudiation roughly tripled from FY22 to FY26.
- When cases reach the Ombudsman or the courts, consumers win in most of them.

### Cited Findings
- Bima Bharosa complaints, FY2025-26: life 1,19,027 (598 pending); non-life 60,462 (3,710 pending); health 1,17,979 (5,664 pending); total 2,97,468 (9,972 pending). Unfair-business-practice grievances, including mis-selling, totalled 26,667 in FY25. — [LS USQ 1203, 27.07.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1203_Z7OCVp.pdf)
- Health-claim complaints on Bima Bharosa: 47,658 (FY24); 64,365 (FY25); 73,729 (FY26 up to Feb 2026). — [LS USQ 5816, 30.03.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU5816_xz9Ew6.pdf)
- Insurance Ombudsman complaints by stream:
  - FY23: life 19,786; general 5,444; health 25,873.
  - FY24: life 16,252; general 4,833; health 31,490.
  - FY25: life 15,431; general 5,016; health 32,655.
  - FY25 total received was 53,102 and 48,898 were disposed.
  - Source: [CIO Annual Report 2024-25, Preface and p.69](https://cioins.co.in/annualreports/AnnualReport2024-2025.pdf)
- How the 48,898 FY25 Ombudsman disposals ended:

- Ahmedabad Ombudsman, FY25: 4,314 complaints, 82% health. Claim rejections were 91.56% of cases taken up for hearing, and ₹18.67 crore was awarded. — [Gujarat Samachar, 30 Sep 2026](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/national/gujarat-insurance-complaints-4314-cases-reach-ahmedabad-ombudsman-in-2024-25-53368328074)

LocalCircles also reports "health insurance complaints increased 41% to 1,37,361 in FY2025". — [LocalCircles, Mar 2026](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/health-insurance-claim-survey). **Possible conflict** with Bima Bharosa's 1,17,979 health complaints for FY26; the scope differs or is unclear.

### Inferences
- Of Ombudsman cases that were either mediated or adjudicated in FY25, about 73% went the complainant's way ((15,191 + 9,234) / 33,362). In courts and forums, policyholders won 55% of insurance cases in FY26. Insurer first-level decisions are frequently wrong, and the cost falls on consumers who have to escalate.
- Ombudsman health complaints are growing more slowly (+3.7% in FY25) than Bima Bharosa health-claim complaints (+35%), and the stock of litigated cases keeps rising (+142% FY22–FY26). Pain is growing faster than the low-cost redress channels are absorbing it.
- Health claims (cashless approval at discharge, partial disallowance, rejection) rank first among regulated sectors on severity × growth × resolution failure.

### Gaps
- IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25/2025-26 grievance tables and claim settlement ratios by insurer were not accessed directly.
- The Ombudsman nature-of-complaint classification (statements L7G7H7 in the CIO report) was not transcribed.

## 6. Telecom (TRAI/DoT): service complaints, spam/UCC, Sanchar Saathi/Chakshu

### Takeaway
Spam is the fastest-growing complaint stream with official data. Unsolicited commercial communication (UCC) complaints rose from 13.6 lakh (2023) to 19.4 lakh (2024) to 31.1 lakh (2025): +42%, then +60%. 84% of 2025 complaints were against unregistered telemarketers, and surveys show near-universal daily exposure. Core telecom service complaints reaching TRAI are flat at about 40k a year, but surveys show about half of complainants remain unresolved.

### Cited Findings
- UCC complaints: 12,84,061 (2021); 10,83,049 (2022); 13,62,932 (2023); 19,38,757 (2024); 31,09,835 (2025). — [LS Starred Q 474, 25.03.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AS474_kHdre1.pdf)
- UCC complaints split by telemarketer type:
  - 2023: 1,39,886 against registered telemarketers, 12,22,946 against unregistered.
  - 2024: 1,91,430 registered, 17,47,327 unregistered.
  - 2025: 5,07,761 registered, 26,02,074 unregistered.
  - Source: [LS USQ 4249, 18.03.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU4249_w7m7rE.pdf)
- TRAI enforcement and DLT figures:
  - 18.8 lakh numbers disconnected after TRAI's 13 Aug 2024 direction.
  - TRAI imposed ₹153.8 crore in financial disincentives on operators for improperly closing UCC complaints.
  - Over 7.5 crore SMS and calls were blocked per day in Dec 2025, and over 31 crore were flagged as suspected spam per day.
  - Source: [LS USQ 771, 04.02.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU771_e4MwgQ.pdf)
- About 22.3 crore consumers have registered preferences on DLT; 8.3 crore SMS and calls were blocked per day in Jan 2026; government says the spoofed-call system cut spoofed calls by about 99% at launch (Oct 2024). Data on financial losses from telecom-based fraud "is not separately maintained". — [LS SQ 474](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AS474_kHdre1.pdf)
- As of 15 Jul 2026, 11.18 lakh citizen reports of suspected fraud communication on Sanchar Saathi led to 50.90 lakh mobile connections being disconnected. Over 15.75 lakh SIMs and 5.77 lakh IMEIs had been blocked on police reports by 30 Jun 2026. — [LS USQ 1484, 28.07.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1484_wSTFOV.pdf)
- Complaints received by TRAI against private mobile operators: 41,380 (2022), 41,460 (2023), 39,410 (2024). In 2024: Airtel 18,878, Jio 13,464, Vi 7,067. — [LS USQ 2828, 06.08.2025](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/185/AU2828_8eGMEI.pdf)
- LocalCircles, Dec 2025 (48,000+ responses, 349 districts):
  - Top issues: network coverage 65%; unsolicited calls/SMS 54%; data speed 29%; billing 23%.
  - 47% of complainants said their complaints were mostly or all unresolved.
  - 23% never complained because "it's not easy".
  - Unresolved rates by operator: Vi 62%, Airtel 49%, Jio 37%, BSNL 25%.
  - Source: [LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/mobile-services-consumer-experience)
- LocalCircles, Sep 2024 (71,000+ responses): 95% receive pesky calls daily and 77% get three or more a day. 96% of DND-registered users still get them. Financial services account for 66% of these calls and real estate 22%. — [LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/pesky-calls-survey-2024) (2024 data, older)

### Inferences
- Spam is now India's highest-volume consumer complaint stream that has official data (31 lakh complaints in 2025, more than the NCH's e-commerce volume). It feeds directly into fraud. Financial services and real estate are the main senders, so the industries that create spam are also high-pain sectors themselves.
- Telecom service complaints are under-reported at TRAI (only about 40k a year) because TRAI does not handle individual complaints. Survey-level unresolution (47%) is the better measure.

### Gaps
- No public Chakshu volume time series for 2025–26 beyond the cumulative Sanchar Saathi figures above. No TRAI data on broadband service complaints.

## 7. CPGRAMS (central public grievances)

### Takeaway
CPGRAMS is large: 26.15 lakh grievances in 2024 across centre and states, and 18.34 lakh for central ministries in FY26. Reported disposal is fast (13–15 days). It is mainly a government-service channel, though financial services, telecom, EPFO and railways sit inside it. Department-wise rankings were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Central ministries/departments: FY24 received 17,37,599, disposed 17,05,792, average 16 days. FY25 received 16,73,645, average 15 days. FY26 received 18,33,972, disposed 18,12,923, 83,148 pending, average 14 days. — [LS USQ 4034, 12.08.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU4034_rMyCth.pdf)
- All CPGRAMS (centre and states): 2021: 20,00,590 received. 2022: 19,18,238. 2023: 19,53,057. 2024: 26,15,321. 2025 (to 30 Nov): 20,53,859. Pendency fell from 10.23 lakh (start of 2021) to 2.62 lakh (30 Nov 2025). — [LS USQ 1647, 10.12.2025](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/186/AU1647_tE5FjD.pdf)
- 2023–2025: 51,15,713 central grievances, of which 43,56,331 (about 85%) were disposed within the prescribed time. — [LS USQ 1874, 11.02.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU1874_Y3kxoa.pdf)

### Inferences
- CPGRAMS volume grew about 34% in 2024 and about 10% in FY26. It is a state channel, but it absorbs consumer-like grievances such as banking (DFS), telecom, posts, railways and EPFO.

### Gaps
- Department-wise volumes for 2025–26 (for example, which ministries top the list) and satisfaction/appeal rates ("Poor" ratings) were not found.

## 8. Cyber fraud (I4C / NCRP / CFCFRMS)

### Takeaway
Cyber financial fraud is the most severe pain by money lost: about 65.9 lakh complaints and ₹55,050 crore reported lost in 2021–25. Resolution is very weak: about 15% of reported money is put under lien, under 2% of the liened amount is actually refunded (2023–25), and about 3% of complaints become FIRs.

### Cited Findings
- 2021–2025: more than 65,89,201 financial-fraud complaints on NCRP. More than ₹55,050 crore reported, more than ₹8,189 crore marked as lien, and more than 1,95,760 FIRs. — [LS USQ 251, 21.07.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU251_hTDkTd.pdf)
- 2023–2025: more than 50,11,083 cyber-fraud complaints. Lien of more than ₹7,788 crore, but only more than ₹132 crore refunded. — [LS USQ 1603, 28.07.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1603_6Nenau.pdf)
- Cumulative to 30 Jun 2026: more than ₹11,158 crore "saved" across more than 32.80 lakh complaints via CFCFRMS. The Suspect Registry (from Sep 2024) has 30.48 lakh identifiers and has shared 32.08 lakh mule accounts, declining transactions worth ₹25,698 crore. — [LS Starred Q 337, 11.08.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS337_x3tuGy.pdf)
- Delhi 2025: 4,003 cyber financial-fraud cases; ₹849.80 crore reported; ₹112.76 crore recovered (about 13%). — [LS SQ 337](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS337_x3tuGy.pdf)
- NCRB cyber-crime FIRs: 50,035 (2020), 52,974 (2021), 65,893 (2022), 86,420 (2023), 1,01,928 (2024). — [LS USQ 251 (NCRB Crime in India)](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU251_hTDkTd.pdf)
- Survey evidence on UPI fraud:
  - LocalCircles, Mar–Jun 2025 (32,000+ responses): 1 in 5 families experienced UPI fraud in three years, and 51% of victims filed no complaint.
  - Of those who did complain, 38% used the cyber portal, 25% the UPI app and 13% their bank.
  - Source: [LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/upi-fraud-complaint)
  - LocalCircles, May 2023 (older): 39% of families had faced online financial fraud in three years; only 24% recovered money and 70% got no resolution. — [LocalCircles 2023](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/financial-fraud-survey-india)

### Inferences
- Fraud is the deepest money-loss pain, but it is not a "sector" that a consumer platform can serve the way it would serve a market. The addressable opportunities lie next to it: recovery navigation (filing within the golden hour, follow-up on liens), fraud-safe transaction layers, and spam-driven acquisition in finance and real estate.

### Gaps
- Separate annual NCRP complaint counts and amounts lost for 2024, 2025 and 2026 were not isolated from the cumulative figures.

## 10. Where complaints are growing fastest and where resolution is worst: the pain map

### Takeaway
Combining volume, growth, severity and resolution failure, the top-tier unresolved pain sits in five areas:
1. Health insurance claims.
2. Housing/real estate. Highest value per dispute and the slowest redress, but almost no current official complaint data.
3. Credit (loans and credit cards) plus banking access.
4. Spam and fraud. Fastest growth and largest losses, but structurally hard to "fix" with a product.
5. Consumer durables and electronics after-sales.
E-commerce has the highest raw volume, but its tickets are lower-severity and it already has the most redress capacity.

### Cited Findings
- Fastest-growing official complaint streams (latest year):
  - UCC/spam: +60% (2025). — [LS SQ 474](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AS474_kHdre1.pdf)
  - NCH total: +56% (FY26). — [Dataful](https://dataful.in/datasets/20716/)
  - E-commerce "refund/replacement not as per policy": +83%, and non-delivery: +68% (2025). — [LS USQ 4041](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU4041_1jbDze.pdf)
  - Karnataka travel complaints: +98% (2025). — [LS USQ 3040](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU3040_MV7JFl.pdf)
  - Bima Bharosa health-claim complaints: +35% (FY25). — [LS USQ 5816](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU5816_xz9Ew6.pdf)
  - Credit card complaints to the RBI Ombudsman: +20% (FY25). — [CAalley](https://www.caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/rbi-ombudsman-complaints-rise-13-6-in-fy25-led-by-loan-and-card-issues)
  - NCRB cyber crimes: +18% (2024). — [LS USQ 251](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU251_hTDkTd.pdf)
- Worst resolution, by objective measures:
  - Cyber fraud: refunds are about 1.7% of the liened amount (2023–25). — [LS USQ 1603](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1603_6Nenau.pdf)
  - Housing disputes: 448 days at the NCDRC, and 83% of Bengaluru housing cases take more than six months. — [IJR](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/IJR_Consumer%20Justice%20Report_2026_English.pdf)
  - Insurance: 73% of mediated or adjudicated Ombudsman outcomes favour complainants. — [CIO AR 2024-25](https://cioins.co.in/annualreports/AnnualReport2024-2025.pdf)
  - Telecom: 47% of complainants are unresolved. — [LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/mobile-services-consumer-experience)
  - Banking: 85% of central RBI complaints are non-maintainable. — [AffairsCloud](https://affairscloud.com/rbis-annual-report-of-ombudsman-scheme-2024-25-complaints-up-13-55-loans-and-credit-cards-lead-grievances/)
- Real estate complaint data is not centrally maintained by MoHUA. RERA annual reports are a state matter, and the Union government says the information sought "is not centrally maintained". — [LS Starred Q 297, 12.03.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AS297_KGrvsO.pdf)
- Aviation: the AirSewa / Passenger Assistance Control Room took 64,641 grievances in 1 Jan–30 Jun 2026 and reports 99.85% "resolved". Category counts to 9 Feb 2026: ticketing and fare 16,634; flight delays 8,498; baggage 5,102; refunds 1,153 via social media/calls. — [LS SQ 172, 30.07.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AS172_nVwjHV.pdf); [LS SQ 198, 12.02.2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AS198_UGRDZt.pdf)

### Inferences
**Pain map (analyst synthesis; scores 1–5 are relative judgements based on the cited data above, not official figures)**

| Rank | Sector | Volume (latest official) | Growth | Severity (₹/life impact) | Resolution failure | Product/AI winnability (for a solo founder) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Health insurance claims | 1.18L Bima Bharosa health complaints (FY26); 32.7k Ombudsman (FY25, 61.5%); about 25% of district consumer cases | 5 (+35% FY25; repudiation complaints to Ombudsman 3x FY22–26) | 5 (hospital discharge, ₹ lakhs) | 5 (73% of Ombudsman outcomes favour complainant; 55% in courts; 50% wait 6–48h) | High: claim-file prep, pre-auth tracking, appeal drafting, Ombudsman filing |
| 2 | Housing/real estate (builder delays, possession, refunds, RWA services) | 44% of NCDRC filings; 18.7% State; RERA data not centralised | 3 (no reliable series) | 5 (life savings) | 5 (448 days NCDRC; 83% of Bengaluru cases >6 months) | Medium: buyer due-diligence, RERA complaint navigation, society-maintenance ops (lifts: 46% stuck) |

| 4 | Spam/UCC to fraud | 31.1L UCC complaints (2025); 65.9L fraud complaints 2021–25 | 5 (+60% UCC) | 5 (₹55k crore lost) | 5 (about 1.7% of lien refunded; FIRs 3%) | Low to medium: structural/regulatory problem; recovery-assist possible |
| 5 | Consumer durables, electronics and repair/after-sales | Mobile screens 8.7k; TV/AC/fridge warranty about 6.9k (2025); Karnataka electronics +40% | 4 | 3 | 4 (only 24% use brand AC service; 30% could not get brand help, 2022) | High: multi-brand service marketplace with quality guarantees |
| 6 | Travel, tourism and airlines | NCH #2 in refunds (₹10+ crore); 64.6k AirSewa grievances in H1 2026 | 5 (Karnataka travel +98%) | 3 | 3 | Medium: refund/claim automation |
| 7 | E-commerce and quick commerce | 5.11L NCH (2025), the largest sector | 3 (+16%) | 2 | 3 (non-delivery +68%; policy refusals +83%) | Low for a solo founder: incumbents dominate |
| 8 | Telecom service/broadband | About 40k TRAI complaints/year; broadband 4.4k Karnataka | 2 | 2 | 4 (47% unresolved per survey) | Low |
| 9 | Private schools and coaching (fees, refunds) | No official series; coaching refunds ₹1.56 crore for 600+ students | n/a | 4 | 5 (2% say states cap fees) | Low to medium: regulatory/advocacy-heavy |
| 10 | Private hospital billing | No official series | n/a | 5 | 4 (only 14% report no issues) | Medium: bill audit and estimate transparency |

- The quadrant that looks best for an experience-led, AI/product-led entrant has three properties: high severity, high resolution failure, and a process that is document-heavy and can be standardised. Health insurance claims, credit/banking disputes, housing transactions and appliance after-sales fit this best. Spam and fraud have the highest pain but the lowest controllability. E-commerce has the highest volume but is dominated by incumbents.
- **"No good option exists" signals:**
  - Schools: 9 in 10 parents say government efforts are ineffective.
  - Appliance servicing: consumers are leaving brand service for unorganised local technicians (44%) despite quality concerns.
  - Telecom: even the best operator leaves 25–37% of complainants unresolved.
  - Insurance: insurer-level redress is routinely overturned.
  - These are indirect proxies, not direct "satisfaction with available providers" questions.

### Gaps
- No survey found that directly asks "is there a good provider available" or gives sector-level NPS across Indian consumer sectors in 2025–26.
- National NCH sector-wise volumes for FY25/FY26 are missing beyond e-commerce, which limits precision in the volume column. Real estate has no current national complaint series. RBI FY26 and SEBI, EPFO and Income Tax grievance data are missing.
- If more search capacity becomes available, the highest-value follow-ups are:
  - The DoCA/NCH annual sector table for FY26.
  - The RBI Ombudsman Annual Report 2025-26 (expected around Dec 2026–Jan 2027).
  - IRDAI Annual Report 2025-26 grievance tables.
  - State RERA complaint totals (UP, Maharashtra, Haryana, Karnataka).
  - Redseer or KPMG CX index releases.
