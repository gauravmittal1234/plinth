## Q1. How common and how long are delays and stalls in NCR and India? How did delay rates change before RERA (pre-2017) vs after?

### Takeaway
NCR is the single worst market in India for stalled and delayed housing. It held about 52% of all stuck or delayed units in the top 7 cities and about 66% of completely stalled units (ANAROCK, 2021). In the post-RERA launch cohort it also has the lowest completion rate (74% vs 86% for the top 7 cities). RERA measurably reduced delays: the best academic estimate is about 5 months less delay for RERA-registered projects, against a pre-RERA average delay of about 14 months. RERA did not remove the legacy stock, which is concentrated in projects launched in 2010–2014.

### Inferences
- NCR's base rate for "materially delayed" is high even after RERA. About 26% of NCR's H2 2017–2018 RERA launches were still incomplete about 5–6 years after launch (ANAROCK 2023), and a typical large NCR project takes about 5.4 years. A score's "on time" baseline for NCR should be calibrated well below the national average.
- PropEquity's 2024 NCR figures sum to about 1.9 lakh stalled units: Gurugram, Noida, Greater Noida (Sept report), Ghaziabad, Faridabad and Delhi. That is roughly 38% of India's 5.08 lakh. Gurugram has nearly as many stalled projects as Noida and Greater Noida combined, so the risk is not only a UP or authority-lease story.
- The Agarwal et al. estimate (about 14 months mean delay, about 5 months RERA effect) is the most rigorous quantitative anchor found. A model could use about 14 months as the pre-RERA prior mean delay. The RERA effect is heterogeneous: smaller for luxury, larger for affordable.
- The 2026 force-majeure extensions (West Asia) and GRAP winter halts mean RERA "revised completion dates" in 2026 are systematically pushed out. A score should separate regulator-granted blanket extensions from project-specific Section 6 extensions.

### Gaps
- No public, comprehensive statistic was found for the share of HARERA or UP RERA projects that sought Section 6 extensions. Regulators publish individual extension orders only. This would need to be built by scraping the RERA portals.
- A Liases Foras figure of more than 23 lakh houses "running behind schedule" appeared in a search summary, but its date and source page could not be verified.
- No study was found that directly compares NCR project-level delay distributions before and after RERA using RERA QPR data.

## Q2. Which factors predict delay or stall, and how important is each?

### Takeaway
Across the evidence, stalls (as opposed to ordinary delays) are driven mainly by developer financial distress and fund diversion. Typical signs are cash collected far ahead of construction, buyer money used for land or other debt, and authority land dues. Developer concentration and a poor track record amplify this, as do a boom-era (2010–2014) launch cohort, NCR authority-lease land, and large multi-tower projects. Litigation and environmental orders cause long but often survivable delays. Macro shocks (COVID, GRAP, West Asia) cause shorter, system-wide delays that regulators routinely extend, so they are better modelled as timeline adjustments than as stall predictors.

### Inferences
**How a stall typically unfolds.** The mechanism common to almost every NCR stall case is the same. The developer collects most of the price early, through construction-linked or subvention plans. That cash goes to land, new launches, sister entities or other debt. Construction then lags collections, followed by lender default or NCD default and an RC or NCLT filing. Features that measure this cash-versus-progress gap should carry the most weight:
- % of units sold vs % construction complete, from RERA QPRs
- collections vs escrow withdrawals
- unpaid authority dues
- lender defaults and ratings

**Suggested relative importance for a project or developer score.** This weighting is judgement synthesised from the evidence above, not a fitted model.

| Tier | Factors | Evidence strength | Main outcome predicted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (highest) | Developer financial distress: rating at BB or below, "Issuer not cooperating", missed NCD or bank payments, Section 7 or 9 petitions, unexecuted RERA RCs | Strong (CRISIL default tables; Supertech, Jaypee, Ansal, Vatika, Mahagun) | Stall or insolvency |
| 1 | Collections far ahead of physical progress; escrow or fund-diversion findings; subvention-heavy sales | Strong (OSB, Mahira, Amrapali, Unitech, CBI subvention probe) | Stall |
| 1 | Developer's past record: prior insolvency episodes, RERA revocations, delayed projects, repeat RC defaulter | Strong (Raheja 4 episodes, HRERA defaulter list) | Stall or long delay |
| 1 | Unpaid authority land dues (Noida, GNIDA, YEIDA) or lease-condition breaches | Strong, NCR-specific (Rs 44,700 crore dues; package non-compliance; Lotus Panache sealing; Arena) | Stall, no OC, no registry |
| 2 | Launch cohort 2010–2014 / pre-RERA / not RERA-registered | Strong (ANAROCK, Agarwal et al.) | Delay or stall |
| 2 | Project scale (very large multi-tower townships) and many concurrent projects | Moderate (ANAROCK completion times; PropEquity 2012; HARERA policy) | Delay |
| 2 | Segment: fixed-price affordable (Haryana AHP) and luxury | Moderate (ThePrint; Agarwal et al.) | Delay or stall |
| 2 | Land title, acquisition and environmental litigation (Shahberi, Okhla, sports city, building-plan violations) | Moderate | Long delay, rarely full loss |
| 3 | Macro shocks: GRAP, COVID, material and labour (West Asia 2026) | Moderate, system-wide | Months of delay, usually covered by blanket extensions |

- Listed or "national" developer status is mainly protective because it proxies for low leverage, access to capital and reputational cost. It should be a moderate positive, not a free pass; listed Unitech and Jaypee both failed.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study was found that isolates the effect of JDAs, landowner disputes or change of promoter on delay in NCR. HARERA treats landowners as co-promoters, but no outcome data was found.
- No data was found on contractor quality (EPC contractor identity) as a predictor in Indian residential projects.
- No empirical study was found on unsold inventory or sales velocity as a predictor of project stall in NCR. It is intuitive and used by rating agencies, but un-sourced here.
- No public analysis was found of MCA charge filings (new or modified charges, INC-related filings) as an early warning in Indian real estate. This is plausible but untested.
- No direct study was found measuring how many days of GRAP Stage III/IV construction bans occur per year, or how much they delay projects.

## Q3. Case histories: what warning signs were visible 1–3 years before buyers were hurt?

### Takeaway
In nearly every major NCR failure, public warning signs appeared years before insolvency or court takeover. The usual sequence was: losses or a credit downgrade, then missed debt payments (banks, NCDs, LIC, fixed deposits), then visible construction slowdowns and buyer protests, then RERA or court orders and unexecuted RCs, then NCLT. Lead time from the first rating, default or fixed-deposit event to formal insolvency or takeover ranged from about 15 months (Jaypee) to more than 6 years (Unitech, Ansal API).

- IREO has Rs 24 crore, Parsvnath Rs 74 crore and ILD Millennium Rs 8.59 crore of unexecuted HRERA RCs. 71 firms are on the Gurugram defaulter list, and many orders have gone unexecuted for over 4 years (Sep 2026). — [The Financial World](https://www.thefinancialworld.com/ansal-raheja-vatika-top-%E2%82%B9446-cr-gurugram-hrera-defaulters/)
- Unitech: 15,000 buyers saw construction restart after 12 years. — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/in-a-relief-to-15-000-buyers-unitech-begins-construction-after-12-years-606164/amp)

### Inferences
**Earliest observable signals, roughly in the order they appeared across cases:**
1. Losses or a credit downgrade to BB, D or INC. Supertech's "D" came about 3.4 years before NCLT. Jaypee's losses came about 2 years before CIRP.
2. Missed payments on fixed deposits, NCDs or bank loans, often to NBFCs, trustees or LIC. These came about 1–2 years before CIRP: Jaypee, Vatika, Mahagun, Ansal API.
3. Construction progress falling behind collections, buyer protests, and RERA complaints piling up.
4. RERA refund or interest orders not complied with, then RCs that stay unexecuted.
5. Unpaid authority dues, sealing, or plan-revalidation refusals.
6. Repeat insolvency petitions across different projects of the same group (Raheja).
7. Agency action (ED, CBI, EOW) is usually late. It confirms the stall rather than predicting it.

**Using the signals.** A "watchlist" trigger could use any two Tier-1 signals within 24 months. Its lead time would be about 1–3 years before formal insolvency in most cases above.

**Repeat behaviour.** Raheja, Ansal and Unitech had chronic distress over a decade or more, so developer-level history should decay slowly in the score.

**Positive turnarounds.** Mahagun's settlement and Lotus Panache's buyer-led completion show that distress does not always mean permanent loss. Rescue probability needs its own component (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No reliable sourced timelines were found for Today Homes (Gurugram), Orris, Logix (Noida), Ireo (beyond RC dues and ED references) or Gaursons. Gaursons in particular was asked about as a possible "positive turnaround", but no evidence was found either way.
- No source was found on Amrapali's pre-2018 warning signs (dated buyer protests, NCLT petitions in 2017, or brand-ambassador exits); these were not verified.
- RERA QPR-based construction progress histories for these cases (for example, % complete by quarter) are not available in news. They would need to be scraped from the UP RERA and HARERA portals.

## Q4. Positive signals: what distinguishes reliable deliverers?

### Takeaway
Evidence for "reliable deliverer" status is mostly indirect: market-share consolidation toward listed and national developers, investment-grade ratings with strong collections, and specific on-time or early delivery records (for example, Signature Global's affordable projects). No independent, systematic on-time-delivery league table for NCR developers was found. A score will have to build one from RERA OC dates versus original RERA completion dates.

### Cited Findings
- ANAROCK: the share of listed and leading developers in top-7-city sales doubled from 17% to 34% over about six years to H1 FY24. Consolidation was driven by stricter regulation and buyer preference. — [ANAROCK/NAREDCO 2024 (PDF)](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/Real_Estate_Unboxed_The_Modi_Effect_5ef38a29b7.pdf)
- ANAROCK (Jun 2026): national developers' share of NCR new supply rose from 3% (about 700 of 25,355 units, 2022) to 13% (about 8,100 of 61,775 units, 2025). Within that, Godrej had 47%, Prestige 27% and Sobha 10%. By geography it was Gurugram 47%, Ghaziabad 27%, Noida 13% and Greater Noida 12%. — [ConstructionXperts](https://constructionxperts.co.in/from-3-to-13-supply-share-national-developers-quadruple-down-on-ncr/)
- Signature Global delivered its first three Haryana affordable projects as follows: Solera (1,000 units, Oct 2018), Synera (820, Nov 2019) and Andour Heights (980, Mar 2020). It handed over Sunrise (Karnal) 6 months early and targets 36–42 months against the policy's 48. — [Realty n More](https://realtynmore.com/signature-global-delivers-sunrise-before-time/); [Realty n More](https://realtynmore.com/signature-global-yet-again-delivers-before-time/); [MediaBulletins](https://mediabulletins.com/business-world/signature-global-received-occupation-certificate-for-2200-affordable-housing-flats-the-company-plans-to-deliver-another-5500-flats-within-fy-2021-22/)
- ThePrint (Sep 2025) reported Signature Global had undertaken 22 affordable projects and delivered 12. The company had grown faster than its delivery count, which is consistent with a growing pipeline rather than delays, but this was not verified. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/gurugram-affordable-housing-isnt-affordable-for-builders-or-buyers/2746570/)
- ICRA (FY2026) rationales for large listed developers show strong collections and comfortable leverage. Examples: Prestige pre-sales up 64% and collections up 51% YoY in FY2026; total external residential debt/CFO expected at 2.0–2.5x by Mar 2027 for some issuers. — [ICRA rationale](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRationalReportFilePdf?id=144026); [ICRA rationale](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRationalReportFilePdf?id=145044)
- CRISIL: issuers rated AA or AAA have near-zero 1–3 year default rates (AA 3-year CDR 0.14%; AAA 0%). — [CRISIL FY2026 study (PDF)](https://www.crisilratings.com/content/dam/crisil/our-analysis/publications/default-study/crisil-ratings-annual-default-and-ratings-transition-study-fy-2026.pdf)
- ANAROCK's chairman said larger, better-capitalised developers are taking over smaller players' languishing projects, and smaller developers "in for a quick buck" have exited under RERA. — [DNA](https://www.dnaindia.com/mumbai/report-mumbai-has-more-stalled-projects-but-ncr-has-3x-pending-inventory-2776772)

### Inferences
- Positive features likely to predict on-time delivery:
  - Investment-grade rating (A or above) and a low external debt/CFO multiple.
  - A high ratio of collections to construction spend.
  - Most past projects reaching OC within the RERA date; this should be computed, not assumed.
  - No unexecuted RERA RCs and no NCLT petitions.
  - Freehold or fully paid-up land, with no authority dues.
  - Listed or national-brand status.
  - Moderate project size, phased launches, and absence of subvention schemes.
- Being listed is protective but not sufficient: Unitech and Jaypee Infratech were listed. Rating, cash-flow and track-record features should dominate over the listing flag.

### Gaps
- No verified, systematic delivery-record data was found for DLF, Godrej, Sobha, Max Estates or Experion (for example, % of NCR projects delivered by the RERA date, or average months early or late). Company claims exist but were not independently verified. This must be computed from RERA OC data.
- No study was found that quantifies on-time rates of listed vs unlisted developers in NCR.

## Q5. How effective are post-RERA and IBC remedies for buyers, and how should a score treat "rescue probability"?

### Takeaway
Remedies exist but are slow and partial:
- RERA refund and interest orders are often not executed. UP RERA had recovered only about 20% of RC value by Sep 2023, and Gurugram still had Rs 446 crore unexecuted in Sep 2026.
- IBC resolutions take about 600–750 days on average. Homebuyers fare relatively well in real estate resolution plans, often 75–100% of claims mostly in kind, against lenders' heavy haircuts, but resolution is slow and sometimes reversed on appeal.
- Rescue vehicles (SWAMIH about 61,000 homes; NBCC for Amrapali about 25,000 and now Supertech; UP's Amitabh Kant package) have rescued a minority of stuck units after 5–15 years.

"Rescue probability" should be a separate component that modifies expected loss, not the main score.

### Cited Findings
**RERA recovery certificates (RCs)**
- UP RERA, Sep 2023: RCs worth Rs 3,105 crore issued for 8,575 buyers. Rs 630.70 crore was recovered (about 20%) for 3,421 complainants, plus Rs 338.17 crore settled amicably in 1,185 cases. 3,470 projects were registered. — [propnewstime, 5 Sep 2023](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MjY2MQ==/up-rera-provides-relief-to-homebuyers-with-rs-3-105-crore-in-recovery-certificates)
- UP RERA, Jul 2025:
  - Rs 1,410 crore recovered in 5,700 matters. Annual recoveries were Rs 380 crore (2023), Rs 463 crore (2024) and Rs 251 crore (2025 to mid-July).
  - About 1,650 RCs were settled mutually.
  - Total discharged or settled: 15,850 complainants and Rs 5,180 crore, including settlements.
  - [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/business/up-rera-recovered-rs-1410-crore-against-5700-recovery-certificates-so-far-1503462359.html); [UNI](https://uniindia.com/up-rera-recovers-rs-1410-crores-against-5700-recovery-certificates/business-economy/news/3525600.html) (both pages blocked; figures from search summary)
- HRERA Gurugram, 30 Sep 2026: the district administration froze bank accounts of 71 firms to recover Rs 446 crore of unexecuted RCs, many pending for more than 4 years. Largest: Ansal Rs 91 crore, Raheja Rs 90 crore, Vatika Rs 80 crore, Parsvnath Rs 74 crore, Ramprastha Rs 57 crore, IREO Rs 24 crore. — [The Financial World](https://www.thefinancialworld.com/ansal-raheja-vatika-top-%E2%82%B9446-cr-gurugram-hrera-defaulters/)
- RERA Section 7 revocation is used for diversion cases (Mahira, Mar 2024), but the outcome for buyers is uncertain. The HARERA chairman hoped for "some viable option to complete the projects". — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/rera-revokes-all-5-affordable-housing-projects-of-mahira-homes-in-gurugram-602242)

**IBC / NCLT outcomes**
- IBBI (to Mar 2025):
  - Creditors realised Rs 3.89 lakh crore under resolution plans, against claims of Rs 11.87 lakh crore. That is a haircut of about 67% against admitted claims, or about 170% of liquidation value.
  - The 1,194 resolved CIRPs took 597 days on average. The 2,758 liquidation orders took 508 days.

  - Real estate made up 18.8% of all IBC realisations.

- UP Amitabh Kant package: partial compliance (Noida: 22 of 57 builders paid 25% by Apr 2026). — [the420.in](https://the420.in/noida-builders-dues-auction-unsold-properties-amitabh-kant-panel/)

### Inferences
**Suggested structure.** Model expected outcome as P(on time) / P(delay) / P(stall), and conditional on stall, model a separate P(rescue within N years). Rescue probability is higher when:
- the project is physically advanced (SWAMIH-type funding needs near-completion and positive net worth);
- buyers are organised: 100+ allottees or 10% can file Section 7, and buyer associations have completed projects such as Lotus Panache;
- the land is freehold or authority dues are resolvable. Courts now strip penal interest (Earth Infra), and the UP package offers a zero period;
- unsold inventory or FAR can be monetised (NBCC–Amrapali sold 6,686 units);
- a credible resolution applicant exists (Suraksha for Jaypee).

**Time cost.** Even successful rescues imply 5–15 years from launch to possession: Amrapali launches about 2010, possession 2024–25; Jaypee promised 2011–12, OCs 2025–26; Earth abandoned 2014, plan restored 2026. For a buyer, "rescued" still means severe delay. The score should not treat a rescue as recovering on-time value.

**Weak refund enforcement.** UP RERA recovered about 20% of RC value by 2023, and Gurugram's RCs sit unexecuted for 4+ years. A refund-with-interest order should be valued at a steep discount. In practice, a high count of unexecuted RCs is better used as a risk signal for the developer's other projects.

### Gaps
- No IBBI breakdown was found of real-estate-only recovery rates for homebuyer claims or real-estate-only CIRP durations for 2024–2026. An IBBI committee reportedly made 155 recommendations on real estate insolvency in April 2026, but the report itself was not fetched.
- No authoritative data was found on overall compliance with the 70% escrow rule (for example, audits finding violations across projects). Evidence is case-based only (Mahira, OSB).
- No count was found of UP RERA Section 8 takeovers (projects handed to allottee associations or a new promoter), or of their completion rates.
- No statistic was found on the share of SWAMIH-funded projects located in NCR.

## Q6. What machine-learning and statistical models exist for predicting construction delays or developer defaults?

### Takeaway
The published machine-learning work on construction delay mostly uses small survey or expert-rated datasets (dozens to a few hundred projects) and reports high but non-generalisable accuracies (about 70–95%). Its features are mostly managerial and site factors, not the financial and regulatory signals that drive NCR stalls. The most useful quantitative anchors for an NCR developer and project score are econometric: Agarwal et al.'s project-level delay panel and RERA effect, CRISIL's rating-to-default tables, and China presale default-spillover evidence. No published model was found that predicts Indian project delay or stall from RERA QPR, MCA or RERA-order data.

### Cited Findings
- Yaseen et al. (2020, *Sustainability*): a Random Forest classifier optimised with a Genetic Algorithm (RF-GA) was trained on questionnaire-quantified delay sources from past construction projects. It achieved 91.67% accuracy, kappa 87% and classification error 8.33%, beating plain RF. — [Crossref DOI 10.3390/su12041514](https://doi.org/10.3390/su12041514); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/12/4/1514)
- Patel et al. (2024, *ASCE Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction*): an ANN predicts delay occurrence in disputed real estate projects using about 180 past judgments from five Gujarat municipal corporations. — [DOI 10.1061/JLADAH.LADR-1086](https://doi.org/10.1061/JLADAH.LADR-1086)
- Sahu et al. (2026, *International Journal of Construction Management*): ML, deep-learning and hybrid models on data from 68 Indian construction projects. A CNN–BiLSTM hybrid reportedly reached 95.56% accuracy and 95.58% F1 (accuracy from search summary; abstract not fetched). — [DOI 10.1080/15623599.2026.2619678](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15623599.2026.2619678); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400564536_AI-powered_delay_risk_prediction_in_construction_projects_leveraging_machine_learning_deep_learning_and_hybrid_models)
- Other studies in search summaries reported XGBoost at about 71% accuracy and SVM at about 79% for delay classification, with features such as project type, phase, district, planned duration, budget and start date, and 24 delay factors drawn from expert surveys. The underlying papers were not fetched, so attribution is uncertain. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393624556_Predicting_Delays_and_Cost_Overruns_in_Construction_Projects_A_Machine_Learning_Approach); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399404642_Machine_Learning-Based_Assessment_of_Construction_Project_Delays)
- Doloi et al. (2012): factor analysis and regression on Indian survey data. Planning, finance and site management dominate; client influence is the strongest factor in the SEM model. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251542658_Analysing_factors_affecting_delays_in_Indian_construction_projects)
- Agarwal et al. (2024): a difference-in-differences design on 13,357 Indian projects. Project-level variables include location, developer, segment, a "project score", RERA status, delay (months), unit count, unit size and price. The delay outcome falls about 5.1 months with RERA. — [ABFER PDF](https://abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2024/annual-conference/papers-household/AC24P7045-Does-Regulating-Developers-Democratize-Credit-and-Homeownership.pdf)
- CRISIL rating-based cumulative default rates (FY16–26) give off-the-shelf probability-of-default priors by rating band (see Q2). — [CRISIL (PDF)](https://www.crisilratings.com/content/dam/crisil/our-analysis/publications/default-study/crisil-ratings-annual-default-and-ratings-transition-study-fy-2026.pdf)
- Zhang (2026, *JUE Insight*, SSRN): staggered difference-in-differences on Chinese presale data. A developer default lowers neighbouring projects' sell-through by about 5 percentage points within 5 km, which supports "nearby distress" as a feature. — [SSRN DOI 10.2139/ssrn.6312634](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6312634)
- Financial-distress prediction for listed property firms (Indonesia; 38 firms, 2018–2020; logistic regression) found debt-to-equity, sales growth and operating capacity not significant. Small samples limit inference. — [IJCSRR 2022](https://doi.org/10.47191/ijcsrr/v5-i7-62)

### Inferences
- **Label design.** For NCR, the most informative model would be supervised on RERA-era outcomes: OC date vs original RERA completion date, Section 6 extensions, revocations and NCLT admissions. Features fall into three groups:
  1. Developer level: rating and rating actions; NCLT, IBBI and NCD defaults; RERA complaints, orders and unexecuted RCs; count of delayed live projects; group-entity count; authority dues.
  2. Project level: QPR construction % vs % sold and % collected; size and towers; launch cohort; segment and price; land tenure (authority lease vs freehold); litigation flags; subvention use.
  3. Environment: micro-market stalled-project density; GRAP days; blanket force-majeure extensions.
- **Model choice.** Gradient-boosted trees or survival models (time-to-OC with censoring) suit this data better than the small-N neural nets in the literature. Survival models handle ongoing projects naturally.
- **Validation.** Published accuracies of 90%+ come from survey-based or small datasets and are likely optimistic. The NCR cases suggest validating on whether a model would have flagged known failures (Supertech, Jaypee, Ansal, Raheja, Vatika, 3C, Earth) 1–3 years ahead.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed model was found that uses Indian RERA QPR or registry data to predict delay or stall, or that predicts Indian developer default from MCA or RERA data. This appears to be an open space.
- Full abstracts and feature lists for several ML papers (Sahu et al. 2026; Patel et al. 2024; XGBoost and SVM studies) could not be retrieved (403 errors), so their reported accuracies and features are partly unverified.
