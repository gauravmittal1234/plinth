## 1. Indian credit rating agency (CRA) methodologies for real estate developers and project debt (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings, Acuité, Infomerics, Brickwork), and whether NCR developer rationales are public

### Takeaway
All four large Indian CRAs (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings) publish qualitative frameworks. None of them publishes numeric factor weights: rating committees use judgment. They all converge on the same core: (a) execution track record, (b) sales velocity and booking ratio, (c) collection efficiency, (d) the ratio of committed receivables to pending construction cost plus debt, (e) leverage measured on cash flow (debt/CFO, debt/(WIP minus advances)), (f) approvals and land title, (g) funding closure and refinancing, and (h) RERA escrow ring-fencing. Rating rationales for NCR developers (Signature Global, Max Estates, Gaurs, DLF) are public through the CRAs and press. They are debt-default ratings, not delivery ratings for home buyers.

### Cited Findings

**CRISIL Ratings: "Rating criteria for real estate developers" (July 2024 version; previous version June 2023)**
- Four risk pillars: business, financial, project and management risk. These are "combined to arrive at the standalone rating", then notched up or down for parent/group support. — [CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)
- Residential project lifecycle risk sequence: "At the start of the project, funding risk is a key element. Once funding is secured, implementation risk and demand risk take frontstage. Once project construction is complete, demand risk comes into play." Typical end-to-end tenure is 3–5 years. — [CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)
- Business risk is assessed at the micro-market level, not industry level. Industry risk "may have limited relevance" because developers operate in only a few cities. Market position covers: development track record (area developed, "time or cost overruns", pipeline vs past execution, years in the micro market), brand equity, diversification by type and geography, and micro-market share. — [CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)
- Operating efficiency is judged on construction progress and sales-booking progress in ongoing projects, and on project mix by stage. Customer advances "may fund 50-70% of project cost". A portfolio skewed to early-stage projects carries implementation and funding risk. — [CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)
- Explicit formulas published by CRISIL:
  - CFO (adjusted) = Collections − construction expenses − other operating expenses − JV/JDA/DM outflows
  - Debt/CFO(adj)
  - CFO(adj)/interest
  - Cash buffer ratio = (CFO adj − tax + debt availed for construction + other cash items)/(principal + interest)
  - **Adequacy of committed receivables = Committed receivables from ongoing projects / (Balance construction cost + cumulative debt servicing of ongoing projects)**. "The corollary of this ratio is the velocity of new sales required to meet the ongoing construction expenses and debt obligations."
  - Leverage = Debt/Equity
  - Single-project SPV leverage = Total debt / (Inventory + Debtors + Cash)
  - — [CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)
- Financial flexibility covers cash and unutilised lines, ability to raise debt against land bank and leased assets, capital-market access, and "Ability to refinance". — [CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)
- Management risk covers integrity, risk appetite and competence. Risk appetite is assessed "through aggression in land bank acquisition". "Any history of litigation or regulatory action reflects negatively on management integrity." — [CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)
- No numeric factor weights or rating-category thresholds appear in the published criteria (verified from the full PDF text). — [CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)

**ICRA: "Rating Methodology for Real Estate Entities"**
- Older version: the copy retrieved is stamped "This methodology document stands superseded". It appears to be the c. June 2019 update of a March 2017 note. The current ICRA version was not retrieved.
- Factors fall under four heads: Industry Risk, Business Risk, Financial Position, Management Quality. — [ICRA methodology (superseded version)](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)
- IBC homebuyer risk: since July 2018 homebuyers are financial creditors who can trigger insolvency. ICRA "assesses the risk of such claims by homebuyers by looking at the project commencement dates and contracted handover dates to identify delayed projects". It views pre-RERA launches as higher risk. — [ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)
- Analysts "typically visit some of the completed and ongoing projects and obtain feedback from execution agencies and channel partners". — [ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)
- Funding-risk metrics:
  - Committed cash flows = (balance collections from sold area + undrawn credit lines) / (balance cost + debt)
  - **Collection efficiency** = amounts received ÷ demands raised
  - Advances received vs value of sale agreements should match the construction stage
  - Share of end-users with home loans, which lowers cancellation risk
  - Deferred-payment and subvention schemes
  - Financial-closure status
  - — [ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)
- Market risk: booked share of area, sales and pricing trend since launch, break-even sales volume, and **years of unsold inventory = area pending sale ÷ trailing-12-month average sales**. — [ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)
- Construction risk: "compares the physical and financial progress on the projects vis-à-vis the initial targets including the completion date as per RERA registration". It also covers contractor and consultant track record and approvals: building plan, commencement certificate (CC), occupancy certificate (OC), environmental clearance, airport and fire NOCs, RERA. RERA violations can bring penalties "up to 10% of the project cost". — [ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)
- RERA ring-fencing: 70% of collections go to the project escrow, and withdrawals are tied to cost incurred. ICRA estimates cash trapped per project when judging fungibility across projects. — [ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)
- Formulas:
  - Leverage = Debt / (WIP − Customer Advances). A ratio above 100% can signal diversion of funds to future growth.
  - Adequacy of Committed Cash Flows = Receivables from sold area / (Pending cost + Debt)
  - Cash Flow Security Cover = (Receivables + Value of stock − Pending cost) / Debt
  - Also sensitivity tests on price, bookings, construction schedule and sales velocity.
  - — [ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)
- Management quality credits "discipline … to maintain project level cash flows without diversion and/or commingling of funds". Debt-servicing track record and contingent liabilities (bank guarantees to authorities) are also assessed. — [ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)

**CARE Ratings (CareEdge): "Rating Methodology – Real Estate Sector" (issued March 2023; previous version March 2021)**
- Four heads: Industry risk (demand-supply, cyclicality, corporate governance), Management evaluation, Operational risk (execution and marketing), Financial risk. — [CARE methodology, Mar 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/pdf/get-rated/Rating%20Methodology%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Sector%20March%202023.pdf)
- Management signals include "auditor qualifications, pledge of promoter shareholding, RERA details to get an understanding if there are any litigations against the developer". Legal constitution also counts: partnerships and proprietorships carry capital-withdrawal risk. — [CARE methodology, Mar 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/pdf/get-rated/Rating%20Methodology%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Sector%20March%202023.pdf)
- Execution risk:
  - Scale of ongoing projects vs area delivered in the past.
  - Stage of construction = cost incurred ÷ total project cost, checked against physical progress. "CARE also monitors the update on construction progress as per the RERA website."
  - Regulatory and approval risk, and land-title risk.
  - — [CARE methodology, Mar 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/pdf/get-rated/Rating%20Methodology%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Sector%20March%202023.pdf)
- Marketing risk:
  - Booking ratio = units or area booked ÷ launched.
  - Registration of agreements as a share of bookings, which correlates with fewer cancellations.
  - Sales velocity as "months to sell the unsold inventory".
  - Adequacy of committed receivables vs balance cost plus debt, looked at as a trend over time.
  - — [CARE methodology, Mar 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/pdf/get-rated/Rating%20Methodology%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Sector%20March%202023.pdf)
- Financial risk:
  - Cash coverage ratio, a multi-quarter project cash-flow model including collection efficiency, stress-tested for completion delays, lower collections, price declines and cost rises.
  - Liquidity, escrow, DSRA, land bank, funding pattern (promoter funds : debt : customer advances), and gearing.
  - — [CARE methodology, Mar 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/pdf/get-rated/Rating%20Methodology%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Sector%20March%202023.pdf)
- "Rating determination is a matter of experienced and holistic judgment by the Rating Committee". No weights are published. — [CARE methodology, Mar 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/pdf/get-rated/Rating%20Methodology%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Sector%20March%202023.pdf)
- CARE disclaimer on business model: "Most entities whose bank facilities/instruments are rated by CARE Ratings Limited have paid a credit rating fee." — [CARE methodology, Mar 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/pdf/get-rated/Rating%20Methodology%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Sector%20March%202023.pdf)

**India Ratings & Research (Fitch group): "Rating Approach of Residential Real Estate Entities" (special report, Dec 2023 / Jan 2024; explicitly "not a criteria")**
- Ind-Ra applies its general Corporate Rating Methodology plus Parent-Subsidiary Linkage criteria. The real-estate-specific twist is that RERA restricts project cash-flow fungibility. "the consolidated liquidity analysis alone could be incomplete or misleading." — [India Ratings special report, Dec 2023](https://www.indiaratings.co.in/data//Uploads/CriteriaReport/2023/Rating%20Approach%20of%20Residential%20Real%20Estate%20Entities-Dec.pdf)
- Single-project SPVs "with moderate size and scale… can still be rated in investment grade categories". It uses a weak-link approach: the parent's rating may be constrained "closer to that of stressed SPVs" if there is no plan to fund SPV liquidity gaps. — [India Ratings special report](https://www.indiaratings.co.in/data//Uploads/CriteriaReport/2023/Rating%20Approach%20of%20Residential%20Real%20Estate%20Entities-Dec.pdf)
- Related-party land deals, development contracts and loans are "frequent in non-investment grade entities". Where group data is unavailable, ratings are constrained "to account for information risk". — [India Ratings special report](https://www.indiaratings.co.in/data//Uploads/CriteriaReport/2023/Rating%20Approach%20of%20Residential%20Real%20Estate%20Entities-Dec.pdf)

**Infomerics, Brickwork, Acuité**
- Infomerics settled with SEBI (Nov 2025, Rs 65.25 lakh) over alleged lapses during a 2021 inspection. Allegations included "failure to use correct Rating Models", "manipulation and tampering with weights in the Rating Models thus inflating the final model ratings", and "arbitrary deactivation of certain parameters". This implies Infomerics uses internal weighted scoring models that are not published. — [Moneycontrol, 13 Nov 2025](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/infomerics-valuation-and-rating-pays-rs-65-25-lakh-to-settle-case-with-sebi-13674757.html)
- SEBI cancelled Brickwork Ratings' licence on 6 Oct 2022 (wind-down within 6 months). — [Times of India, 6 Oct 2022](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/sebi-cancels-registration-of-brickwork-ratings-directs-it-to-wind-down-operations-in-6-months/amp_articleshow/94687030.cms)
  - The SEBI/RBI inspection found failure to follow the rating process, missing records supporting rating decisions, delayed dissemination, and conflict-of-interest issues.
  - The Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) partly set aside the cancellation in June 2023, calling the violations "routine operational errors".
  - — [BusinessLine, Jun 2023](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/sat-quashes-sebi-order-cancelling-brickwork-ratings-licence/article66938006.ece)
- Acuité's real estate methodology was not retrieved (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The most transferable pieces for a home-buyer score are the CRA cash-cover ratios:
  - committed receivables ÷ (balance cost + debt), from CRISIL, ICRA and CARE
  - collection efficiency
  - months or years of unsold inventory
  - debt ÷ (WIP − advances)
  - physical vs financial progress against the RERA completion date

  Several of these can be approximated per project from RERA quarterly progress reports (QPRs), Form-3/annual audit reports and CA certificates. None of the CRAs publish weights, so the founder must set and calibrate weights empirically.
- Indian CRA ratings answer "will lenders and bondholders be paid on time?", not "will my flat be delivered on time?". Signature Global's A+ rests heavily on homebuyer receivables, the same cash that funds construction. Debt safety and delivery safety are correlated but not identical. A developer can service debt from new launches while an old project stalls.
- Only larger, debt-raising NCR developers carry public ratings. Most small builders, where delivery risk is concentrated, have none or only bank-loan ratings. A buyer-facing score needs a fallback for unrated developers.

### Gaps
- ICRA's current (post-2019) real estate methodology, Acuité's real estate criteria, Infomerics' published criteria, and Brickwork's criteria were not retrieved. Whether any of them publish numeric weights is unconfirmed.
- Current ratings for M3M, ATS, Mahagun, Ace, and DLF's 2026 rating were not found. CRA rationale pages could not be searched directly because the search budget ran out and CRA sites blocked automated queries.
- No CRA sector-specific default/transition study for real estate was retrieved, so the accuracy track record of Indian real estate ratings is unquantified here.

## 2. Project and home-buyer gradings in India: CRISIL CREST and developer grading, RERA gradings (MahaRERA, UP-RERA, HARERA), CREDAI, consultant rankings, portals and startups

### Takeaway
India has had buyer-facing project and developer gradings since about 2008–2010 via CRISIL CREST (0–7 stars, city-relative). These are developer-paid, opt-in and surveillance-fee based, and still marketed by Crisil Intelligence. Regulators have tried and stalled. CRISIL designed a detailed 5-star project and 5-grade promoter framework for UP-RERA (proposed, c. 2020), but no evidence of launch was found. MahaRERA built a disclosure-style "grading matrix" (2023) and then halted it in Nov 2024 because the RERA Act gives no power to grade. Homebuyer groups (FPCE, Oct 2025) are still asking for developer track-record checks. This leaves a gap that an independent, data-driven score could fill, but it carries legal and reputational risk.

### Cited Findings

**CRISIL Real Estate Star Grading (CREST): project-level**
- CREST "indicates the quality of a graded real estate project in terms of pre-agreed specifications and timeliness of delivery". It is an eight-point scale from 7 star ("Highest likelihood… delivered with the least delay") to 0 star ("lowest likelihood… maximum delay"). — [Crisil CREST FAQs](https://www.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/sme_ratings/star-rating-brochure/crest-faqs.pdf)
- The scale is "specific to a city": it "benchmarks the graded project against other real estate projects in the same city". It is called a grading, not a rating, because "CREST does not assess the creditworthiness of projects/developers". — [Crisil CREST FAQs](https://www.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/sme_ratings/star-rating-brochure/crest-faqs.pdf)
- Inputs and process:
  - Project-specific parameters: "sponsor, construction quality, legal documentation, financial planning, and innovation".
  - Amenity level is excluded, so "an affordable housing project [can] obtain a higher grading than a luxury apartment project".
  - Information required: developer track record, three years of audited accounts, project approvals, timelines and quality, plus "specific undertakings".
  - Process takes 4–5 weeks with a site visit.
  - — [Crisil CREST FAQs](https://www.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/sme_ratings/star-rating-brochure/crest-faqs.pdf)
- Surveillance and business model:
  - Crisil monitors approvals, construction stages, financial closure, disbursement and sales bookings, with annual review and site visit.
  - "A surveillance fee is payable each year until Crisil receives the occupancy/completion certificate".
  - The grading can be withdrawn only after completion and OC. Developers may display it in advertisements.
  - Disclaimer: "Crisil or its associates may have other commercial transactions with the company/entity."
  - — [Crisil CREST FAQs](https://www.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/sme_ratings/star-rating-brochure/crest-faqs.pdf)
- Launch date: one secondary source says CRISIL introduced real estate star ratings in August 2010. The prompt's "about 2008" date was not verified, and no evidence was found that CREST was discontinued. The FAQ is current Crisil Intelligence branding. — [TheWealthWisher (secondary, via search snippet)](https://www.thewealthwisher.com/real-estate-star-ratings-from-crisil/); [Crisil Real Estate Project Grading page](https://www.crisil.com/en/home/our-businesses/sme-solutions/sector-gradings/real-estate/crisil-real-estate-project-grading.html)

**CRISIL Developer Grading: developer-level (criteria dated July 2018; older)**
- Assesses "a real estate developer's capability to execute a project as per specified quality standards and its ability to transfer a clear title within the stipulated schedule". — [Crisil developer grading criteria, Jul 2018](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/crest/national-developer-rating/crest-criteria-developer-grading.pdf)
- Scale: eight points from DA1+ (highest) to DA5 (lowest), with "+" for DA1–DA3. The grading is valid 12 months and under surveillance "until such time we receive communication from the developer requesting withdrawal". — [Crisil developer grading criteria](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/crest/national-developer-rating/crest-criteria-developer-grading.pdf)
- Framework has three blocks:
  1. Track record: legal, construction and market track record, plus "Likelihood of time and cost overruns".
  2. Organisational strength: systems, structure, strategy, management.
  3. Financial strength: financial profile and flexibility, including "ability to tie up a project's end-to-end funding".
  - Track-record inputs include statutory compliance, quality, scale, infrastructure, reputation and "Customer feedback".
  - — [Crisil developer grading criteria](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/crest/national-developer-rating/crest-criteria-developer-grading.pdf)
- A Crisil press release title shows the product is still used: "Crisil reaffirms Real Estate Developer Grading of 'DA1+' to …" (Prestige Estates). The date was not verified. — [Crisil press release PDF (title via search result)](https://www.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/crest/national-developer-rating/press-release/prestige-estates-projects-limited.pdf)

**UP-RERA × CRISIL grading framework (most NCR-relevant precedent; PDF dated April 2020; labelled "Proposed")**
- **Promoter grading** (Grade I lowest to V highest, reviewed annually) assesses the "ability to execute real estate projects as per the specified quality and within the stipulated time schedule". Parameter groups:
  - UP-RERA compliance: QPR uploads, escrow norm adherence, timely CC/OC upload, end-date commitment, annual report upload, end-date extensions, handover to association.
  - Legal track record: OC/CC received as % of completed projects; "Willful default, history of litigations against the company/promoters/land, NCLT, land issues"; months to society formation.
  - Track record: geographic and segment diversity, area delivered in last 5 years, ratio of ongoing to completed area, average area of ongoing projects, "Average delay in months in physical possession w.r.t. … RERA end date", average delay in obtaining OC, overall delay in ongoing projects.
  - Organisational strength: ISO, LEED, geotagged on-site lab photos, promoter experience.
  - Financial quality: turnover, PAT margin, gearing, net worth, achievement of financial targets.
  - Customer feedback: "Scoring based on number of complaints".
  - — [UP-RERA CRISIL Grading Framework PDF](https://up-rera.in/frm_Ifram_for_PDf.aspx?Param=111UP_RERA_CRISIL_Grading_Framework.pdf)
- **Project grading** (5 star to 1 star; "likelihood of a project being delivered as per the agreed quality and within the stipulated time schedule"). Blocks:
  - Construction quality: on-site testing, material testing, soil investigation, seismic design, ISO/LEED.
  - Likelihood of time and cost overruns: expected delay in physical completion (months), expected delay in OC, expected cost overrun.
  - UP-RERA compliance.
  - Legal quality: clear and marketable title, unencumbered land, sale-agreement clauses.
  - Financial quality: % units sold, project debt-to-equity, financial targets, financial closure, "Advance received divided by the total cost".
  - Customer complaints.
  - Sponsor quality = the developer grade.
  - — [UP-RERA CRISIL Grading Framework PDF](https://up-rera.in/frm_Ifram_for_PDf.aspx?Param=111UP_RERA_CRISIL_Grading_Framework.pdf)
- Hygiene overrides:
  - **Deflators** pull the score down: pending dues to development authorities; appearing in UP-RERA defaulter or de-registered lists; many court cases or recovery certificates (RCs).
  - **Weak-link** caps for projects: defaulter project status, unclear land title, land encumbrances.
  - — [UP-RERA CRISIL Grading Framework PDF](https://up-rera.in/frm_Ifram_for_PDf.aspx?Param=111UP_RERA_CRISIL_Grading_Framework.pdf)
- Data availability was a problem. The framework's "Data source" column marks many key parameters "Not available": construction quality tests, expected time overrun, OC delay, land-title clarity, area delivered, past delays, ISO/LEED. RERA-portal compliance data, % sold, gearing, advances/cost and complaints are marked "Yes". — [UP-RERA CRISIL Grading Framework PDF](https://up-rera.in/frm_Ifram_for_PDf.aspx?Param=111UP_RERA_CRISIL_Grading_Framework.pdf)
- Governance recommendations:
  - Promoters upload data through the portal; data is validated; site visits are recommended and mandatory on triggers (timeline extended, defaulter project, high complaints/court cases/RCs, delayed projects).
  - 12-month renewal; "non-cooperating" status leads to suspension or withdrawal; appeal within 7 working days.
  - "There is significant reputational and legal risk involved in the real estate grading process. To reduce this risk, recommends the final grading could be given by an independent agency rather than by UP-RERA under its own brand".
  - "the digitised approach to grading should be supplemented with manual validation".
  - — [UP-RERA CRISIL Grading Framework PDF](https://up-rera.in/frm_Ifram_for_PDf.aspx?Param=111UP_RERA_CRISIL_Grading_Framework.pdf)
- UP-RERA runs an "abeyance list" of stalled projects. ### Inferences
- The UP-RERA/CRISIL framework is effectively a ready-made factor list for an NCR delivery-risk score. Its "deflator" and "weak-link" design is a sound way to handle disqualifying red flags inside an otherwise additive score: defaulter list, RCs, title problems, authority dues. It also documents which inputs are not observable from public data. Those are the factors the founder must proxy or collect.
- Regulator-run grading has twice stalled (UP-RERA proposal not launched as far as found; MahaRERA halted on legal grounds, with CREDAI opposition). CRISIL itself advised that grades be issued by an independent agency, not under the regulator's brand. That supports a private, independent score, but implies real legal risk from rated developers.
- CREST's city-relative scale and its "no withdrawal until OC" rule are useful design patterns. The first avoids penalising markets with structurally slower approvals. The second prevents a developer from hiding a downgrade.
- Blanket force-majeure extensions (2020 COVID, 2026 West Asia) mean "delay vs RERA end date" needs a regulator-extension adjustment. Otherwise both the score and its outcome labels get distorted.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found that UP-RERA actually published CRISIL-framework grades, and the framework's numeric weights (likely in images in the PDF) were not extractable.
- HARERA (Haryana, Gurugram) grading: none found. Search results showed only enforcement actions.

The general search budget ran out and fallback engines returned irrelevant results. Their methodology and existence remain unconfirmed.
- The number of CREST and developer gradings outstanding, and any record of CREST delivery outcomes, were not found.

## 3. Global analogues: NSW iCIRT, UK HBF/NHBC stars, Singapore CONQUAS, Dubai DLD/RERA escrow, China "three red lines" and whitelist, Moody's/S&P homebuilder methods, US

### Takeaway
The closest analogue is NSW's iCIRT: an Equifax-run, developer-paid, 0–5 star rating of a business's "6 Cs", with public listing only at 3 stars or above. The UK HBF stars show how customer-survey ratings inflate: most big builders get 5 stars despite high defect counts, and the method was reworked in 2026. CONQUAS shows objective, weighted quality scoring by an independent assessor. Dubai and China show that structural protections (100% escrow, presale controls, financing whitelists) and bright-line balance-sheet tests often matter more than grades. S&P publishes explicit sub-factor weights and leverage bands for homebuilders.

### Cited Findings

**NSW iCIRT (Australia)**
- iCIRT is "an industry-led market surveillance tool using a star-rating methodology" provided by Equifax.
  - Scale is 0–5 stars; only businesses with 3+ gold stars appear on the register.
  - Criteria: Capability, Conduct, Character, Capacity, Capital, Counterparties, covering transparency, trustworthiness, experience, track record, financial stability and regulatory compliance.
  - Stages: Application, Assessment, Validation, Rating. Ratings are valid 12 months.
  - — [NSW Government, Building Commission iCIRT page (updated 1 Dec 2023)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/building-commission/icirt)
- Paid by the rated business:
  - Standard Report A$3,050 ex-GST (public plus self-provided data, max 4 stars).
  - Detailed Report A$8,250 (public and private data, max 5 stars, register-eligible at 3+ stars, includes a marketing badge).
  - — [iCIRT order page](https://www.icirt.com/order.html)
  - Conflict: a third-party site quotes A$680–5,950. — [Chipkin](https://store.chipkin.com/articles/icirt-by-equifax-construction-company-star-ratings)
- Status and rollout:
  - Voluntary as of June 2022, covering developers, builders and certifiers of Class 2 (apartment) buildings.
  - The first 19 developers and builders were rated in June 2022.
  - Non-listed firms "may face questions from purchasers".
  - — [Allens, Jun 2022](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2022/06/Rate-my-builder-next-step-in-NSW-Government-rating-regime/)
  - Launched December 2021. — [Canberra Times, updated 2 Oct 2026](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9361219/what-is-an-icirt-rating-and-which-sydney-developers-have-it/)
- Limitation as stated in the press: iCIRT measures "the trustworthiness, track record and financial capacity of the business, not the individual building… It does not inspect the apartment." — [Canberra Times, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9356255/deicorp-icirt-rating-how-nsw-checks-reveal-developer-record/)

**UK HBF/NHBC new-homes customer satisfaction stars**
- Voluntary for HBF and Homes for Scotland members since 2005. The NHBC survey goes out 8 weeks and 9 months after move-in. — [HBF CSS page (search snippet)](https://www.hbf.co.uk/policy/policy-and-wider-work-program/customer-satisfaction-survey/latest-results/)
- New method from the 2026 award:
  - Composite of four questions: build quality and service-after-sale, each asked at 8 weeks and 9 months, scored 1–5 and averaged.
  - 5 stars needs ≥4.15 in year one, with the threshold set to rise.
  - The change followed an Ipsos MORI 2021 review.
  - — [HBF Star Rating rules 2025 (search snippet; HBF site blocked fetch)](https://www.hbf.co.uk/documents/14404/HBF_-_CSS_and_HBF_Star_Rating_rules_2025.pdf)
  - Previously the star rating was based on the 8-week "recommend to a friend" question. — [new-home-blog (advocacy, older)](http://www.new-home-blog.co.uk/new-home-buyers-fooled-bogus-builder-satisfaction-star-rating/)
  - Conflict: a Sept 2026 consumer blog still describes 90%/80%/70% recommend thresholds, which looks outdated. — [new-builds.co.uk, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.new-builds.co.uk/blog/hbf-star-rating-compare-new-build-developers)
- 2026 results: 93% of buyers "would recommend their builder". — [HBF news 2026 (search snippet)](https://www.hbf.co.uk/news/new-build-homeowners-report-high-levels-of-satisfaction-2026/)
- Criticism (older, advocacy sources):
  - The HBF 2019 survey showed 99% of buyers reporting defects, and 42% reporting more than 10.
  - Ratings were described as so skewed that 42 of 48 rated builders got 5 stars.
  - Allegations that builders nudge buyers to rate positively.
  - — [new-home-blog, 2019](http://www.new-home-blog.co.uk/hbf-satisfaction-survey-2019-shows-number-of-defects-still-high/); [Housing Today: "HBF defends rating system"](https://www.housingtoday.co.uk/news/home-builders-federation-defends-rating-system/5103406.article)

**Singapore BCA CONQUAS (archived factsheet, c. 2013; older)**
- Introduced 1989 as the national standard. It scores Structural, Architectural and M&E works, adding up to 100.
  - Structural and M&E are assessed during construction; Architectural on the completed building.
  - Sampling is sized to the building.
  - Rectified work "will not be re-scored", to encourage "doing things right the first time".
  - — [CONQUAS factsheet (NAS archive)](https://www.nas.gov.sg/archivesonline/data/pdfdoc/20130523003/factsheet_-_conquas.pdf)

**Dubai (DLD / RERA)**

**China: "three red lines" (Aug 2020) and project "whitelist" (2024–)**
- Thresholds:
  - Liability-to-asset ratio excluding advance receipts < 70%.
  - Net gearing < 100%.
  - Cash / short-term debt ≥ 1.0.
  - In 2020 the average developer was at about 75% and 1.4 net gearing, breaching thresholds. Financing froze afterwards.
  - — [CKGSB Knowledge](https://english.ckgsb.edu.cn/knowledge/professor_analysis/series-chinas-real-estate-problem-1-the-three-red-lines/)
- Traffic-light tiers on interest-bearing debt growth: red (breaches all three) 0% growth; orange 5%; yellow 10%; green 15%. — [SMM news](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/101253537-three-red-lines-and-the-red-orange-yellow-and-green-in-the-performance-report)
- The "whitelist" is a project-level, city-nominated list of projects eligible for completion financing. Approved loans exceed 7 trillion yuan (as of Oct 2025).
  - An expert describes eligible projects as compliant, fully permitted, "free of legal or debt disputes", with "proper capital supervision".
  - Survey frictions: collateral (about 60%) and loan size tied to sales and cash flow (about 60%).
  - — [China Daily, 21 Jan 2026](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202601/21/WS697028e1a310d6866eb34dc9.html)
  - "Over 7.5 million presold homes" were delivered. — [USCBC (search snippet; site blocked fetch)](https://www.uschina.org/articles/understanding-chinas-housing-downturn-and-the-path-ahead/)
- Academic evidence (192 listed Chinese developers, 2013–2022):
  - Entering the CRIC top-100/200 sales ranking was followed by debt-to-capital ratios 1.88–3.66 points higher.
  - Sales rankings worked as a credit signal that fuelled over-leverage. Example: Country Garden, a perennial top-ranked developer that later defaulted.
  - — [Humanities & Social Sciences Communications (Nature), 20 Jun 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05154-7)

**S&P and Moody's homebuilder/developer methodologies**
- S&P "Key Credit Factors For The Homebuilder And Real Estate Developer Industry". Older: Feb 2014, republished Apr 2019, partly superseded by the 2019 Ratios & Adjustments criteria.
  - Industry cyclicality is "high" (5), calibrated from an average revenue drop of 20% and profitability decline of 26% in downturns.
  - Competitive position weights: competitive advantage 30%, scale/scope/diversity 30%, operating efficiency 40%.
  - — [S&P KCF via Maalot](https://www.maalot.co.il/Publications/MT20190514121552.PDF)
- S&P specifics:
  - Speculative (unsold built) inventory as % of total is a negative factor.
  - Pre-sale regimes matter: Shanghai requires two-thirds of building height before pre-sale.
  - Third-party contractor terms on overruns and delay penalties are reviewed.
  - Core ratios are FFO/debt and debt/EBITDA, with a supplemental **debt/(debt+equity)** band (historical cost): <25% minimal; 25–35% modest; 35–45% intermediate; 45–50% significant; 50–60% aggressive; >60% highly leveraged.
  - Cash + inventory / unadjusted debt is a liquidity metric for homebuilders, but not for developers given illiquid inventory.
  - — [S&P KCF via Maalot](https://www.maalot.co.il/Publications/MT20190514121552.PDF)
- Moody's "Homebuilding and Property Development" methodology (Jan 2018 per search results) uses a weighted scorecard, but the document was not retrievable (Moody's site returned 403). See Gaps.

### Inferences
- iCIRT's design maps directly onto an India developer score:
  - The "6 Cs" cover integrity of directors, track record, dispute and defect conduct, capacity vs pipeline, capital, and supply-chain counterparties.
  - The "3-star pass mark, publish only passes" rule is a way to limit defamation exposure.
  - Its developer-pays model and the "business, not the building" limitation are the trade-offs to avoid or disclose.
- HBF shows that survey-based "satisfaction" ratings drift toward everyone getting 5 stars and lag latent defects. An India score should weight verifiable delivery outcomes (OC dates vs promised, RERA complaints and orders) over sentiment, and should take later-dated signals (9-month or 1-year post-possession) into account.
- China's experience shows two things. Bright-line balance-sheet tests are blunt but legible to buyers. And scale or sales rank is not safety. Brand/scale weights should be capped, and leverage and liquidity should be able to override them.
- Dubai's 100% escrow vs India's 70% RERA escrow: escrow compliance and withdrawal patterns are a high-value, structural input.

## 4. Consumer credit-score analogues: CIBIL/FICO structure, reason codes and adverse-action explanations, explainable scorecards, PD calibration, validation (Gini/AUC, calibration)

### Takeaway
Consumer scores pair a simple public explanation with a statistically fitted model:
- **Published factor categories with approximate population weights.** FICO: 35/30/15/10/10, with the caveat that importance "may vary from one person to another".
- **A bounded numeric scale.** FICO 300–850; CIBIL 300–900.
- **A small number of specific reason codes per decision.** US Reg B says more than four is "not likely to be helpful".

Validation norms come from Basel: discrimination (AUC, Accuracy Ratio/Gini) and calibration (binomial, Hosmer-Lemeshow, traffic lights). Low-default portfolios, like rare project failures, are hard to validate.

### Cited Findings
- FICO factor categories: payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of credit history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%. "The importance of these categories may vary from one person to another." — [myFICO: What's in my credit score](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score)
- "A FICO® Score is a three-digit number, ranging from 300 to 850". — [myFICO: What is a FICO Score](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/what-is-a-fico-score)
- CIBIL scores run 300–900. Banks describe bands such as 750–900 as excellent. — [Axis Bank blog](https://www.axis.bank.in/blogs/credit-score/cibil-score-ranges)
  - Factor weights for CIBIL (for example 35/30/10/10/10) appear only on third-party blogs and mirror FICO. TransUnion CIBIL's own page blocked fetching, and no official CIBIL weights were verified. — [Paytm blog (unofficial)](https://paytm.com/blog/credit-score/how-cibil-score-is-calculated-key-factors-determining-your-creditworthiness/)
- US Regulation B (ECOA), official interpretation of §1002.9(b)(2):
  - "disclosure of more than four reasons is not likely to be helpful to the applicant".
  - Reasons "must relate to and accurately describe the factors actually considered or scored".
  - Acceptable reason-selection methods: identify factors where the applicant's score "fell furthest below the average score for each of those factors achieved by applicants whose total score was at or slightly above the minimum passing score", or below the average of all applicants.
  - — [CFPB Reg B Official Interpretation, §1002.9](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/interp-9/)
- CFPB Circular 2022-03:
  - Creditors using "complex algorithms" must still give specific, accurate principal reasons. ECOA does not permit models whose reasons cannot be identified.
  - The circular was withdrawn on 12 May 2025, but the underlying statutory duty is unchanged.
  - — [Federal Register, 14 Jun 2022](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/06/14/2022-12729/consumer-financial-protection-circular-2022-03-adverse-action-notification-requirements-in); [CFPB withdrawn guidance list](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/guidance/withdrawn-guidance/)
- Scorecard construction (industry practice, secondary sources):
  - Weight-of-Evidence binning, then logistic regression, then points scaling: Score = Offset + Factor × ln(odds), Factor = PDO/ln(2).
  - Example: 600 points at 50:1 odds, with 20 points to double the odds.
  - Each prediction comes with reason codes.
  - — [MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/blogs/credit-scoring-logistic-regression/); [GitHub example scorecard (WoE/IV, PDO, AUC/KS/Gini, calibration)](https://github.com/Shashwat-Kumar/credit-risk-scorecard)
- Basel Committee Working Paper 14, "Studies on the Validation of Internal Rating Systems" (19 May 2005; older but foundational):
  - Discriminatory power uses CAP/Accuracy Ratio and ROC/AUC, with AR = 2·AUC − 1.
  - Calibration "means that the PD estimates are accurate", tested via binomial, Hosmer-Lemeshow and traffic-light tests.
  - It flags low-default portfolios and small samples as limiting validation power, and suggests external benchmarking.
  - — [BIS BCBS WP14](https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs_wp14.pdf)

### Inferences
- A "credit score for projects" can use the same three-layer communication:
  - (i) a bounded score (for example 300–900, to mirror CIBIL familiarity) mapped to an explicit probability, such as "chance of OC more than 12 months after the RERA date";
  - (ii) published factor groups with approximate weights;
  - (iii) up to four project-specific reason codes, ranked by contribution relative to well-scored peers (the Reg B method).

  This gives explainability without exposing the full model.
- Outcome labels exist in NCR history: stalled and delayed projects (ANAROCK 2021), SWAMIH revivals, IBC cases, RERA delay orders. But true failures are a low-default problem. Expect a wide AUC confidence interval, and plan for calibration by band (deciles of predicted delay probability vs realised outcome) plus benchmarking against CRA ratings and CREST where available.
- Weights should be fitted, then disclosed at category level. The Infomerics SEBI case shows that manually "tampering with weights" is a recognised failure mode. Version-controlled weights with an audit trail are a credibility feature.

### Gaps
- No official TransUnion CIBIL factor weights or reason-code catalogue was retrieved, and Indian rules on adverse-action explanations (RBI) were not researched.
- No academic paper specifically modelling Indian residential project delay or developer default with ML was found within the budget. The only empirical developer-distress paper retrieved is the 2025 China study above.

## 5. Lessons and pitfalls: issuer-pays conflicts, rating inflation, lagging ratings (IL&FS, Jaypee, Amrapali, Unitech), regulatory and legal pushback

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are: issuer-pays and rating shopping (2008), over-reliance on management information and slow downgrades (IL&FS 2018, which led SEBI to fine ICRA, CARE and India Ratings), and debt ratings that turn to "default" only after home buyers are already stuck. Jaypee is the NCR example: construction stalled 2013–14, CARE cut Jaiprakash Associates to D in July 2015, and insolvency followed in 2017. Rating systems also face pushback: developers and CREDAI opposed MahaRERA grading, and SAT overturned a CRA licence cancellation. Survey-based star schemes inflate (HBF).

### Cited Findings

**Issuer-pays and rating inflation (global)**
- In the 2008 crisis, "investment banks therefore paid handsome fees to the rating agencies to obtain the desired ratings".
  - The FCIC found 73% of Moody's 2006 AAA mortgage-backed securities were downgraded to junk by April 2010, and called the agencies "essential cogs in the wheel of financial destruction".
  - A former Moody's MD described clients threatening to move to Fitch or S&P (rating shopping).
  - — [Wikipedia: Credit rating agencies and the subprime crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_rating_agencies_and_the_subprime_crisis)

**IL&FS (India, 2018)**
- Rating agencies kept AAA or similar ratings on IL&FS entities "for a long time". ICRA and CARE then downgraded several instruments from investment grade to default or junk in Aug–Sep 2018. — [Wikipedia: IL&FS scam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IL%26FS_scam)
- SEBI fined India Ratings, CARE and ICRA Rs 25 lakh each (Dec 2019).
  - Allegations: "excessive reliance on the submissions made by the IL&FS management", failure to downgrade or place on "watch" despite slow asset monetisation, and failure to consider the latest financials.
  - Quote: "lethargic indifference and needless procrastination and laxity".
  - — [The Hindu, 26 Dec 2019](https://www.thehindu.com/business/ilfs-sebi-fines-3-rating-agencies-25-lakh-each/article30405219.ece)
- SEBI raised penalties to Rs 1 crore each (Sep 2020). Quotes:
  - "Had the noticee downgraded the ratings at the appropriate time and thereby forewarned the investors, the impact of the default… could not have been this severe".
  - CRAs are "financial 'gatekeeper[s]'".
  - — [Free Press Journal, 22 Sep 2020](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/ilfs-case-sebi-raises-penalty-to-rs-1-crore-each-on-icra-care)
- CareEdge account: a Grant Thornton audit found IL&FS management gave incomplete or inaccurate information to CRAs. "The issuer-pays model… has been noted by analysts as a potential source of conflicts of interest". — [Wikipedia: CareEdge Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CareEdge_Group)

**Lagging ratings vs homebuyer harm (NCR)**
- Jaypee Infratech (Wish Town, Noida/YEIDA): "Construction stalled around 2013–2014, leaving a large number of homebuyers stranded". Suraksha Group's NCLT-approved takeover (March 2023) affected "more than 20,000 homebuyers" after "a decade-long legal battle". — [Wikipedia: Jaypee Infratech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaypee_Infratech)

### Inferences
- Revenue model: a buyer-pays or platform-funded model (lead-gen, subscription, lender or insurer data licensing) avoids issuer-pays bias, but cuts off private developer data. CRISIL's UP-RERA notes show many key inputs are "Not available" publicly, so the score must lean on public RERA, court and NCLT, authority-dues and complaint data, plus on-ground verification (geotagged progress photos).
- Timeliness: the IL&FS and Jaypee lessons argue for leading indicators that move before debt default:
  - QPR progress slippage vs the RERA timeline
  - collection efficiency and escrow withdrawals
  - stalled booking velocity
  - new RERA complaints and RCs
  - authority dues
  - promoter-group defaults elsewhere (UP-RERA "deflators")
  - Also: a "watch" flag, and refresh at least quarterly to match the QPR cadence.
- Legal defensibility, from iCIRT, CREST and SEBI practice:
  - Use probabilistic, "opinion" framing ("likelihood of delivery as per agreed timeline").
  - Publish the methodology and factor categories; cite source documents for each reason code.
  - Offer a developer response or appeal window (UP-RERA's 7 working days); keep version-controlled weights.
  - Consider "publish positives, gate negatives" (iCIRT 3+ star register) or show raw facts for low scores.
- Avoid scale/brand dominance: cap brand and size weights, and let hard red flags (defaulter list, NCLT admission, title encumbrance) override via weak-link caps.
