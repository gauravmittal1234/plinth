## Key Question 1: Which 3–5 additional scores would add the most buyer value relative to data feasibility, for a v1 product in Gurugram and Noida?

### Takeaway
Four additional scores give the best value-to-feasibility ratio for a v1, plus one buyer-side calculator:
1. **Transferability / clean-ownership** (registry status, OC integrity, title, transfer blockers). This is the highest-severity risk: an unregistered Noida flat "cannot be sold or mortgaged".
2. **Waterlogging and flood exposure at society level**, built on observed evidence rather than a modelled number.
3. **Society health and running-cost** (maintenance trajectory, RWA/AOA handover, water, power, lift and fire compliance, structural-audit flags).
4. **Fair-price range** against registered comparables.
5. **Affordability and total-cost stress test** (a calculator, not a property score).

Other hazards should appear as factual proximity flags, not composite scores, because they barely differ within NCR (seismic, regional AQI) or lack data at flat level (noise, high-tension lines, heat). These are AQI, noise, landfill, high-tension lines, heat and seismic zone. Liquidity, rental yield and a predictive construction-quality score need transaction and listing history the platform will only build later (v2).

### Inferences
**Evaluation matrix.** Ratings are judgement calls on a 1–5 scale (5 = best for the product), based on the evidence in Key Question 2.

| Candidate score | Buyer pain severity | Data feasibility for v1 (Gurugram + Noida) | Differentiation vs portals | Harm if wrong | v1 verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transferability (registry, OC integrity, title, transfer charges) | 5 (deal-breaker; blocks loans and resale) | 4 at project level (authority/RERA/DTCP lists); 2–3 at unit level (needs documents) | 5 | High (legal reliance) | **v1: project-level flags plus document checklist** |
| Waterlogging/flood exposure | 4 (recurring; power, vehicles, access) | 3 (terrain + drainage + geocoded incidents + resident reports) | 5 | High (price effect; seller and developer pushback) | **v1: evidence-led bands** |
| Society health and running cost | 4 (recurring cost; quality of life) | 3 (crowd-sourced plus public lists; improves with users) | 5 (moat) | Medium | **v1: for ready and resale societies** |
| Fair-price range | 4 (largest single ₹ decision) | 3–4 (licensed deeds; Phase-1 AVM design) | 3 | Medium | **v1 (already planned in Phase-1)** |
| Affordability/total-cost stress test | 3–4 | 5 (user inputs plus public rates) | 2 | Low | **v1 as calculator** |
| Resale liquidity | 3–4 | 2 (needs listing-duration history) | 4 | Medium | v2 (proxy flags in v1) |
| Rental yield / tenant demand | 2–3 (investors) | 2–3 (listing rents only) | 3 | Low–Medium | v2 |
| Construction quality (predictive) | 4 | 2 (audit lists are sporadic) | 4 | High (defamation) | Fold known-issue flags into society score; predictive belongs in developer score |
| Livability / commute | 3 | 4 (OSM, routing APIs) | 2 (commodity) | Low | Context layer, not a "risk" score |
| Environmental nuisances (AQI, noise, landfill, HT lines, heat, groundwater, seismic) | 2–3 each | 2–4, varies | 3 | Medium | Proximity facts and flags, not a composite |
| Master-plan conformity | 3 for builder floors/plots; 2 for licensed group housing | 2 (map digitisation) | 4 | High | v2 (with builder floors/Delhi) |

- **Why transferability comes first.** It is binary, high-stakes and largely knowable at project level from authority and RERA records. The Phase-1 report already calls registry status the single biggest liquidity driver in Noida. The Gurugram OC audit shows that "has OC" is no longer a sufficient green light. The score should cross-check OC date against completion certificate (CC), the audit list, and ground evidence such as photos and resident confirmation.
- **Why waterlogging is the strongest location score.** The pain recurs every monsoon, varies from society to society (basement flooding, switchgear damage) and is not captured by official road "hotspot" lists (see the conflict in Key Question 2). The US experience in Key Question 3 shows that modelled flood scores drive buyer behaviour but also provoke backlash when they conflict with observed history. An NCR score should be built evidence-first (observed incidents) and model-second (terrain and drainage).
- **Why society health builds a moat.** Its inputs (maintenance bills, outage logs, RWA status) come largely from residents, so coverage compounds with users and portals cannot easily copy it.
- **Product framing.** Present these as separate, named sub-scores (for example "Ownership & paperwork", "Flood & waterlogging", "Society & running costs", "Price fairness"). Do not fold them into one "credit score". Each sub-score has a different evidence base and a different failure mode. A single composite would hide which risk is driving the number and would be harder to defend.
- **Conflict-of-interest risk.** If the platform earns channel-partner commissions from developers (Phase-1's revenue model), its location and society scores will face the same pressure that CRMLS put on Zillow (Key Question 3). A published methodology and a firewall between ratings and revenue are prerequisites.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifies how NCR buyers rank these risks against one another (for example flooding vs maintenance vs registry). LocalCircles survey pages could not be retrieved, and the web-search budget ran out before a targeted LocalCircles pollution or maintenance survey could be found.
- No Indian study prices waterlogging, maintenance quality or registry status into resale values (Phase-1 found none either). Weightings would have to be learned from the platform's own data.
- No authoritative 2026 count of unregistered Noida/Greater Noida flats was found beyond the recurring "75,000" estimate.

## Key Question 2: What evidence exists of buyer pain for each candidate score, and what would each score predict, use and risk?

### Takeaway
Every candidate has documented NCR pain, but the evidence differs in kind.
- **Registry/OC/title and society governance:** hard, current evidence (authority actions, the July 2026 OC audit, August 2026 RWA membership fights, a 90% maintenance hike in October 2026).
- **Waterlogging and water supply:** vivid, recurring event evidence, but contested official metrics (GMDA claims hotspots fell from ~90 to 6 by July 2026).
- **Structural quality:** concentrated in a few severe cases (Chintels Paradiso) plus RWA self-reports (65+ high-rises in January 2024).
- **AQI, seismic risk and groundwater:** severe but region-wide, so they differentiate little between societies.
- **Noise, heat and high-tension lines:** thin NCR evidence at flat level.

**1c. Water supply and groundwater**

Demand is 815 MLD against 770 MLD supplied, projected to reach 1,135 MLD by 2031. MCG plans to shut 94 of 581 tubewells by January 2027. Of 60 builders and industrial units identified as extracting illegally, only 9 borewells have been sealed — [The Tribune, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/gurugram/gurugram-groundwater-falls-7-cm-annually-despite-monsoon-floods).

GMDA said society pipelines are the developer's responsibility (June 2024) — [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/200-societies-along-dwarka-e-way-suffer-at-the-hands-of-tanker-mafia-634420/).

### Inferences
Per-candidate design notes. "Predicts" means the outcome the score should be validated against.

**A. Transferability / clean ownership (combines candidates 2 and 3, plus OC integrity)**
- **Predicts:** probability that the buyer can register, get a loan, take possession and later resell without a legal or administrative block within X months. Also the expected extra cost to clear blockers (TM charges, dues, mutation).
- **Inputs:**
  - Project level: authority dues and registry-permission status (Noida, GNIDA, YEIDA lists; Amitabh Kant 25%-paid status); OC/CC dates and whether the OC is under the 2025–26 audit; RERA registration and complaints; litigation; lease type (Noida leasehold, TM schedule); builder-floor status (stilt+4, RERA exempt).
  - Unit level (user-uploaded documents): chain of deeds, GPA links, NOCs, mutation, CERSAI search.
- **Feasibility:** project level is feasible as a curated database for a few hundred Gurugram and Noida societies. Unit level needs document AI plus a lawyer-in-the-loop, consistent with Phase-1's "flag, not certify".
- **Wrong-risk:** very high reliance. Present as "checks done / issues found / not checked", never "title clear". Cite each flag's source document and date.
- **Precedents:** UK local-authority and environmental searches in conveyancing; Landeed; CRISIL CREST (legal-documentation pillar).

**B. Waterlogging and flood exposure (society level)**
- **Predicts:** probability that, in a typical monsoon, the society's access roads become impassable or the basement/stilt floods (with knock-on outages). A secondary output is riverine-flood exposure (Yamuna/Hindon floodplain).
- **Inputs:**
  - Observed: geocoded news and authority incident reports from 2016 onwards; resident-reported events with photos; insurance/vehicle claims if a partnership is possible.
  - Static: elevation relative to surroundings (DEM); distance to drains and their completion status (GMDA sector drainage); floodplain zone; basement depth.
  - Mitigation: GMDA works completed (Leg-4 drain etc.); society sump pumps; raised switchgear.
- **Feasibility:** medium. A v1 can use bands (Low / Moderate / High / Known-recurring) with an "evidence" panel. Official hotspot lists alone would mislead, because they cover roads and the official count fell from ~90 to 6 in July 2026 while society-level flooding persists.
- **Wrong-risk:** high, as the First Street and Zillow controversy shows. Mitigate by:
  - showing observed incidents separately from modelled exposure;
  - offering a dispute/right-of-reply process (First Street's refusal to update on individual disputes fuelled backlash);
  - avoiding probabilities with false precision.

**C. Society health and running-cost score (ready/resale)**
- **Predicts:** expected maintenance-charge escalation over the next 3 years; probability of a material service failure (water, power, lift, fire) in a year; governance risk (no AOA/RWA handover, membership disputes, litigation).
- **Inputs:**
  - Charges: current ₹/sq ft and history, compared with peer societies; IFMS refunded or not.
  - Governance: handover status and maintenance-agency ownership; RWA/AOA registration and elections; HRERA/UP-RERA and court cases.
  - Utilities: GMDA water connection vs tanker dependence; DHBVN/PVVNL connection type and outage history.
  - Compliance: lift registration (UP Act); fire NOC validity; structural-audit list membership.
  - Resident ratings.
- **Feasibility:** moderate. Public lists (audit lists, lift and fire non-compliance) seed it, and resident contributions scale it. This is the score most likely to become a moat.
- **Wrong-risk:** medium. Societies and builders may contest it, so show sources and dates.

**D. Fair-price range**
- As designed in Phase-1 (hedonic/GBM with society effects and conformal ranges). Add a "premium vs registered comps" indicator and a circle-rate floor.
- Mark low confidence where comparables are thin, for builder floors and for Noida unregistered stock.

**E. Affordability and total-cost stress test (buyer level)**
- **Predicts:** probability that the EMI exceeds a safe share of income under a +100–200 bp rate shock; all-in cost including stamp duty, GST, PLC/EDC, IFMS, TM, brokerage and maintenance.
- **Feasibility:** high, from user inputs.
- **Risk:** avoid personalised investment advice. Frame it as arithmetic.

**F. Lower-priority candidates for v1**
- **Liquidity and rental yield:** in v1, show proxies (society size, active listings vs units, registry status, metro distance). Build days-on-market from the platform's own listing logs before scoring.
- **Construction quality:** in v1, show known-issue flags (audit lists, Chintels-type orders, OC-audit membership) inside the society score. Predictive quality scoring belongs in the developer score (RERA complaints by developer).
- **Environmental nuisances:** show distance-based facts rather than a composite:
  - distance to Bandhwari/Ghazipur landfill;
  - inside the IGI 55 dB contour or not;
  - distance to HT lines and highways;
  - nearest CAAQMS reading with "station is X km away".
  Seismic zone is uniform (Zone IV), so instead show building age, design-code era and structural-audit flags.
- **Groundwater:** the extraction stage is city-wide, so the actionable society-level variable is "GMDA/authority piped supply vs borewell/tanker dependence".

## Key Question 3: How do global platforms present such scores, and what impact have they had?

### Takeaway
US portals use simple 1–10 or 0–100 scales with plain-language labels, a 30-year (mortgage-length) horizon and click-through explanations. Field evidence shows these scores change buyer behaviour and prices: in Redfin's randomised trial, buyers shown flood scores bid on homes with 54% less risk, and high-risk homes' prices fell 1.7%. That impact also triggers backlash when scores clash with lived experience. Zillow removed First Street climate scores from listings in late 2025 after California MLS complaints, while Redfin kept them. Crime scores were dropped by Realtor.com and Redfin in 2021 over bias concerns. The UK takes the regulatory route: mandatory "material information" disclosure plus colour-coded pass/"further action" conveyancing searches. Australia mostly offers paid or third-party reports rather than listing-page scores.

### Cited Findings
**US — climate/flood scores (First Street)**
- **Redfin's flood-score scale.** First Street's Flood Factor runs from 1 to 10: Minimal (1), Minor (2), Moderate (3–4), Major (5–6), Severe (7–8) and Extreme (9–10). It reflects the likelihood of flooding at least once over a 30-year mortgage. Redfin shows it in an expandable "Flood Risk" section with future risk, FEMA zone and insurance information — [Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/redfin-users-interact-with-flood-risk-data/).
- **Redfin's randomised trial.** It ran from 12 October 2020 to 3 January 2021 with 17.5M users. Users who had viewed homes averaging 8.5 went on to bid on homes averaging 3.9 (54% less risk); the control group stayed at 8.5. Search shifted 10% in week one and 25% after nine weeks. There was no significant effect for users browsing lower-risk homes (published 12 September 2022) — [Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/redfin-users-interact-with-flood-risk-data/).
  - When all viewers were treated, prices of high-risk homes fell about $7,000 (1.7%). The authors' linear extrapolation to Redfin's full market share is about 21% — [Banker & Tradesman](https://bankerandtradesman.com/large-study-of-home-searchers-finds-homes-flood-risk-substantially-lowers-buyer-interest/); [NBER w33119](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33119/w33119.pdf). The price figures come from search summaries and were not verified on the page.
- **Realtor.com** shows five First Street factors (flood, wildfire, heat, wind, air quality) on a 1–10 scale, with current risk and 15- and 30-year projections. It frames them as reducing "surprise costs related to insurance" (March 2024) — [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2024/03/13/realtor-com-listings-now-display-5-climate-risk-factors).
- **Zillow launch and removal.** Zillow launched climate risk scores (flood, wildfire, etc.) in September 2024, saying 80%+ of buyers consider climate risk. It removed them from more than 1 million listings around 1 December 2025 and replaced them with a link to First Street's site — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/01/zillow-drops-climate-risk-scores-after-agents-complained-of-lost-sales); [RISMedia](https://ace.rismedia.com/2025/12/02/zillow-removes-climate-risk-scores/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-removes-climate-risk-data/).
  - The trigger was the California Regional MLS (CRMLS). Its CEO Art Carter said predictive flood data "does not always align with observed conditions". He cited listings with "a very high projected chance of flooding… despite having no flood activity for decades", and said displaying flood probabilities "can have a significant impact on the perceived desirability". CRMLS asked Realtor.com, Redfin and Homes.com to remove such data too. Redfin still displays scores — [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/12/01/why-did-zillow-remove-climate-data-from-its-home-listings); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/01/zillow-drops-climate-risk-scores-after-agents-complained-of-lost-sales).
  - First Street's response: "the risk doesn't go away; it just moves from a pre-purchase decision into a post-purchase liability" — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/big-tech/zillow-removes-climate-risk-scores-after-agents-complain-about-sales-164405763.html); [RISMedia](https://ace.rismedia.com/2025/12/02/zillow-removes-climate-risk-scores/).
- **Insurance link.** California agents report that 14% of deals fell through over insurance in 2024, rising to 16%+ in 2025 — [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/12/01/why-did-zillow-remove-climate-data-from-its-home-listings).
- **Accuracy critiques**
  - In Asheville (NC), homes that stayed dry during Hurricane Helene carried 10/10 "extreme" flood scores. A realtor cited "20 properties that are not in a flood zone, did not flood, and have very high ratings". First Street removed one rating after admitting "an area of known limitation", but said "as a rule, we do not update factor scores based on individual requests or disputes" (July 2025) — [WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/local/western-north-carolina-wnc-property-homeowners-question-accuracy-climate-risk-ratings-impacting-home-sales-investigation-data-first-street-tech-asheville-hendersonville-realtor-flood-helene).
  - In Los Angeles, First Street and a detailed local model (PRIMo) diverged 4- to 18-fold at municipal scale, with a Model Agreement Index of 24% — [University of Miami dataset record](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/dataset/Comparing-first-street-foundation-and-PRIMo/991032075183802976).
  - Critics argue that a single 1–10 number per house "conveys some precision that isn't really there" — [SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/science/article/zillow-home-climate-risk-20147318.php) (as quoted in search summary).

HUD later said agents may lawfully discuss crime and school data, reversing Biden-era guidance (date not verified) — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/hud-says-realtors-can-discuss-crime-and-school-data/).

**US — Walk Score**
- Walk Score runs 0–100. It awards full points for amenities within a 5-minute walk (~0.25 mile) and none beyond 30 minutes (~1.5 miles), with adjustments for intersection density and block length — [Redfin: How Walk Score Works](https://www.redfin.com/how-walk-score-works).
- Impact: each extra point was associated with a $500–3,000 higher home value in typical metros, and above-average walkability with a $4,000–34,000 premium. Las Vegas showed a negative association and Bakersfield none — [Cortright, "Walking the Walk" (CEOs for Cities)](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/walking_the_walk_cortright.pdf).

**UK — disclosure and conveyancing searches**
- National Trading Standards' Material Information guidance covers Parts A, B and C:
  - Part B covers every property (type, construction, utilities, parking).
  - Part C applies only when relevant, for example flood risk: whether the property flooded in the last 5 years, the sources of risk (river, sea, groundwater, surface water) and defences. Coastal erosion and restrictive covenants are also covered.
  - Agents must disclose under consumer protection regulations.
  — [National Trading Standards](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/news/full-material-information-guidance-published/); [Rightmove Parts B & C factsheet](https://hub.rightmove.co.uk/content/uploads/2023/11/Material-Information-Parts-BC-Factsheet-Checklist-Final.pdf).
- Groundsure reports for conveyancers use "colour-coded risk ratings, clear pass and next-step indicators". A "Further Action" flood result prompts a property-specific flood report (£40.50). The Homebuyers report costs £111.12 — [Severn Trent Searches](https://www.severntrentsearches.com/residential-conveyancing-searches/flood-risk-searches/groundsure-flood-report/); [Severn Trent Searches](https://www.severntrentsearches.com/residential-conveyancing-searches/residential-environmental-searches/groundsure-homebuyers/).

**Australia**
- PropTrack and the Climate Council found about 2M homes (~17%) affected by flood risk. At least 70% of them are in areas where exposure lowers values, a combined A$42.2bn discount — [Climate Council](https://www.climatecouncil.org.au/resources/property-value-report-how-climate-change-could-worsen-australias-42-billion-flood-risk/); [Broker News](https://www.brokernews.com.au/news/breaking-news/flood-risk-slashes-42-billion-from-property-values-288278.aspx).
- Domain points buyers to a Climate Valuation tool. It projects inundation, bushfire, subsidence and wind impacts on value over a 30-year mortgage for any of 14.6M homes, as a purchasable report — [Domain](https://www.domain.com.au/advice/tools-for-home-buyers-to-assess-climate-change-risks-870093/).
- ClimateNest offers a 0–100 score per property combining 8 hazards from 17 official datasets — [ClimateNest](https://climatenest.org/).
- No evidence was found that realestate.com.au shows climate scores on listing pages.

### Inferences
- **Scale and labels.** Use a short ordinal scale with words (for example 5 bands: Low / Moderate / Elevated / High / Severe) rather than percentages or decimals. US practice pairs numbers with labels and a 30-year horizon. In NCR, the useful horizon for waterlogging and society risk is "typical monsoon" and "next 3 years", not 30-year climate projections.
- **Evidence over model.** The Zillow, CRMLS and Asheville episodes show that the trust-breaking failure is a modelled score contradicting lived history. An NCR score should lead with dated, sourced events ("basement flooded 1 Sep 2025 — 3 reports, 2 news items") and put modelled exposure second.
- **Dispute process.** First Street's refusal to update scores on individual disputes became a reputational liability. An NCR platform should accept RWA or seller evidence, show "under review" status and log changes.
- **Disclosure vs scoring.** The UK model (mandatory facts plus searches with pass/"further action") suits legal and transferability risk better than a numeric score. Use a checklist with green/amber/red and a "what to do next" action (for example "ask seller for Noida TM receipt").
- **Avoid crime and neighbourhood-composition scores.** The 2021 US removals over bias concerns suggest avoiding area-level "safety" scores built from thin NCRB district data. Community-level labels in India could also map onto caste or religion and create discrimination risk. This is an inference; no Indian precedent or regulation was researched.
- **Impact expectations.** The Redfin trial suggests risk scores shift demand mainly at the high-risk tail. Expect the waterlogging and transferability scores to matter most for the worst ~10–20% of societies, and expect organised pushback from sellers, brokers and developers in those societies, as with CRMLS.
- **Monetisation precedents.** UK searches (£40–111) and Australia's purchasable climate reports show buyers or conveyancers will pay for property-specific reports. Phase-1's AVM report (₹499 at Liases Foras) suggests the Indian price point. Combining these, a paid "society due-diligence report" is a plausible product (inference).
