# Legal/regulatory risk (India) of publishing developer and project risk scores, and how to design and explain consumer-facing risk scores

Research date: 18 October 2026. Scope: a platform that publishes a "credit score for real estate developers and projects" for buyers of under-construction flats in Delhi NCR. These are research notes, not legal advice. Items marked **[LAWYER REVIEW]** need an Indian advocate (defamation/media, RERA, data protection) to confirm before launch. All dates are as reported by the cited source.

---

## A1. Defamation exposure: civil and criminal law, defences, and real cases against critics, reviewers and rating publishers

### Takeaway
Defamation is the largest legal risk. Criminal defamation still exists in India (BNS s.356 replaced IPC 499/500 with the same substance). Builders and companies do sue critics and analysts, and Indian courts, the Delhi High Court especially, will grant interim takedown orders in some cases. But recent Supreme Court and High Court rulings protect consumer criticism that is fact-based, made in good faith and in temperate language, and they set a high bar for gagging publications before trial. A score built from documented facts, with a published methodology and presented as opinion, is much easier to defend than a bare label or adjectives like "fraud".

### Cited Findings
**Statute (criminal)**
- Under BNS 2023, criminal defamation moved from IPC ss.499/500 to s.356, and the substance of the law is largely unchanged. — [Niyam blog](https://niyam.ai/blog/defamation-law-india); [LawRato BNS 356](https://lawrato.com/bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita/bns-section-356)
- BNS s.356 punishment is simple imprisonment up to 2 years, or a fine, or both, or community service. The offence is non-cognizable and bailable. — [Urban Rights Legal, s.356 BNS](https://urbanrightslegal.com/laws/bns/section-356)
- The ten exceptions carried over from IPC 499 include: (1) true imputation for the public good; (3) conduct touching any public question; (6) merits of a public performance; (9) imputation in good faith to protect one's own or others' interests; (10) caution in good faith for the good of the recipient or the public. — [Urban Rights Legal](https://urbanrightslegal.com/laws/bns/section-356); [ApniLaw](https://www.apnilaw.com/bare-act/bns/section-356-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-bns-defamation/)
- **Truth works differently in criminal and civil cases.** Criminally, truth must be paired with "public good" (Exception 1). In civil defamation, truth (justification) alone is a complete defence. A true statement published out of malice can still attract criminal liability if public good is not shown. — [Legal Service India](https://www.legalserviceindia.com/Legal-Articles/defamation-under-bhartiya-nyaya-sanhita/) (secondary; consistent with the statutory text of Exception 1)
- The constitutional validity of criminal defamation was upheld in *Subramanian Swamy v. Union of India* (2016). — [Urban Rights Legal](https://urbanrightslegal.com/laws/bns/section-356); [Medianama, Sept 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-supreme-court-observation-decriminalising-defamation-journalism-india/)
- On 22 Sept 2025, while hearing The Wire's plea in the Amita Singh defamation case, Justice M.M. Sundresh said "I think time has come to decriminalise all this". Experts note the remark has no legal effect unless *Subramanian Swamy* is reviewed. — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/time-to-decriminalise-defamation-says-sc-while-hearing-plea-in-the-wire-case/); [DNA](https://www.dnaindia.com/india/report-supreme-court-big-remark-on-colonial-era-defamation-law-time-has-come-to-decriminalise-all-this-the-wire-petition-former-jnu-professor-amita-singh-legal-news-updates-3181245)

**Civil defamation of companies and analysts**
- Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas (5 Jul 2021) lists the elements of corporate defamation as: a false statement, referring to the plaintiff, published, and tending to harm reputation in the eyes of right-thinking people. Truth and bona fide fair criticism are the primary defences, and "statements based on factual analysis cannot be categorized as defamatory". — [CAM blog](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2021/07/corporate-defamation-a-perspective-on-analyst-reports/)
- In the Veritas–Indiabulls matter, the Delhi High Court criticised the company's aggressive response to analyst research and noted that litigation could have a "chilling effect" on publishing. Separately, Edelweiss filed a USD 100 million defamation suit against Moody's in the Bombay High Court over misreported numbers. — [CAM blog](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2021/07/corporate-defamation-a-perspective-on-analyst-reports/)
- *Jindal Power Ltd v. ICRA Ltd* (Delhi HC, 18 Aug 2020) treats credit ratings as expert opinions. The court will not interfere unless the rating is shown to be "perverse, arbitrary and mala fide". — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/54995907/); [Zenodo analysis](https://zenodo.org/records/7606495)

**Interim injunctions and SLAPP suits**
- *Bloomberg TV Production Services v. Zee Entertainment* (SC, 22 Mar 2024, 2024 INSC 255) set aside an ex parte order directing Bloomberg to take down an article. The Court held that pre-trial injunctions against journalistic content should be granted only in exceptional cases after applying the three-fold test (prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable harm), and that the test must not be applied mechanically. It expressly flagged that ad-interim injunctions can be misused in SLAPP suits. — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/106783348/); [SCC Online blog](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/03/26/sc-set-aside-order-directing-bloomberg-to-remove-article-on-zee-entertainment-legal-news/); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/03/223-supreme-court-sets-aside-bloomberg-take-down-order-zee-defamation/)

**Builder vs homebuyer, activist and reviewer cases**
- *Shahed Kamal v. A. Surti Developers Pvt Ltd* (SC, 17 Apr 2025, 2025 INSC 502). Homebuyers put up a banner listing grievances (no society formed, no accounts provided, construction defects, water and maintenance problems). The builder filed a criminal complaint under IPC 500. The SC quashed it under Exception 9 (good faith to protect interests), stressing the "mild and temperate language" and peaceful manner. It held: "A right to protest peacefully without falling foul of the law is a corresponding right, which the consumers ought to possess just as the seller enjoys his right to commercial speech." — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/peaceful-protest-by-consumer-a-corresponding-right-to-commercial-speech-by-seller-2025-insc-502-shahed-kamal-v-a-surti-developers-pvt-ltd-1574491); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/peaceful-protest-not-defamation-supreme-court-quashes-defamation-complaint-against-homebuyers-3498953)
- The banner was described as factual, without words like "fraud" or "cheating". — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/libel-defamation/1615158/homebuyers-victory-supreme-court-quashes-builders-defamation-claim)
- *Lodha v. Krishnaraj Rao* (Bombay HC, Justice Gautam Patel, 26 Apr 2019). Lodha sued over YouTube videos (including one in which a punched wall cracked) questioning construction quality at its New Cuffe Parade project, seeking ₹100 crore (suit filed 17 Jan 2019). The injunction was refused. The court found that Lodha "failed to make out a prima facie case even on these five statements", treated the statements as fair comment with a factual basis, said online journalism gets equal protection, and declined to order YouTube takedowns. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/lodha-defamation-case-judge-gautam-patel-strikes-down-injunction-request/57039.html); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/video/922069/bombay-high-court-win-for-activist-posting-videos-questioning-quality-of-lodha-group-constructions); [LatestLaws headline: "To say the emperor has no clothes is not defamation"](https://www.latestlaws.com/latest-news/to-say-the-emperor-has-no-clothes-is-not-defamation-bombay-hc-boost-free-speech/)
- **Contrast case:** House of Abhinandan Lodha Pvt Ltd filed a ₹15 crore defamation suit against Goan activist Swapnesh Sherlekar and others. The Bombay HC passed an interim order that the impugned statements "shall not further be disseminated to the public at large" until the next date. — [The Goan](https://www.thegoan.net/goa-news/%C3%AF%C2%BB%C2%BFrealty-firm-files-rs-15-cr-defamation-suit-against-activist/147924.html); [Goemkarponn](https://goemkarponn.com/bombay-hc-restrains-circulation-of-defamatory-videos-on-lodha/) (date and final outcome not established)
- The Madras HC (Crl.R.C. No. 445 of 2023) held that a negative Google review of services received is protected by Art. 19(1)(a) and is not defamation. It added that posting false, derogatory statements causing reputational harm would be defamation. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/madras-high-court/madras-high-court-right-to-free-speech-includes-right-to-share-reviews-on-online-platform-237667); [judgment PDF](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/vp-sarathi-v-s-kiruthigha-492975.pdf)

**Review sites and content creators**
- By Aug 2013, MouthShut.com had received 790+ takedown notices and 240 legal notices, and faced 11 court cases. The Bombay HC ordered it to delete critical user reviews of Sharda University. — [Medianama 2013](https://www.medianama.com/2013/08/223-mouthshut-coms-tally-over-790-takedown-notices-240-legal-notices-11-court-cases/); [Wikipedia: MouthShut.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MouthShut.com)
- MouthShut was a petitioner in the litigation that led to the Supreme Court striking down s.66A of the IT Act (24 Mar 2015). The Court read down s.79 and the IT Rules so that takedown obligations arise on a court order or government notification. — [Wikipedia: Mouthshut.com v. UoI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouthshut.com_versus_Union_of_India)
- The Delhi HC has recently ordered takedowns in creator cases. It directed YouTuber Shyam Meera Singh to take down a video on Isha Foundation and Sadhguru (2025). It later directed Google and Nakkheeran to remove 44 more videos in the Isha Foundation suit (reported 3 Aug 2026). ANI has sued YouTuber Mohak Mangal in the Delhi HC (2025). — [LiveLaw (Shyam Meera Singh)](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-orders-youtuber-shyam-meera-singh-to-take-down-defamatory-video-on-sadhguru-isha-foundation-286340); [SCC Online, Aug 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/08/03/delhi-high-court-directs-google-nakkheeran-remove-44-additional-videos-isha-foundation-defamation-suit/); [LiveLaw (ANI v Mangal)](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/ani-sues-mohak-mangal-defamation-copyright-extortion-video-293627)
- **John Doe order example:** On 10 Nov 2025 a Patiala House (Delhi) court directed removal of news reports and judicial records, including from Indian Kanoon and the Indian Express, in a defamation suit by a person discharged in a criminal case. The Delhi HC dismissed the appeal on 18 Dec 2025. The Supreme Court stayed both orders on 6 Feb 2026. — [Internet Freedom Foundation](https://internetfreedom.in/supreme-court-stays-order-invoking-the-right-to-be-forgotten-for-removal-of-news-reports-judicial-records-from-the-public-domain/); [Medianama Feb 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-supreme-court-right-to-be-forgotten-indian-express-privacy-case/). *Conflict:* one search summary links the person to the Moser Baer money-laundering case, while IFF says AgustaWestland. The underlying case is not material here.
- Glassdoor: an Indian court can direct Glassdoor to remove a review or to disclose the reviewer's account data (email, IP) in a defamation suit. — [RepuLex blog, 2026](https://www.repulex.com/blog/glassdoor-review-removal-india-what-works-2026) (secondary, from a reputation-management vendor; no specific Indian judgment identified)

### Inferences
- **The platform's own score is its own "publication"** (see A3). There is no intermediary shield, so the defences that matter are justification (truth), fair comment or honest opinion on true facts, and good faith or public good. The design should make every negative signal traceable to a verifiable public record such as a RERA order, QPR filing, court case number or MCA charge.
- The pattern in the cases is consistent. Courts protect consumer speech that is factual, temperate and in good faith (*Shahed Kamal*, *Lodha v Rao*, Madras HC). Courts restrain speech that alleges extortion or fraud without a factual basis, or that is personally abusive. Use descriptive, data-anchored labels ("12 RERA complaints upheld in the last 24 months") rather than characterisations ("fraud builder", "scam").
- The *Jindal Power v ICRA* standard ("perverse, arbitrary, mala fide") suggests courts may defer to a documented, consistently applied expert methodology. This is a reason to publish the methodology and to apply it the same way to every developer. Whether that deference extends to an unregulated private scorer is untested. **[LAWYER REVIEW]**
- NCR developers are likely to sue in the Delhi HC, which has granted takedowns in creator cases. *Bloomberg v Zee* is the best shield against ex parte gag orders, so keep a litigation-ready evidence file for every score (source documents, timestamps, version of the model).
- Criminal complaints can be filed by a developer or its promoters personally. Founders face personal criminal process risk (summons, appearance), even if they ultimately win.

### Gaps
- I found no Indian case on an algorithmic or data-driven risk score or rating of a real estate developer or project by a private platform or property portal. This would be a novel fact pattern.
- I could not retrieve specific Delhi HC John Doe ("Ashok Kumar") orders in corporate defamation (vs. IP) cases. The search budget was exhausted and fallback search engines returned CAPTCHAs.
- I found no India-specific Glassdoor judgment and no final outcome for *Lodha v Sherlekar* (Goa) or *Lodha v Rao* at trial.

## A2. Is "rating" or "credit rating" a regulated term in India? SEBI, RBI, ESG precedent, MahaRERA and UP-RERA grading, and safe terminology

### Takeaway
SEBI's CRA Regulations cover the business of rating securities that are listed or proposed to be listed. RBI-linked regimes cover bank loans, NBFC deposits and similar instruments. A score on a housing project or developer for homebuyers is not a securities rating, so SEBI registration does not appear to be required. However, SEBI ring-fences its standardized rating symbols (AAA–D) even within CRA groups, ESG ratings were brought under SEBI registration in 2023, and incumbents brand their real estate products as "grading". Avoid "credit rating", "rating agency" and SEBI rating symbols, and use "risk score", "grade" or "assessment" with an explicit "not a credit rating" disclaimer.

### Cited Findings
- SEBI's FAQ (2 Mar 2026) defines a CRA as "a body corporate which is engaged in, or proposes to be engaged in, the business of rating of securities that are listed or proposed to be listed on a stock exchange recognized by the SEBI". A credit rating is an opinion on the likelihood of a debt obligation being repaid in full and on time. — [SEBI FAQs on CRAs, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
- SEBI regulates ratings of listed or to-be-listed securities and other ratings required under SEBI regulations. CRAs also rate instruments under other regulators' guidelines. For example, RBI accredits CRAs for bank loan ratings, independent credit evaluation of resolution plans, and NBFC public deposit ratings. — [SEBI FAQs, Q5](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
- SEBI standardised rating symbols and definitions (circular dated 15 Jun 2011). Examples: AAA = "highest degree of safety regarding timely servicing of financial obligations", D = in default or expected to default soon. Symbols must carry the CRA's name as a prefix. — [SEBI FAQs, Q8](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
- SEBI's CRA ring-fencing rules require non-SEBI-regulated activities to sit in a separate business unit or entity. Credit rating scales prescribed by SEBI "shall not be used by any non-rating entities of the CRA", and marketing for non-SEBI activities must carry explicit disclaimers. — [PreRatings](https://preratings.com/article.php?category=news&id=3); [TaxGuru (operational circular)](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-issues-operational-circular-credit-rating-agencies.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/sebi-directs-credit-rating-agencies-to-up-firewall-with-non-rating-entities-122092101169_1.html)
- A SEBI consultation (9 Jul 2025) proposed letting CRAs rate products under other regulators' purview through separate units, with disclosures that such activities lack "SEBI investor protection mechanisms". It does not address whether non-registered entities may use rating terms. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/measures-regulation-activities-credit-rating-agencies-cras.html); [SEBI board meeting file, Jan 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/jan-2026/1767338482565_1.pdf)
- **ESG precedent:** SEBI amended the CRA Regulations (notification dated 3 Jul 2023) to create ESG Rating Providers (ERPs). No person may act as an ERP without a SEBI certificate, and anyone providing ESG ratings to listed entities must be registered. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/sebi-introduces-framework-for-esg-rating-providers/); [SEBI ERP FAQs, Jan 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/jan-2025/1737114577492.pdf)
- **Incumbent terminology:** CRISIL offers "Real Estate Developer Grading" and "Real Estate Project Grading". Its real estate star grading uses a 0–7 scale and states that it denotes CRISIL's opinion on ability to execute to stated quality and timelines, financial stability and ability to transfer clear title. CRISIL launched project grading in 2010. — [CRISIL Developer Grading](https://intelligence.crisil.com/en/homepage/what-we-do/risk-solutions/assessments/sector-grading/real-estate/crisil-real-estate-developer-grading.html); [CRISIL Project Grading](https://intelligence.crisil.com/en/homepage/what-we-do/risk-solutions/assessments/sector-grading/real-estate/crisil-real-estate-project-grading.html); [Square Yards blog](https://www.squareyards.com/blog/real-estate-rating-system-in-india)
- **RERA statute:** s.32(f) of RERA 2016 lists among the Authority's promotional functions "measures to encourage grading of projects on various parameters of development including grading of promoters". — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/grading-promoters-rera-2016.html); [RERA Act text (UP-RERA)](https://www.up-rera.in/pdf/reraact.pdf); [IBC Law s.32](https://ibclaw.in/section-32-of-real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016-rera-functions-of-authority-for-promotion-of-real-estate-sector/)
- **MahaRERA grading:** Circular No. 46/2023 (28 Aug 2023) created a "Grading Matrix" with four snapshots: project overview, technical (QPRs, % completion, time elapsed, bookings), financial (audit reports, CA qualifications, penalties, encumbrances) and legal (complaints, warrants, NCLT). It was to be generated every six months, starting with 1 Oct 2023 – 31 Mar 2024. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/understanding-mahareras-new-grading-matrix-for-real-estate.html)
- In Oct 2024 MahaRERA halted the grading because RERA 2016 "does not empower regulators to assign grades to projects". The initiative had been prompted by a Bombay HC query on whether MahaRERA could rate or classify developers. — [PropNewsTime, 23 Oct 2024](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTE0NTU=/maharera-halts-real-estate-project-grading-initiative-due-to-regulatory-constraints)
- **UP-RERA (covers Noida, Greater Noida and Ghaziabad):** On 22 Apr 2020 UP-RERA invited comments (due 13 May 2020) on a grading framework for projects and promoters. Parameters were financial quality, organisational structure and certificates, track record, compliance and customer feedback. Grades were to be published on the UP-RERA portal and renewed periodically. — [TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/8778/uttar-pradesh-rera-issues-grading-framework-for-real-estate-projects-a/); [UP-RERA framework PDF](https://up-rera.in/frm_Ifram_for_PDf.aspx?Param=111UP_RERA_CRISIL_Grading_Framework.pdf). Implementation status was not confirmed.
- The SEBI FAQ says a rating is "not a recommendation to buy, hold or sell". Because "there is no contract between an investor and a rating agency", the investor is free to accept or reject the opinion. — [SEBI FAQs, Q3 & Q12](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)

### Inferences
- A homebuyer-facing project or developer score is not a "rating of securities", so SEBI CRA registration does not appear to be triggered. Three things would change that: rating a developer's NCDs or bonds, selling scores to lenders or investors for credit decisions, or rating listed developers in a way that could be framed as investment research. **[LAWYER REVIEW]**, particularly if the platform later sells developer analytics to NBFCs or AIFs, which could touch the SEBI Research Analyst or CRA perimeter.
- **Terminology to use:** "Delivery Risk Score", "Project Risk Grade", "Developer Track-Record Index", "Buyer Risk Assessment".
- **Terminology to avoid:**
  - "credit rating" and "rating agency": these suggest a SEBI or RBI-regulated status, which risks a misleading claim (see A3).
  - "credit score": this suggests a credit bureau or Credit Information Company product. **[LAWYER REVIEW]** under CICRA 2005, which was not researched.
  - The SEBI symbol set (AAA, AA+, BBB-, D) and "investment grade".
- Marketing "a credit score for builders" as an analogy is probably fine as a tagline. The product label itself should differ, with a disclaimer such as: "Not a credit rating under SEBI (CRA) Regulations, 1999; not issued or endorsed by any RERA."
- MahaRERA's retreat (no statutory power) and UP-RERA's unconfirmed framework show that regulators see grading as valuable (s.32(f)) but have not delivered it. This is a market gap. It also means the platform cannot claim regulatory endorsement.

### Gaps
- I found no explicit statute or RBI statement prohibiting non-registered entities from using the words "rating" or "score" for non-financial products.
- CICRA 2005 (credit information companies) and any RBI view on "credit score" terminology were not researched.
- Whether UP-RERA's 2020 grading framework was ever implemented was not established.

## A3. RERA, consumer protection (misleading information, unfair trade practices) and IT Act liability (publisher vs intermediary)

### Takeaway
No RERA provision was found that bars a private platform from grading RERA-registered projects. If the platform facilitates sales for commission, it becomes a RERA "real estate agent" with registration and truthfulness obligations. Consumer protection law adds exposure in both directions. Overclaiming the score's reliability ("RERA-verified safe project") can be misleading advertising. False, disparaging facts about a developer's services are listed as an unfair trade practice. The score is the platform's own content, so IT Act s.79 safe harbour does not apply to it.

### Cited Findings
- RERA s.11(2) requires any advertisement of a project to show the RERA registration number and the State RERA website address. Common misleading patterns include "ready to move" claims without an Occupation Certificate, and "RERA approved" when RERA registration implies no quality endorsement. — [CCPA/RERA summary via search; TaxGuru "Say what you mean"](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/esay-mean-misleading-real-estate-ads-rera-asci.html)
- RERA s.9 requires registration of any person facilitating sale or purchase of units in a registered project (valid 5 years). Registered agents must not facilitate sales in unregistered projects or provide false information about projects. — [IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/rera-agent-registration) (secondary)
- CPA 2019 s.2(47)(j) lists as an unfair trade practice giving "false or misleading facts disparaging the goods, services or trade of another person". — [Indian Kanoon s.2(47)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/117738049/); [CPA 2019 text, NCDRC](https://ncdrc.nic.in/bare_acts/CPA2019.pdf)
- The CCPA Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements (June 2022) apply to all advertisements in any medium and to advertisers, agencies and endorsers. Disclaimers "shall not attempt to hide material information". Penalties run up to ₹10 lakh, and up to ₹50 lakh for subsequent contraventions. — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1832906&reg=48&lang=2); [Khaitan ERGO](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2022-06/Ergo_11062022.pdf)
- IT Act s.79(1) protects an intermediary for "third party information, data, or communication link made available or hosted by him". This is conditional on the intermediary not initiating the transmission, not selecting the receiver, and not selecting or modifying the information (s.79(2)(b)). Protection is lost on conspiracy or on failing to act after actual knowledge or government notice (s.79(3)). — [Indian Kanoon, s.79](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/844026/)
- Commentary describes safe harbour as available to platforms acting as a "passive conduit" that "did not create, start, or modify the content". — [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/section-79-of-it-act/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/safe-harbour-defence-in-india-from-the-presprective-of-information-technology-laws)
- After *Shreya Singhal* (2015), s.79 takedown obligations are read as arising from a court order or government notification. — [Wikipedia: Mouthshut.com v. UoI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouthshut.com_versus_Union_of_India)

### Inferences
- **The score, labels, reason codes and AI-written summaries are first-party publications.** Section 79 cannot shield them. User reviews and comments hosted on the platform could get intermediary protection if IT Rules due diligence is followed, but only if the platform does not "select or modify" them. Keep editorial scoring and user content structurally separate.
- **Two-way consumer-law exposure:**
  1. *Towards buyers.* Claims such as "safe", "guaranteed on-time" or "verified" invite misleading-ad complaints to the CCPA if a highly rated project then stalls. Use risk language ("lower observed risk"), not assurance language.
  2. *Towards developers.* If the platform is itself a trader or service provider (brokerage, leads, paid listings), a developer could argue that a wrong negative score is "false or misleading facts disparaging" its services under s.2(47)(j). Accuracy controls and a correction process are therefore a legal requirement, not just a UX feature. **[LAWYER REVIEW]** on standing and forum (CCPA vs civil court).
- If the business model involves commissions on bookings, RERA agent registration in Haryana and UP (and Delhi if relevant) is needed. Agent duties around not giving false information then sit alongside defamation exposure. A pure information or subscription model avoids the agent perimeter. **[LAWYER REVIEW]**
- No RERA rule was found restricting third-party comparison or grading. RERA advertising rules bind promoters and agents. If developers quote the platform's grade in ads, the platform should have terms-of-use rules on how its grade may be cited, to avoid being drawn into misleading-ad complaints. The iCIRT misrepresentation episode in B2 is a cautionary example.

### Gaps
- I could not research state-specific RERA advertisement regulations (HRERA, UP-RERA) or any RERA order against property portals.
- The Consumer Protection (Dark Patterns) Guidelines 2023 and E-Commerce Rules 2020 were not researched. They may apply if the platform sells to buyers.
- I found no case applying s.2(47)(j) to an information platform's rating.

## A4. Data legality: reusing RERA, court and MCA data, licences and scraping, and the DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025

### Takeaway
Facts themselves are not copyrightable, and government portals generally allow reproduction with attribution. The standard Indian government copyright clause requires material to be reproduced "accurately" and not "in a derogatory manner or in a misleading context", which is awkward for a risk-scoring product. Contractual and technical restrictions (site terms, IT Act s.43) and database-copyright claims (*OLX*) make aggressive scraping riskier than official downloads or APIs.

On personal data, the DPDP Act's exclusion for data made public "under a legal obligation" plausibly covers MCA filings and court records. Commentators read it narrowly, and it may not cover scraped or aggregated data. There is no journalism exemption, and DPDP accuracy and correction duties apply where the exclusion does not. Courts are also expanding de-indexing ("right to be forgotten") of criminal case records against named individuals.

### Cited Findings
**Government data licences and portal terms**
- The Government Open Data Licence – India (GODL), gazette-notified 13 Feb 2017, gives a worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive licence to use, adapt and publish shareable non-sensitive government data for lawful commercial and non-commercial purposes. It requires attribution of the provider, source and licence (DOI, URL or URI). It is linked to NDSAP (17 Mar 2012). — [GODL (data.gov.in)](https://jk.data.gov.in/godl); [Wikipedia: NDSAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Data_Sharing_and_Accessibility_Policy)

**Scraping and database rights**
- IKIGAI Law (2 Jul 2020) makes three points:
  - In *OLX v. Padawan*, the Delhi HC granted a permanent injunction against scraping OLX's data, which was treated as a proprietary database and original literary work.
  - Website terms prohibiting scraping are enforceable as electronic contracts.
  - IT Act s.43 penalises extracting data "without the owner's permission", and courts have not settled whether this applies to public data.
  — [IKIGAI Law](https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/263/legality-of-data-scraping-in-india)
- *ANI v. OpenAI* (Delhi HC, order 24 Jul 2026) dismissed ANI's interim injunction. The court held that "copyright protects only the original expression of a work and not the underlying facts", that public availability does not remove copyright in expression, and that training use was fair dealing. It also noted ANI had not used available opt-out mechanisms against crawling. — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/intellectual-property/indias-first-ai-copyright-ruling-the-delhi-high-court-in-ani-v-openai); [Order PDF (mirror)](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/ANI-v.-OpenAI-Delhi-HC-July-24-2026.pdf)

**DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025**
- The DPDP Rules were notified on 13–14 Nov 2025. Implementation is phased over 18 months, ending 12–13 May 2027, with core obligations (notice and consent, purpose limitation, erasure workflows, security) due within that window. — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014&reg=3&lang=2); [Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-rules-2025-india-data-protection-law-compliance-40769.html/)
- s.3(c)(ii) excludes personal data made publicly available by the Data Principal, or by another person under a legal obligation to publish it. Corporate filings, court records and regulatory disclosures are examples where law requires publication. The test is narrow: data obtained by circumventing technical or contractual restrictions, aggregation or enrichment, or scraping that violates terms may not qualify, and companies should document their basis. — [India Briefing, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html); [Law School Policy Review, Jan 2026](https://lawschoolpolicyreview.com/2026/01/13/publicly-available-data-under-the-dpdp-act-the-limits-of-exemptions-in-ai-driven-processing/)

- The constitutionality of s.3(c)(ii) has been questioned on proportionality grounds. — [Indian Law Review 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/24730580.2026.2619382)
- There is no journalistic exemption. The Editors Guild sought a class exemption under s.17(5). The research exemption (s.17(2)(b)) is unavailable where data is used to "take a decision specific to the Data Principal". s.12 provides correction and erasure rights. — [Medianama, Feb 2024](https://www.medianama.com/2024/02/223-editors-urges-ministry-exempt-journalists-provisions-data-protection-act/); [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-of-india-explained/)
- s.8(3): where personal data is likely to be used to make a decision affecting the Data Principal, or is disclosed to another Data Fiduciary, the fiduciary must ensure its completeness, accuracy and consistency. — [DPDPA s.8 commentary](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html); [MeitY Act PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)

**Court records and the right to be forgotten**
- On 24 Jul 2024 the SC stayed a Madras HC direction (3 Mar 2024) requiring Indian Kanoon to take down a judgment. The CJI said "once the judgement is delivered it is part of the public record", distinguishing masking of names (permissible) from removing judgments. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-to-settle-law-on-right-to-be-forgotten-stays-hc-direction-to-indiankanoon-to-pull-down-judgment-264401)
- In *Laksh Vir Singh Yadav v. UoI* (Delhi HC, Justice Sachin Datta, June 2026, 31+ petitions), the court directed Google and other search engines to de-index name-based results for judgments and reportage in cases ending in acquittal, discharge, quashing or settlement. Indian Kanoon must restrict name-based search for those records, while access by case number or citation remains. Indian Kanoon has reportedly challenged the order (July 2026). — [SCC Online, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/06/03/del-hc-verdict-on-de-indexing-masking-and-right-to-be-forgotten/); [Medianama Jun 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-google-de-indexing-indian-kanoon-search-restrictions-right-to-be-forgotten/); [Medianama Jul 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-indian-kanoon-petition-delhi-hc-right-to-be-forgotten-order/)

### Inferences
- **Preferred data acquisition order:**
  1. Official downloads, APIs or data.gov.in datasets under GODL.
  2. Manual or low-rate collection with attribution.
  3. Automated scraping only after reviewing each portal's terms and robots.txt.

  Keep provenance (URL, timestamp, hash) for every data point. This doubles as defamation evidence and as documentation for DPDP s.3(c)(ii).
- The "derogatory manner or misleading context" clause in RERA copyright policies is a plausible argument a developer or regulator could raise against a negative grade built from RERA data. Mitigations: reproduce RERA facts verbatim with links, separate "facts from RERA" from "our assessment", and attribute clearly. **[LAWYER REVIEW]**
- **Personal data:** score companies and projects (non-personal data), not individuals. Where promoter or director names, DINs or individual criminal cases appear:
  - Rely on s.3(c)(ii) only for data published under legal obligation (MCA, RERA promoter disclosures, court orders).
  - Do not surface criminal cases that ended in acquittal, discharge or quashing (aligned with *Laksh Vir Singh Yadav*).
  - Build a DPDP-grade correction and erasure intake by May 2027 anyway.
- s.8(3) accuracy duties map well onto a "data correction request" workflow for developers and promoters, which also reduces defamation risk.

## A5. Conflict of interest: commissions and leads from developers, the issuer-pays critique, and SEBI CRA and Research Analyst rules as a voluntary benchmark

### Takeaway
Earning from the entities being scored is the main credibility and legal risk multiplier. Issuer-pays is widely blamed for rating failures in the 2008 global crisis and in India's IL&FS collapse, and iCIRT's developer-paid, opt-in model has drawn criticism. SEBI's CRA and RA regimes give a ready benchmark: disclose conflicts and compensation, publish methodology and rationale, monitor continuously, and wall off commercial teams from rating teams.

### Cited Findings
- In India the debt issuer pays for the credit rating (the "issuer-pays" model). — [SEBI FAQs, Q9](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
- SEBI requires CRAs to disclose information that may materially bear on ratings, "any sources of conflicts of interest", the rating methodology and the rationale. Conflicts include key managerial personnel having any relationship with or interest in the rated entity or its affiliates. — [SEBI FAQs, Q17–18](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
- After 2008, criticism of CRAs centred on the issuer-paid conflict, with mortgage-backed securities carrying top ratings that proved far too optimistic. — [Columbia Business Law Review / academic commons](https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/tfjw-9h64/download); [Oxford Business Law Blog](https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/business-law-blog/blog/2022/03/conflict-interest-investor-paid-credit-ratings)
- **IL&FS:** a forensic audit found that CRAs gave top ratings despite weak financials. It flagged a potential conflict because IL&FS and IFIN held about 5–9% of CARE from 2007 to 2013. Ratings fell from AAA to D within days, and SEBI widened its probe into five CRAs. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pti-stories/il-fs-case-sebi-widens-probe-into-role-of-rating-agencies-119072100157_1.html); [BusinessToday](https://businesstoday.in/current/corporate/ilfs-crisis-sebi-expands-probe-in-role-of-credit-rating-agencies/story/366369.html); [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/business/ilfs-moodys-fitch-care-icra-rating-companies/amp)
- **SEBI RA amendment (16 Dec 2024):** research reports must disclose any financial interest in the subject company, any compensation received from it, any investment banking relationship, and any material conflict. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-research-analysts-amendment-regulations-2024.html); [Compliance Calendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/sebi-research-analyst-third-amendment-regulations-2024)
- **CRA ring-fencing:** non-SEBI activities must run through a separate business unit with Chinese walls, on an arm's-length, fee-based basis. — [PreRatings](https://preratings.com/article.php?category=news&id=3)
- **iCIRT funding and publication:** the rated business pays, with pricing by report type. Only businesses with 3 or more gold stars appear on the public register, and Standard Report holders cannot market their rating. — [iCIRT FAQs](https://icirt.com/business/faqs-business)
- **iCIRT criticism (1 Nov 2023):**
  - A developer's sales representative claimed a five-star iCIRT rating that did not exist.
  - The opt-in design lets developers avoid scrutiny ("local reputation was good").
  - Detailed reports cost upwards of A$1,750 + GST.
  — [Flat Chat](https://www.flatchat.com.au/developers-fake-icrt-ratings/)
- In 2024 Trustpilot changed its process after "some businesses were abusing our reporting tools to suppress negative feedback". — [Trustpilot Trust Report 2025](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/trust/trust-report-2025)

### Inferences
- **Credibility:** if developers pay (leads, featured listings, commissions), buyers and journalists will reasonably suspect that scores bend. That undermines the core value proposition.
- **Legal:** a developer whose score fell could argue malice or bias (defeating good faith and fair comment) if it can show that competing developers who pay were scored favourably. Conflicts are therefore direct defamation exposure, not just a reputational issue.
- **Recommended voluntary standard, adapted from SEBI CRA and RA rules:**
  - Scores are never sold, and no developer can pay to change, suppress or pre-review a score.
  - A firewall separates the scoring and methodology team from sales and partnerships, and sales staff cannot see pre-publication scores.
  - Every project and developer page carries a disclosure: "We earn [commission / lead fees / advertising] from [this developer / X% of developers]. Commercial relationships do not affect scores (see policy)."
  - Founders and staff publish any holdings or relationships with rated developers.
  - Publish an annual "conflicts and integrity" report covering how many scores were disputed, changed and why.
- **Avoid iCIRT's design flaws:**
  - Score every RERA-registered project in scope, so developers cannot opt out of low grades.
  - Show "Insufficient data" rather than hiding the project.
  - Ban developers from quoting the grade except via a verifiable badge or link that shows the current score and date.
- Monetising buyers (subscriptions, reports, legal-check services) or banks and insurers (with care) is structurally cleaner than developer-paid revenue. If developer revenue is unavoidable, prefer flat, non-performance-based fees disclosed on-page.

### Gaps
- I found no Indian property-portal-specific disclosure norms for paid listings vs ratings.
- I found no empirical study on how disclosed conflicts change consumer trust in property ratings.

## A6. Mitigations and dispute processes: methodology, right of reply, disclaimers, insurance, and how Glassdoor, Trustpilot, Zillow/Redfin, MahaRERA, iCIRT and CRAs handle disputes

### Takeaway
Defensible rating systems combine:
- a published methodology,
- a defined route to see the evidence behind a rating and correct factual errors,
- final editorial discretion retained by the publisher,
- transparency about disputes and outcomes,
- continuous monitoring.

The weakest public example (iCIRT) is criticised for having no formal appeal. The strongest (SEBI-regulated CRAs) require continuous monitoring, public rationale, and a non-cooperation label instead of silence.

### Cited Findings
- **Glassdoor:** removes reviews only for guideline or terms violations, not because an employer disagrees. Employers flag via the Employer Center with reasons such as false information, harassment or confidential data. A decision reportedly comes within about 72 business hours. Glassdoor states that final content decisions are at its sole discretion. — [RetainTrust](https://retaintrust.com/how-to-remove-negative-glassdoor-reviews/); [Minc Law](https://www.minclaw.com/remove-negative-fake-glassdoor-reviews/) (secondary, vendor sources)
- **Trustpilot:**
  - "Transparent Flagging" shows publicly how a business reports reviews. — [Trustpilot Help](https://help.trustpilot.com/s/article/Transparent-Flagging-See-how-a-business-is-reporting-its-reviews?language=en_US)
  - In 2024 it removed 4.5 million fake reviews (7.4% of submissions; 90% removed automatically). Businesses flagged 61,000 reviews and consumers 92,000. It issued 19,000 warnings and 5,000 cease-and-desist letters, and had 9,000 active consumer warnings on business profiles. — [Trustpilot Trust Report 2025](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/trust/trust-report-2025)
  - Disputes go to a senior Content Integrity reviewer, typically in 3–10 business days. — [Reverbico](https://reverbico.com/blog/remove-trustpilot-reviews/) (secondary)
- **Redfin (climate scores):** sellers who believe a listing's climate information is inaccurate may request its removal. — [HousingWire, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/redfin-climate-risk-transparency/)
- **iCIRT:**
  - To dispute, a business first requests a copy of the individual searches conducted on it, then escalates by email. — [iCIRT FAQs](https://icirt.com/business/faqs-business)
  - Equifax consults the professional during assessment to "correct any errors of fact". — [Allens, 24 Jun 2022](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2022/06/Rate-my-builder-next-step-in-NSW-Government-rating-regime/)
  - "No judicial review mechanism currently exists to challenge a rating", which Allens says would be necessary if the scheme became mandatory. — [Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2022/06/Rate-my-builder-next-step-in-NSW-Government-rating-regime/)
- **SEBI CRAs:**
  - CRAs must continuously monitor ratings during the life of a security, run periodic reviews, and publish changes through press releases. Ratings change because "data or information can change significantly over time". — [SEBI FAQs, Q11, Q15–16](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
  - If a rated entity does not cooperate, it is labelled "Issuer Not Cooperating (INC)" and the rating continues on the "best available information". — [SEBI FAQs, Q19](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
- **MahaRERA:** its matrix was framed as automated information disclosure from developer-filed data (QPRs, audits, complaints) rather than subjective grades, and was then halted for lack of statutory power. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/understanding-mahareras-new-grading-matrix-for-real-estate.html); [PropNewsTime](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTE0NTU=/maharera-halts-real-estate-project-grading-initiative-due-to-regulatory-constraints)
- **Insurance:** media liability insurance covers claims arising from published content (defamation, copyright, privacy, product disparagement), including defence costs and settlements, and is often part of professional indemnity cover. — [BimaKavach (India)](https://www.bimakavach.com/blog/media-liability-insurance-guide/). The same source says a digital news outlet's policy covered a ₹50 lakh suit in 2023, but this is anecdotal and unverified.
- **Disclaimers:** the CCPA 2022 guidelines say disclaimers cannot hide material information. — [Khaitan ERGO](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2022-06/Ergo_11062022.pdf). The SEBI FAQ language ("not a recommendation", "the investor is free to accept or reject the opinion") is a usable template. — [SEBI FAQs](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)

### Inferences
**Recommended mitigation stack (each item maps to a defence or a regulator expectation; [LAWYER REVIEW] on wording):**
1. **Publish the methodology**: factors, weights or feature families, data sources, refresh cadence, version number. This supports fair comment and shows the score was not perverse, arbitrary or mala fide.
2. **Separate facts from opinion on the page.** For example, "Facts from public records (linked)" and "Our assessment: Elevated delay risk (opinion based on Methodology v2.3)". This supports the fact/opinion split and the RERA "not misleading" clause.
3. **Pre-publication notice and right of reply for downgrades into the bottom bands**, for example 7 days to submit factual corrections. Publish the developer's response next to the score, CRA- and iCIRT-style. This shows good faith.
4. **Dispute SLA and public log**: acknowledge in 48 hours, decide in 15 working days, keep editorial discretion, and publish aggregate dispute statistics, Trustpilot-style.
5. **Factual-correction fast lane** for wrong data points (e.g., a RERA complaint mis-attributed to the wrong entity). This meets DPDP s.8(3) and s.12 where personal data is involved.
6. **"Developer not cooperating" or "insufficient data" labels** rather than suppressing or guessing, following the SEBI INC concept.
7. **Language rules**: no "fraud", "scam", "cheat" or "blacklist". Use temperate, descriptive terms (*Shahed Kamal*).
8. **Do not score individuals.** Exclude acquitted, discharged or quashed criminal matters.
9. **Insurance**: buy media liability / E&O (professional indemnity) cover that names defamation and product disparagement before launch.
10. **Litigation readiness**: keep an evidence vault per score, and retain a counsel template for opposing ex parte injunctions citing *Bloomberg v Zee*.
11. **Terms of use for developers quoting grades**: only through a live verification link, to avoid iCIRT-style misrepresentation.

- I could not verify Glassdoor's or Trustpilot's dispute SLAs from primary help pages; the figures come from secondary vendor sources.
- I did not research whether Indian CRAs share draft ratings with issuers before publication (a common global practice).

## B1. How to communicate consumer-facing risk scores: formats and risk-communication research

### Takeaway
The research favours the following:
- categorical bands with plain-language labels and published thresholds (Walk Score, Redfin and First Street, SEBI symbol definitions);
- probabilities expressed as natural frequencies ("about 3 in 10 similar projects…") with icon arrays for low-numeracy users;
- verbal terms always paired with numbers;
- explicit uncertainty, which improves decisions and preserves trust when predictions miss.

Star ratings carry a "reviews" mental model and suffer positivity bias. Traffic lights grab attention but have mixed comprehension evidence. Fine-grained numbers like 300–900 suggest false precision unless the model is very well calibrated.

### Cited Findings
**Credit-score style**
- The CIBIL score is a 3-digit summary from 300 to 900. Closer to 900 means a higher chance of loan approval. Around 750+ is commonly described as strong and below 650 as difficult. — [CIBIL free score page (title/landing)](https://www.cibil.com/freecibilscore); [CreditAssist 2026](https://creditassist.in/blog/credit-score-how-credit-scores-are-calculated-in-india-the-factors-that-matter)
- Factor weights often quoted for CIBIL (payment history 35%, utilisation 30%, etc.) come from third-party blogs and simulators, not official TransUnion CIBIL disclosures. — [Paytm blog](https://paytm.com/blog/credit-score/how-cibil-score-is-calculated-key-factors-determining-your-creditworthiness/); [Priyanka Personal Finance simulator](https://www.priyankapersonalfinance.com/credit-score-simulator) (unofficial)
- Credit Karma labels each credit factor's impact as HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW. — [Credit Karma UK help](https://help.creditkarma.co.uk/articles/en_US/Knowledge/What-key-factors-impact-my-credit-score); [Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/credit/i/what-affects-your-credit-scores)
- Credit Karma has had to explain repeatedly why its VantageScore 3.0 differs from the FICO scores lenders use ("you have many different credit scores based on dozens of credit scoring models"). — [Credit Karma: Is Credit Karma accurate?](https://www.creditkarma.com/credit/i/is-credit-karma-accurate); [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/credit-karma-vs-fico-credit-scores/)
- SEBI's AAA–D symbols come with verbal definitions ("highest degree of safety… lowest credit risk"). Modifiers (+/−) need explaining: "the minus symbol… has no negative connotations", and AA− is stronger than A+. — [SEBI FAQs, Q8](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)

**Banded indices**
- Walk Score (0–100) publishes named bands: 90–100 Walker's Paradise, 70–89 Very Walkable, 50–69 Somewhat Walkable, 25–49 Car-Dependent, 0–24 Car-Dependent (almost all errands). Its distance-decay method and data sources are published. — [Walk Score Methodology](https://www.walkscore.com/methodology.shtml)
- Redfin/First Street show a 1–10 scale with shared labels across hazards: 1 minimal, 2 minor, 3–4 moderate, 5–6 major, 7–8 severe, 9–10 extreme. The horizon is 30 years. — [Redfin Methodology](https://www.redfin.com/guides/climate-change-housing-impact/methodology); [First Street help](https://help.firststreet.org/hc/en-us/articles/360047585694-How-is-my-Flood-Factor-calculated)

**Risk-communication research**
- Natural frequencies (e.g., "74 out of 100") are easier to understand than conditional probabilities and aid Bayesian reasoning. Icon arrays improve accuracy for both low- and high-numeracy people and help people take denominators into account. Fact boxes combined with icon arrays are recommended. — [Bodemer & Gaissmaier, Risk Communication in Health](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/18c766fd-ed55-4bb0-8405-27b54a15bf08/download); [Garcia-Retamero, Galesic & Gigerenzer 2010](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2099684_9/component/file_3565343/content); [Galesic et al., icon arrays & low numeracy](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2099767_4/component/file_2562291/content)
- A NICE/NCBI evidence review notes that up to 13 studies found icon arrays gave higher numerical accuracy than bar charts, with mixed effects on perceived risk compared with numeric text. — [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK572436/)
- **Verbal probability terms ("likely", "unlikely") vary widely between people.** For example, "unlikely" was read as anywhere from 5% to 50%. Pairing a verbal term with a numeric range (e.g., "very unlikely (0–10%)") reduces this vagueness. — [Budescu et al. (IPCC)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275118595_The_Interpretation_of_IPCC_Probabilistic_Statements_Around_the_World); [PLOS ONE 2019](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0213522)
- **Uncertainty information helps rather than confuses.** Weather forecasts that included uncertainty led to better decisions and greater trust than single-value forecasts. Trust was preserved when forecasts erred, because acknowledged uncertainty makes a miss seem "less wrong". — [Joslyn & LeClerc 2012, J. Exp. Psych: Applied](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/xap-18-1-126.pdf); [Joslyn & LeClerc 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0963721413481473); [AMS WCAS 2025](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wcas/17/2/WCAS-D-24-0119.1.xml)
- **Traffic-light labels:** a network meta-analysis found Nutri-Score and Multiple Traffic Light labels the most effective food labels, ahead of warning labels, Health Star Rating and Reference Intakes. — [PMC8491916](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8491916/). Other studies report consumers struggling with what the colours mean and limited effect on choice without other interventions. — [Ecuador qualitative study (Cambridge)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/public-health-nutrition/article/qualitative-study-of-consumer-perceptions-and-use-of-traffic-light-food-labelling-in-ecuador/73D51ECDC1F9C1B6E2147C68261F1019); [ResearchGate systematic review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362464048_Role_of_Traffic_Light_Labeling_on_Point_of_Purchase_Behaviors_A_Systematic_Review)
- **Star ratings:** online star ratings follow a J-shaped, positively skewed distribution driven by purchase and under-reporting bias. A 3-star mean often hides polarised opinions, so information is lost. — [Hu, Pavlou & Zhang (CACM)](https://cacm.acm.org/research/overcoming-the-j-shaped-distribution-of-product-reviews/); [Schoenmueller, Netzer & Stahl 2020](https://www.columbia.edu/~on2110/Papers/Schoenmueller_Netzer_Stahl_2020.pdf); [OECD 2019](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2019/09/understanding-online-consumer-ratings-and-reviews_0251efa5/eb018587-en.pdf)
- **Evidence that showing risk scores changes behaviour:** in a 2020 Redfin experiment with 17.5 million users, people viewing severely or extremely flood-risky homes who saw risk scores made offers on homes with 50% less risk than those who did not. — [HousingWire, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/redfin-climate-risk-transparency/)

### Inferences
**Recommended primary display: a 5-band categorical grade with plain labels and published numeric thresholds.** For example: "Low / Moderate / Elevated / High / Very high delivery risk", optionally mapped to a 1–10 index for sorting.
- Avoid a 300–900 style number unless backtesting supports that resolution, because it invites false-precision comparisons ("why is mine 712 and theirs 718?").
- Avoid AAA–D, which is both regulated-looking and confusing with modifiers.
- Avoid 5 stars, which signals reviews and suffers positivity bias.

**Pair each band with a reference-class natural frequency.** For example: "Of 100 comparable NCR projects with this profile since 2017, about 38 were handed over more than 12 months after the RERA-declared date." Show it with a 10×10 icon array. This is both:
- *more understandable* (Gigerenzer, Galesic), and
- *more legally defensible*: it is a statistical fact about a reference class, not an accusation about this developer.

**Always show numbers with verbal labels** (e.g., "Elevated (25–40% historical delay rate)"), never verbal alone (Budescu).

**Show uncertainty explicitly**, using a range or a confidence badge (High / Medium / Low confidence). The Joslyn findings suggest this increases trust and protects credibility when an individual project defies its band.

**Traffic-light colours can be used as a secondary cue only, never alone.** Include labels for colour-blind and low-literacy users, and avoid red for "insufficient data".

### Gaps
- I found no published A/B evidence specific to homebuyers in India on score formats, or on Hindi vs English labels.
- I found no peer-reviewed comparison of letter grades vs 1–10 vs 0–100 for financial or property risk comprehension.
- Official CIBIL factor weights and CIBIL UX research were unavailable (cibil.com returned 403).

## B2. Examples from property platforms (First Street on Zillow/Redfin/Realtor.com, Walk Score, Zestimate, iCIRT, MahaRERA, Bayut/Property Finder): what worked and what caused backlash

### Takeaway
The clearest backlash case is Zillow's removal of First Street climate risk scores in November 2025. Agents and a large MLS (CRMLS) said aggressive predictive probabilities contradicted decades of observed history and hurt sales, and sellers sued over a lost price. Redfin kept the scores, citing peer review and behavioural evidence, and offered sellers a removal request route. The lessons for a builder-risk score:
- Predictions that conflict with visible history destroy credibility.
- Stale predictions are worse than none.
- The rated party (here, the developer) will organise to push back, so a calibration and dispute story must exist before launch.

### Cited Findings
**Zillow and First Street climate scores**
- Zillow added First Street climate risk data (flood, wildfire, wind, heat, air quality) to for-sale listings in fall 2024 and removed it from listings nationwide in mid-November 2025. It now links out to First Street. — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-removes-climate-risk-data/); [RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2025/12/02/zillow-removes-climate-risk-scores/); [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2025/12/01/zillow-drops-climate-risk-scores-after-industry-pushback/)
- CRMLS cited neighbourhoods shown with a 50% chance of flooding this year and 99% within five years despite no flooding in 40–50 years. Its CEO Art Carter said "most of these predictions have been in place for almost five years, with no updates in the stated probabilities even though… these future predictions ended up being very wrong." — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-removes-climate-risk-data/)
- CRMLS's stated aim was to ensure homebuyers "are not being presented with information that could be misleading or unfair". It also asked Realtor.com, Redfin and Homes.com to remove predictive flood numbers. — [RealEstateNews](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/12/01/why-did-zillow-remove-climate-data-from-its-home-listings)
- Agents complained the scores discouraged buyers. A New York couple sued Zillow, claiming an "extreme" flood designation cost them $100,000 on their sale. — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-removes-climate-risk-data/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/01/zillow-drops-climate-risk-scores-after-agents-complained-of-lost-sales)

**Redfin's response**
- Redfin kept the data. Chief economist Daryl Fairweather said: "We believe the default should be transparency. Ignorance isn't bliss when it comes to your biggest asset." She cited the 2020 experiment and First Street's peer-reviewed methodology, and noted that sellers may request removal of information they believe is inaccurate. — [HousingWire, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/redfin-climate-risk-transparency/)

**First Street update cadence**
- First Street applies improvements through quarterly data updates and annual model updates, and updated its scoring methodology in May 2022. — [First Street help (via search summary)](https://help.firststreet.org/hc/en-us/articles/360053574994-Risk-Factor-improvements-and-updates) (page returned 403 on direct fetch)

**Zestimate**
- The Zestimate's reported median error is about 1.94% for on-market and 7.06% for off-market homes. Reportedly 82/100 homes are within 5%, 95/100 within 10% and 99/100 within 20%. — [The Close](https://theclose.com/how-accurate-is-zillow-zestimate/); [Houwzer](https://houwzer.com/blog/the-zillow-zestimate-can-it-be-trusted/) (secondary; zillow.com returned 403). Zillow publicly discloses error statistics and shows an estimate range, a model of honesty about error.

**iCIRT**
- iCIRT is run by Equifax, not the NSW Government. It rates 0–5 stars across six Cs (Capability, Conduct, Character, Capacity, Capital, Counterparties). Only 3+ stars are listed, ratings are valid for 12 months, and each assessment issues a QR code. — [NSW Building Commission](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/building-commission/icirt); [iCIRT FAQs](https://icirt.com/business/faqs-business)
- **Problems:** fake claims of ratings, low performers opting out, and paywalled detail. — [Flat Chat, Nov 2023](https://www.flatchat.com.au/developers-fake-icrt-ratings/). An Equifax consumer guide frames iCIRT as a tool to "vet a builder". — [Equifax AU](https://www.equifax.com.au/personal/beyond-brochure-how-vet-builder-using-icirt-rating)

**MahaRERA**
- MahaRERA's matrix (2023) was an automated disclosure of four snapshots. It was halted in Oct 2024 for lack of statutory power. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/understanding-mahareras-new-grading-matrix-for-real-estate.html); [PropNewsTime](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTE0NTU=/maharera-halts-real-estate-project-grading-initiative-due-to-regulatory-constraints)

**Bayut and Property Finder**
- Bayut's TruCheck™ badge needs document verification (title deed, owner ID) and an agent site visit photographed in-app, reviewed by Bayut QA. Users can click the badge to see how recently the listing was validated. — [MyBayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/expert-advice-trucheck-bayut/)
- Property Finder's SuperAgent badge reflects broker response and transaction records. — [Oliva comparison 2026](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/property-finder-vs-bayut-platform-comparison-2026) (secondary)

### Inferences
**Lessons for an NCR builder-risk score:**
- **Calibrate against observed history and show it.** Display "track record so far" (actual delays of past projects with dates) next to any forward-looking estimate. CRMLS's attack worked because predictions contradicted visible history.
- **Never let predictions go stale.** Show "last recalculated on" dates and refresh when new QPRs or orders arrive.
- **Expect organised pushback.** In NCR the counterparts to CRMLS and agents are developers, broker networks and possibly CREDAI chapters. Launch with a published methodology, backtest results and a dispute route.
- **Verification-recency badges (Bayut) are a good pattern** for data freshness: "RERA data last synced 3 days ago".
- **Prefer a model where every in-scope project is scored** (no opt-out). Hidden low scores, as with iCIRT, reduce public value and invite misrepresentation.
- **Disclose accuracy publicly, as Zillow does.** For example: "Of projects we rated High risk in 2022, X% were delayed >12 months."

### Gaps
- I found no backlash case in India involving a property portal's builder or project score (Housing.com, 99acres, MagicBricks, Square Yards). Whether any portal tried and withdrew developer ratings was not established.
- The Realtor.com/First Street current status (post-CRMLS request) was not confirmed. As of early Dec 2025, Redfin and Realtor.com still displayed the data.

## B3. Explainability, "what would improve this score", missing data and low confidence, and avoiding false precision

### Takeaway
Use the adverse-action norms from US credit law as a design benchmark:
- Give up to four specific, accurate principal reasons (generic checkboxes are not acceptable even for complex models).
- Add counterfactual "what would change this" explanations.
- Name the model and version, so users understand that different models give different numbers (the Credit Karma lesson).
- Treat sparse data as a first-class state ("insufficient data / low confidence"), not as a mid-range score.

### Cited Findings
- **US Regulation B (ECOA):** adverse action notices must state specific principal reasons. Staff commentary says "disclosure of more than four reasons is not likely to be helpful". Where a credit score was used, the notice must disclose the score, its range, key factors, date and source. — [Consumer Compliance Outlook (Fed)](https://www.consumercomplianceoutlook.org/2021/fourth-issue/advanced-topics-in-adverse-action-notices-under-the-equal-credit-opportunity-act/); [Vergent LMS](https://www.vergentlms.com/glossary/adverse-action-notice/)
- **CFPB Circular 2022-03:** creditors using complex algorithms must still give specific and accurate reasons. — [CFPB Circular 2022-03](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/circular-2022-03-adverse-action-notification-requirements-in-connection-with-credit-decisions-based-on-complex-algorithms/); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/06/14/2022-12729/consumer-financial-protection-circular-2022-03-adverse-action-notification-requirements-in)
- **CFPB 2023 follow-up guidance:** generic sample-form reasons are insufficient if they are not the actual reasons. — [Skadden, Jan 2024](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2024/01/cfpb-applies-adverse-action-notification-requirement); [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2023/09/20/cfpb-revisits-adverse-action-notice-requirements-when-using-artificial-intelligence-or-complex-credit-models/)
- **Counterfactual explanations:** Wachter, Mittelstadt & Russell (2017) describe "the smallest change to the world" that would change the outcome. This explains a decision without opening the black box, and it is model-agnostic and cheap to compute. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3063289)
- **RBI FREE-AI** (13 Aug 2025) embeds explainability as a principle, with documentation and interpretability standards "so that every automated decision can be understood and justified". It sets out 7 sutras and 26 recommendations, including consumer disclosure. — [RBI FREE-AI report](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF); [KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/09/rbis-free-ai-committee-report-in-the-financial-sector.html)
- **Credit Karma** explains that different scoring models "weigh [factors] differently" and that lenders use product-specific scores, which causes user confusion. — [Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/credit/i/is-credit-karma-accurate)
- **SEBI** keeps rating entities with insufficient cooperation visible under an "Issuer Not Cooperating" label rather than dropping them. — [SEBI FAQs, Q19](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
- **iCIRT** does not publish sub-3-star results, which critics say lets weaker firms remain unscrutinised. — [iCIRT FAQs](https://icirt.com/business/faqs-business); [Flat Chat](https://www.flatchat.com.au/developers-fake-icrt-ratings/)
- **Joslyn:** uncertainty information preserves trust when forecasts err. — [Joslyn & LeClerc 2012](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/xap-18-1-126.pdf)

### Inferences
**Reason codes.** Show the top 3–4 drivers, each tied to a clickable source document. Example:
- "QPR shows 41% construction vs 78% time elapsed (UP-RERA QPR, Jun 2026)"
- "6 RERA complaints decided against developer in last 24 months"
- "2 group projects >24 months past RERA date"
- "Escrow/withdrawal irregularity flagged in RERA order no. …"

**"What would improve this score" for developers.** Phrase it as public-record facts that would change the band, for example: "Filing QPRs on time for 2 consecutive quarters; obtaining OC for Tower B." This turns the score into an incentive tool and reduces adversarial disputes.

**Buyer-side counterfactuals ("what would make this safer for you").** Examples:
- "Projects at this stage with OC for earlier phases had half the delay rate."
- "Consider construction-linked payment plans."

Frame these as information, not advice.

**Confidence states.** Use three explicit states:
1. Scored, high confidence.
2. Scored, low confidence (wide range shown, fewer data points).
3. Not scored: insufficient data.

New developers with no track record should get state 3, labelled "new developer, no completed RERA projects". A middling score would mislead in either direction.

**Avoid false precision.**
- Round displayed probabilities to 5 or 10 points ("about 30–40%").
- Display bands, not decimals.
- Keep the fine-grained index for internal ranking only.
- Never claim individual-project certainty.

**Model labelling.** Show "Delivery Risk Model v2.3 (updated Oct 2026)" on every score, so changes are traceable (see B4).

### Gaps
- No Indian legal requirement for reason codes applies to a non-credit consumer score. This is a voluntary benchmark.
- I found no empirical study on counterfactual explanations in property or developer risk contexts.

## B4. Score governance: versioning, recalculation cadence, change logs, alerts and model monitoring

### Takeaway
The benchmarks are consistent:
- SEBI CRAs: continuous monitoring, periodic reviews and public disclosure of every rating change.
- iCIRT: annual reassessment plus event-driven monitoring and annual model checks.
- First Street: quarterly data and annual model updates.
- Bank model-risk guidance (US SR 11-7, replaced in April 2026 by SR 26-2) and RBI FREE-AI (2025): change control, independent validation, effective challenge, audits.

The Zillow/CRMLS episode shows the cost of predictions left unchanged for years.

### Cited Findings
- **SEBI CRAs:** must "continuously monitor" ratings and carry out "periodic reviews of all published ratings". Rating changes must be disclosed through press releases. A downgrade means default risk is higher "than what was earlier predicted". — [SEBI FAQs, Q11, Q14, Q16](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)
- **iCIRT:** ratings are valid for one year and formally reassessed annually, with continuous monitoring between assessments that can trigger changes on material events. Equifax "perform[s] annual checks of our models, methodologies and performance". Non-renewal changes status to "withdrawn". — [iCIRT FAQs](https://icirt.com/business/faqs-business); [Canberra Times / search summary](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9361219/what-is-an-icirt-rating-and-which-sydney-developers-have-it/)
- **First Street:** quarterly data updates and annual model updates. — [First Street help (search summary)](https://help.firststreet.org/hc/en-us/articles/360053574994-Risk-Factor-improvements-and-updates)
- **CRMLS criticism:** the main complaint was that predictions had "been in place for almost five years, with no updates in the stated probabilities". — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-removes-climate-risk-data/)
- **RBI FREE-AI (Aug 2025):** proposes board-approved AI policies, formal model approval and change-control, independent validation, periodic audits, vendor safeguards and a standing oversight committee. — [KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/09/rbis-free-ai-committee-report-in-the-financial-sector.html); [RBI report](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF)
- **SR 26-2:** on 17 Apr 2026 the OCC, Fed and FDIC issued revised interagency model risk guidance (SR 26-2, OCC Bulletin 2026-13, FIL-15-2026) rescinding SR 11-7. The core principles of effective challenge and independent validation carried forward. Generative and agentic AI are reportedly outside its scope. — [RiskTemplate, May 2026](https://risktemplate.com/blog/2026-05-02-occ-fed-fdic-revised-model-risk-management-guidance-2026/); [Elevate Consult](https://elevateconsult.com/insights/sr-11-7-rescinded-model-risk-guidance-ai-gap/) (secondary; not verified against federalreserve.gov)
- **Bayut:** shows how recently a listing was validated on its trust badge. — [MyBayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/expert-advice-trucheck-bayut/)
- **Credit monitoring:** SEBI says investors learn of rating changes via CRA press releases on their websites. — [SEBI FAQs, Q16](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)

### Inferences
**Recommended governance spec (voluntary, modelled on SEBI CRA, iCIRT, FREE-AI and SR 11-7/26-2):**
1. **Versioning:** semantic versions for methodology (v2.3) and data snapshot (2026-10-15). Every displayed score carries both. Keep an archive of all historical scores per project.
2. **Two kinds of change, logged separately:**
   - *Event-driven recalculation* within 24–72 hours of a new RERA QPR, RERA or NCLT order, OC grant or new complaint outcome, with the log saying "what changed and why".
   - *Methodology changes* at most quarterly, with a public changelog, a before/after distribution of scores, and a notice period to developers whose band moves.
3. **Alerts:** notify users who saved, enquired about or booked a project when its band changes or a material event occurs (e.g., "RERA extension granted", "new insolvency petition admitted"). Send the developer the same change notice at the same time, CRA press-release style. Downgrades into the bottom band follow the pre-publication reply window from A6.
4. **Watch states:** add a "Under review / On watch" label when a material event is pending, borrowing the CRA outlook and watch practice. Avoid sudden jumps from top to bottom like IL&FS's AAA-to-D, which signal model failure.
5. **Monitoring and validation:**
   - Quarterly backtests: realised delay rate per band vs predicted, published as a calibration table.
   - Drift checks on input data, such as RERA portal format changes.
   - Independent review (an external statistician or advisory panel) before each methodology version.
   - A "challenge log" from disputes feeding model improvements.
6. **Staleness guardrails:** a score older than X days without a data refresh is shown as "stale" or greyed. Bands are never left unchanged for years when the underlying data has moved (the CRMLS lesson).
7. **Governance body:** a small methodology committee with documented minutes, and the founder recused where conflicts exist. This mirrors FREE-AI's board-approved policy and oversight committee and helps rebut "mala fide" claims (*Jindal Power* standard).

### Gaps
- I found no India-specific regulatory requirement for governance of non-financial consumer scores. All of the above is voluntary best practice.
- I could not verify SR 26-2 details from primary Federal Reserve sources.
- I found no empirical evidence on how buyers react to score-change alerts (alert fatigue, panic cancellations). This should be tested in product.
