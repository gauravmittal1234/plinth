# Trust, not traffic, will win NCR's flat buyers

There is a real business case for an AI-first flat platform in Delhi NCR. It is a case for a trust-and-transaction layer, not for another listings portal with a chatbot. NCR sells about **50,000–60,000 new homes worth ₹1.3–1.6 lakh crore a year**, plus an estimated 40,000–50,000 resale flats. By a rough estimate, ₹2,500–7,000 crore a year goes to the marketing, channel-partner and brokerage work of closing those deals. That is several times the **₹488 crore** 99acres earned across all of India in FY26 ([Knight Frank](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf); [ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/research/equity/trending-news/info-edge-revenue-up-17-to-805cr-fy26-net-profit-jumps-54-to-1-106cr-led-by-recruitment)). The incumbent portals offer near-identical search, yet score **1.1–1.2 on Trustpilot** and sell each enquiry to **4–15 brokers**. They leave buyers to discover alone that the all-in cost runs **20–35% above the quoted price**. They also leave buyers exposed to NCR-specific legal traps: tens of thousands of occupied but unregistered flats in Noida and Greater Noida, GPA title chains, a DDA freehold-conversion freeze and a freeze on new Gurugram builder-floor approvals ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/99acres.com); [ClosingFox](https://closingfox.com/for-business-owners/99acres-vs-magicbricks-vs-housing/); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/the-fineprint/noida-greater-noida-home-buyers-struggle-for-registry-big-players-remain-shy/2406593/)). AI is now cheap: about ₹2–12 to stage a photo, ₹4–6 a minute for a Hindi voice agent and ₹0.5–1 a page to read a deed. That means the AI interface is not the moat, because Aurum (now owner of Housing.com) and 99acres can copy it. The moat is a verified, society-level record of availability, legal status and real closing prices, which only builds up by handling transactions. The platform should earn on closings and home loans, start in two or three Gurugram corridors in the ₹1.5–5 crore band alongside Noida/Greater Noida West resale, and leave Delhi's title-heavy resale until later. It launches into real headwinds, because NCR primary sales fell 11% in January–September 2026, the RBI raised rates on 7 October, paid acquisition costs ₹50,000–6 lakh per booking, and a platform that facilitates deals is likely to be a RERA agent, a PMLA reporting entity and a DPDP data fiduciary from day one.

## NCR sells ₹1.5 lakh crore of new homes a year, but its mid-market buyers are drifting to resale

### Primary volume is flat while value swings on luxury

NCR's primary (developer) market sold **57,220 units in 2025, down 8% from 61,900 in 2024**, by ANAROCK's count ([Telangana Today/ANAROCK](https://telanganatoday.com/housing-sales-volume-down-14-pc-in-2025-in-7-cities-anarock)). Knight Frank's count is **52,452** ([Knight Frank](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf)). Value grew far faster than units. PropEquity puts primary sales at **₹1.53 lakh crore in 2024 and about ₹1.63 lakh crore in 2025**, with Gurugram about 60% of value ([BW Businessworld/PropEquity](https://www.businessworld.in/article/gurugram-tops-india-residential-market-with-rs-1-38-lakh-cr-launches-in-cy-2025-627892)).

2026 has turned down. NAR–CRE Matrix shows H1 2026 value **down 24% to ₹68,217 crore**, which let Mumbai overtake NCR as India's largest housing market by value ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/real-estate/story/mmr-overtakes-ncr-as-indias-biggest-housing-market-mumbai-region-takes-26-share-558022-2026-09-26)). Knight Frank has January–September units **down 11% to 35,574** and calls NCR the "standout underperformer" among India's eight largest housing markets ([BW Businessworld/Knight Frank](https://www.businessworld.in/article/ncr-housing-sales-slip-11-till-september-2026-gurugram-leads-decline-knight-frank-626888)). The trackers disagree on direction, though. ANAROCK's three quarterly counts of 15,200, 13,365 and 13,750 units add up to about 42,300 for the nine months, roughly flat or slightly up ([ANAROCK Q3 2026](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/Q3_2026_Pan_India_Residential_Market_Viewpoints_9cf1a7e616.pdf)). Any sizing deck should therefore use a band of **50,000–60,000 primary units and ₹1.3–1.6 lakh crore a year**, not a single number.

The mix explains why value and volume move differently. More than **55% of 2025 NCR launches were priced above ₹2.5 crore** ([MediaBrief/ANAROCK](https://mediabrief.com/housing-sales-fall-14-year-on-year-in-2025-while-value-rises-6-across-top-7-cities-anarock/)). In H1 2026 the ₹2–5 crore band took **60% of launches but only 43% of sales** ([Knight Frank](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf)). Gurugram's share of NCR units fell from 51% to 42% between H1 2025 and H1 2026, as sales moved to cheaper end-user markets: Greater Noida (26%), Ghaziabad (17%), Noida (9%) and Faridabad (4%). Delhi NCT was just **2%** of primary sales.

### Supply moved upmarket; buyers did not

The stock that is not selling is the cheap stock. Knight Frank counts about **104,000 unsold NCR units**. At current sales rates the ₹0–50 lakh band holds **40 quarters** of inventory, against **1.5 quarters** for ₹5–10 crore homes ([Knight Frank](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf)). ANAROCK's lower count of 86,200 units still equals about **18 months of sales** ([ANAROCK Q3 2026](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/Q3_2026_Pan_India_Residential_Market_Viewpoints_9cf1a7e616.pdf)).

Buyers' budgets sit well below what developers launch. ANAROCK's survey of about 8,250 buyers across 14 cities found the **₹90 lakh–1.5 crore band** most favoured, at 36% ([ANAROCK H1 2025 survey](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/Homebuyer_Sentiment_Survey_H1_2025_770cb18d6a.pdf)). Colliers found 46% preferring ₹50 lakh–1.5 crore ([The Wire/Colliers 2026](https://m.thewire.in/article/ptiprnews/colliers-homebuying-sentiment-survey-2026-around-50-of-prospective-homebuyers-are-likely-to-purchase-a-house-within-2-years)). The average NCR primary ticket in H1 2026, by contrast, was **₹2.77 crore** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/real-estate/story/mmr-overtakes-ncr-as-indias-biggest-housing-market-mumbai-region-takes-26-share-558022-2026-09-26)). Knight Frank says well-located product "increasingly sits in the INR 20 mn and above range, pricing out a significant share of end-users."

This mismatch is the most important structural fact for a new platform. Mid-market end-users are being pushed toward resale, the periphery or renting. In ANAROCK's survey, **71% of buyers were delaying** a purchase and **34% were switching to renting** because of prices.

### Resale is the larger flow for a mid-market platform, but nobody measures it in NCR

Across seven major cities, resale rose to **43% of registered residential transactions in FY2025**, from 38% in FY2019. In Noida and Greater Noida it rose from 29% to **40% of about 30,000 registrations**, which is roughly 12,000 resale deals a year ([Square Yards](https://static.squareyards.com/PrimaryVsSecondary-UnpackingDemandTrendsinIndia'sResidentialMarket-SquareYards.pdf); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/indias-secondary-real-estate-market-grows-to-43-share-by-fy25-square-yards/2605611/)). Square Yards attributes the shift to "past concerns over project delays", and notes that the traditional resale discount "has reduced significantly". Its dataset excludes Delhi and Gurugram.

Applying the 43% share to NCR's primary volume gives an **estimated 40,000–50,000 resale flat and builder-floor deals a year**. At an assumed ₹1–1.5 crore average ticket, that is roughly ₹40,000–75,000 crore of value. Both figures are working assumptions, not sourced numbers.

Delhi NCT is almost entirely a resale market. Its stock is DDA flats, cooperative-society flats, builder floors and homes in **1,797 unauthorised colonies** ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/national-interest/dda-turned-into-delhi-destruction-authority-why-we-applaud-the-market-taking-revenge/1888069/)). On 7 April 2026, 1,511 of the 1,731 colonies covered by the PM-UDAY scheme, home to about 45 lakh residents, were cleared for "as-is where-is" regularisation ([PIB Tracker](https://pibtracker.com/article/2026-04-07/delhi-colony-regularisation-2249686)). No public source splits Delhi registrations by these housing types.

Official counts are also noisy. An income-tax survey found about **25,000 Gurugram registrations filed under a single fictitious PAN** ([PropNewsTime](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MzgxNTA=/gurugram-property-registrations-under-scrutiny-after-25-000-deals-use-same-fictitious-pan)).

Rental returns are thin everywhere, and thinnest in the hottest corridors. After the RBI raised the repo rate to 5.50% on 7 October 2026, SBI home loans start at **7.50%** ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/10/09/rbi-hikes-repo-rate-to-5-50-percent-october-2026/); [SBI](https://sbi.bank.in/web/interest-rates/interest-rates/loan-schemes-interest-rates/home-loans-interest-rates-current)). At that rate, rent on a mortgaged NCR flat does not cover the loan cost. Buying NCR property as an investment is therefore a bet on capital appreciation. That fits Knight Frank's description of NCR as historically showing "a higher degree of investor and speculative participation" than other cities. No credible source sizes NCR's rental market.

### Stalled projects and a rate hike set the mood for 2027

NCR is the centre of India's stalled-housing problem. Using data as of May 2020, ANAROCK counted **2,40,610 stalled or delayed NCR units worth ₹1.81 lakh crore**, 70% of them in Noida and Greater Noida ([LatestLY/PTI](https://www.latestly.com/agency-news/latest-news-homebuyers-in-noida-greater-noida-worst-hit-1-65-lakh-units-of-rs-1-18-lakh-cr-stalled-3873248.html)). By March 2024, Gautam Buddh Nagar had about **2.4 lakh stressed units**. Half were undelivered, and half were occupied but not registered. Developers owed the local authorities ₹44,700 crore ([Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/infrastructure/noida-greater-noida-builders-clear-dues-to-revive-stalled-housing-projects-under-up-governments-relief-package)). No 2025–26 count exists.

The cycle now looks late. Knight Frank says "the monetary tailwind that sustained demand through 2025 has largely run its course". It also names "structural anxiety around AI-driven shifts in white-collar employment" as a source of buyer caution, which matters in Gurugram and Noida, where demand rests on IT and global capability centre (GCC) jobs ([Knight Frank](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3116/documents/en/india-real-estate-office-and-residential-market-h1-2026-12927.pdf)). The October rate hike came after ANAROCK wrote its "stable borrowing costs" outlook. It points to continued pressure on volumes, more developer incentives, and a further shift toward ready and resale homes that buyers can inspect before paying.

## Incumbents have commoditised search and squandered trust

### Indian portals reached feature parity and fail on the same things

The four big horizontal portals ship almost the same toolkit: filters, maps, project pages, price trends, resident reviews, loan referral, rent agreements and interiors. They differ in business model and finances, not in the core search experience.

The gap between app ratings of 4.4–4.8 and Trustpilot scores of 1.1–1.2 is telling. App-store prompts reach satisfied searchers, while Trustpilot captures sellers and paying users.

Their complaints follow the same chain across all four portals. It starts with stale "ghost listings" and brokers posing as owners. It continues with floods of sales calls; one 99acres owner wrote, "In 24 hours, I have got calls from 10 different sales reps". Free listings are throttled to push paid upgrades, relationship managers vanish once a plan is paid for, and refunds are refused ([Trustpilot 99acres](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/99acres.com); [Trustpilot MagicBricks](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/magicbricks.com); [Trustpilot Housing.com](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/housing.com)).

Brokers report the mirror image. Each enquiry is sold to **4–15 brokers at once**, conversion runs at 1–4%, "the broker who calls in two minutes usually wins it", and "no portal publishes its lead-quality numbers" ([ClosingFox](https://closingfox.com/for-business-owners/99acres-vs-magicbricks-vs-housing/)). 99acres even sells brokers access to homeowners who want to sell, through a BOSS plan that unlocks up to 50 owner contacts ([Privyr](https://www.privyr.com/blog/are-99acres-lead-generation-packages-worth-the-investment/)). That explains why simply listing as an owner brings in broker calls.

The root cause is a business model that earns more by selling one enquiry many times. Under that model the buyer's phone number is the product and spam is the inevitable result. A "verified" badge alone cannot fix it.

No incumbent dominates traffic. comScore's May 2026 monthly unique visitors were **MagicBricks 8.0M, 99acres 7.6M, NoBroker 6.1M and Housing.com 5.6M** ([MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/magicbricks-crosses-8-million-monthly-unique-visitors/)). A focused entrant can therefore take share one metro at a time.

The incumbents' AI is mostly back-office or marketing language. Aurum pitches an "AI-native operating system" without naming shipped features ([Aurum](https://www.aurumproptech.in/pulse/media/aurum-proptech-acquires-housing-com-ai-real-estate-operating-system)). Housing.com launched property search inside ChatGPT in June 2026 ([Realty N More](https://realtynmore.com/housing-com-launches-property-search-on-chatgpt/)). This research found no evidence of Hindi voice search, virtual staging or AI-written listings at any Indian portal, though it could not confirm that none exist.

### Money flows to transactions and loans, not listings

Indian results show where profit is made. Square Yards earns on the transaction and the mortgage, and is the only scaled player with steady positive EBITDA. 99acres reached breakeven only after years of losses. Housing.com, despite heavy traffic, sold for about $47.5M. NoBroker concluded that its premium-subscription model "proved inadequate" and moved into loans, movers, interiors and society management ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/nobrokers-future-is-beyond-home-listings-but-will-profits-follow/)).

Aurum says it cut about **₹120 crore of Housing.com costs** in its first week of ownership "without meaningful revenue decline" ([Investing.com](https://ph.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/aurum-proptech-q2-fy27-slides-profit-streak-continues-as-housingcom-deal-closes-93CH-2632633)). A new entrant should expect leaner, AI-branded incumbents competing on price in 2027.

### Global leaders built the trust infrastructure India lacks

The Gulf portals show what verification looks like at scale. Bayut in the UAE combines three trust badges ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bayut.bayutapp&hl=en_IN)). Its TruCheck badge marks listings as "trusted, authentic and available", and Bayut claims those listings earn **20x the impressions and 5x the leads** ([MWM/Bayut listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/bayut-uae-property-search/923263211)). TruEstimate gives AI valuations, and TruBroker flags responsive agents. Bayut also shows official Dubai transaction data under a 2022 MoU with the Dubai Land Department ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayut)). Property Finder runs a similar "Verified True listings" scheme and a SuperAgent programme ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ae.propertyfinder.propertyfinder&hl=en_IN)).

Rightmove offers the best public proof that conversational AI pays. "Ask Rightmove" produced **about 40% more time on site and roughly double the propensity to send leads**, and its AI agent-valuation tool drove about 50% more valuation leads. The business runs at a 69% operating margin with 96% agency retention, and LLM referrals bring it under 0.5% of traffic ([Rightmove H1 2026](https://plc.rightmove.co.uk/content/uploads/2026/07/2026.07.31-Rightmove-final-RNS-H12026-unsigned.pdf)). Only agents may list on it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rightmove)).

realestate.com.au shows an estimated value and sales history for every property in Australia and offers conditional loan approval inside the app ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=au.com.realestate.app&hl=en_IN)). Zillow pairs a BuyAbility score with verified pre-approval. Its Zestimate misses by about **2% on listed homes and 7% on unlisted ones**. Zillow also removed a Climate Risk Score in November 2025 after agents complained, a reminder that transparency provokes the supply side ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zillow)).

Together these platforms define what Indian portals lack. None of them offers listings verified as available, with a visible badge that buyers can sort by. None shows government transaction data to consumers or publishes error rates for its valuation model. None ranks agents by quality rather than by how much they spend, builds financing into search, measures its conversational AI, or separates owner listings clearly from broker listings.

## Buyers pay 20–35% above the sticker and carry risks nobody prices

### Two tracks, eleven stages and a dozen counterparties

An NCR purchase runs through about eleven stages: trigger and budget, discovery, shortlisting, site visits, due diligence, negotiation, financing, token and agreement, registration and TDS, possession and mutation, and ownership. It splits into two tracks. On the **primary track** (new or under-construction flats), the main risks are delivery, opaque add-on pricing and long waits. On the **resale track**, the main risks are title, paperwork and negotiation.

| Stage | Primary (new or under construction) | Resale | What buyers commonly neglect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | Price-led delay; flexi and subvention plans | Price-led delay | Rent vs buy; negative carry; total cost of ownership |
| Discovery and shortlisting | Brand as proxy; super vs carpet area | Stale or duplicate listings; brokers posing as owners | Flooding, AQI, peak-hour commute, society management |
| Due diligence | RERA status, OC/CC, DTCP licence, authority dues | Title chain, society/authority/bank NOCs, GPA chains, registry status | Builder delivery record; authority dues blocking registry |
| Negotiation | Published rates; PLC, parking and floor rise negotiable | 2–15% off asking; circle-rate gap; cash demands | Registered comparable sales |
| Financing | Project must be bank-approved; subvention risk | Bank valuation below deal price; no loans on GPA or no-OC homes | Lender confirmation of any subvention promise |
| Agreement and registration | BBA vs RERA model agreement; 5% GST | Agreement to sell; token forfeiture; 1% TDS, higher for NRI sellers | Correct TDS section and form after April 2026 |
| Possession and after | OC, snagging, IFMS, maintenance | Noida TM or DDA conversion; society transfer; mutation | Resale liquidity; maintenance trajectory |

Time and friction pile up at every stage. A NoBroker-sponsored estimate puts search-to-shortlist at **6–12 months**, including 3–8 weekends of site visits ([Assam Tribune, sponsored](https://assamtribune.com/article/how-long-does-it-really-take-to-find-a-home-in-india-2026-data-1618883)). No neutral Indian data exists on how many homes buyers visit. Bank loan sanction takes "one to two weeks" ([ICICI Bank](https://www.icici.bank.in/personal-banking/loans/home-loan)). A Gurugram primary buyer typically pays a ₹1–5 lakh expression of interest (EOI), then 10–15% at booking, and signs the builder-buyer agreement (BBA) within 30–60 days ([Do Bigha Zamin](https://dobighazam.in/guides/101-buying-high-rise-apartment-gurgaon)). Haryana's registration has been paperless since 1 November 2025. Deeds are auto-checked against Jamabandi land records, and the parties need to attend only one appointment, for biometrics ([Hindustan Era](https://hindustanera.in/explained-haryanas-paperless-land-registration-is-now-live-how-the-e-registration-system-works/)). The process does not end at registration, though. Gurugram still had **7,585 mutation cases pending** in August 2026 ([SKJ Landbase](https://www.skjlandbase.com/gurugram-paperless-property-services-mutation-backlog/)).

Along the way, the buyer deals with portals, brokers, developer sales and CRM teams, banks, lawyers, three RERA authorities, planning and development authorities, sub-registrars, the tax department, the resident welfare association (RWA) and utilities. Most do it through a broker whose incentives point elsewhere.

### Hidden costs and the area illusion

A Gurugram worked example turns a **₹1.5 crore 3BHK into ₹1.879 crore, about 25% more**. The extra comes from preferential location charges (PLC), external and internal development charges (EDC/IDC), the interest-free maintenance security deposit (IFMS), 5% GST, 7% stamp duty, and club and parking charges ([Do Bigha Zamin](https://dobighazam.in/guides/101-buying-high-rise-apartment-gurgaon)). A second example turns ₹1 crore into ₹1.37 crore ([Oak n Stone](https://oaknstoneinc.com/hidden-costs-of-buying-property-in-gurgaon/)).

Two costs barely appear in any calculator. The first is the area illusion. NCR's average loading (super built-up area minus carpet area) reached **41% in Q1 2025, up from 31% in 2019**. A 1,300 sq ft "super area" flat can yield only about 900 sq ft of usable space ([Gulf News/ANAROCK](https://gulfnews.com/your-money/saving-investment/indian-expats-in-uae-buying-property-back-home-why-youre-getting-less-than-you-pay-for-1.500157365)). The second is Noida's resale transfer charge. The Noida Authority levies a Transfer Memorandum (TM) charge as a share of the original allotment price: 2.5% for allotments after September 2021, rising to 50% for pre-1990 allotments, and doubled for GPA transfers. For a 100 sq m flat in Sector 30 it comes to about ₹3.16 lakh ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/real-estate/transfer-memorandum-the-hidden-tax-on-resale-flats-in-noida)).

| Cost item | Primary, under construction | Resale |
|---|---|---|
| Stamp duty + registration | ~6–8% | ~6–8%, on the higher of deal value and circle rate |
| GST | 5% (1% affordable) | Nil on completed units with OC |
| PLC/floor rise, EDC/IDC, parking, club | ~4–10% combined | Mostly not applicable |
| IFMS + advance maintenance | ~1–3% (IFMS ₹100–200/sq ft; maintenance ₹2–6/sq ft/month) | Usually adjusted in deal |
| Brokerage | Usually nil (developer pays channel partner) | ~1–2% + 18% GST |
| Authority transfer | n/a | Noida TM from 2.5% of allotment price; DDA conversion (currently frozen) |
| Loan processing | Up to 2% of loan | Same |
| **Typical total above base price** | **~20–35%**, before delay costs | **~10–15%**, plus any off-book cash demand |

These ranges are synthesised from [Do Bigha Zamin](https://dobighazam.in/guides/101-buying-high-rise-apartment-gurgaon), [Oak n Stone](https://oaknstoneinc.com/hidden-costs-of-buying-property-in-gurgaon/), [RealEstateGurugram](https://realestategurugram.com/maintenance-charges-in-gurgaon-apartments-2026/), [ICICI Bank](https://www.icici.bank.in/personal-banking/loans/home-loan) and [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/real-estate/transfer-memorandum-the-hidden-tax-on-resale-flats-in-noida). Most come from broker blogs, and they conflict on items such as parking (₹1–5 lakh in one, ₹5–15 lakh in another), so treat them as directional. Practitioners still have to advise buyers to demand an "all-inclusive cost sheet" before booking ([Sapient Realty](https://www.sapientrealty.com/hidden-costs-when-buying-a-flat-in-gurgaon-2026-guide/)). That shows how rarely buyers get one.

### The questions buyers cannot answer: future value, liquidity, rent and liveability

Buyers say clearly what they want. **98% demand assurance of timely completion and 93% demand assurance of construction quality** ([ANAROCK H1 2025 survey](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/Homebuyer_Sentiment_Survey_H1_2025_770cb18d6a.pdf)). They have no tools to judge either, so they use brand as a proxy. **54% will pay up to a 20% premium** for an established developer, and 68% would choose an under-construction flat from a reputed developer over a ready flat from a lesser-known one ([Constro Facilitator, citing Housing.com](https://constrofacilitator.com/how-organised-trust-led-developers-will-shape-indias-housing-growth-in-the-next-decade/)). National developers' share of NCR supply rose from 3% in 2022 to over 13% in 2025 ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/amp/story/invest/ncr-homebuyers-are-increasingly-betting-on-trusted-national-real-estate-brands)).

Preferences on readiness are themselves contested. ANAROCK found the ready-to-move vs new-launch preference ratio flipped to 16:29 by H1 2025. Colliers found **more than 60% prefer ready or near-complete homes** ([The Wire/Colliers](https://m.thewire.in/article/ptiprnews/colliers-homebuying-sentiment-survey-2026-around-50-of-prospective-homebuyers-are-likely-to-purchase-a-house-within-2-years)).

**Future value.** Buyers hear broker claims of "8–15% annually" in growth corridors ([Do Bigha Zamin](https://dobighazam.in/guides/101-buying-high-rise-apartment-gurgaon)), but cannot test them. The most rigorous NCR evidence is a hedonic study of Gurugram metro stations ([JCUA 2025](https://www.ijcua.com/ijcua/article/download/544/713)). Homes within 500 m of a station gained **20–25% after the project was announced and about 30% after it opened**. About half the premium arrived before opening, and it vanished beyond 1.5–2 km.

**Liquidity.** Appreciation and resale liquidity can diverge. Dwarka Expressway prices rose 135% from 2021 to H1 2026. Yet the corridor carries about 18 months of overhang, with **95% of its stock still under construction**, so a resale seller there competes with thousands of new units ([Trade Brains/ANAROCK](https://tradebrains.in/money/dwarka-expressway-property-prices-surge-135-in-5-years-check-prices-rents-and-growth-potential-of-this-ncr-corridor-12515457)).

**Liveability.** This is the least systematically assessed dimension. Gurugram has spent **₹500 crore since 2016** and still floods every monsoon, at about 100 hotspots including Golf Course Road and Sohna Road ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/despite-rs-500-cr-spend-gurugram-sinks-every-monsoon)). Winter AQI reached 491 in November 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution_in_Delhi)). Stages of GRAP, Delhi's graded pollution action plan, regularly halt construction, which makes delay a predictable seasonal risk for under-construction projects. In stilt-plus-four builder-floor areas, sewers and water lines built for two or three families per plot now serve about five ([Box n Move](https://boxnmove.com/blog/gurgaon-stilt-4-hc-stay-buyers-tenants)).

No NCR study quantifies resale premiums or discounts for floor, facing, maintenance quality, carpet efficiency, AQI or waterlogging. Those effects will have to be learned from the platform's own data.

### NCR's distinctive legal traps

Primary buyers walk into a long history of developer failure. Jaypee left more than 25,000 buyers stranded, and its former chairman was arrested in November 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaypee_Infratech_homebuyers_fraud)). Sixteen Supertech projects saw no construction for 21 months, even with NBCC appointed to oversee completion ([Urban Acres](https://urbanacres.in/supertech-homebuyers-escalate-protest-as-16-projects-remain-idle/)). Under subvention schemes, builders promised to pay buyers' EMIs until possession, then defaulted, and banks turned to the buyers for payment. The Supreme Court said this "hints towards a possible collusion" between banks and builders ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/supreme-court-blasts-builderbank-nexus-in-home-loan-interest-subvention-scheme-warns-of-cbi-investigation/76613.html)). By July 2025 the CBI had registered **22 FIRs and searched 47 locations** across NCR ([India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/india/ncr-builders-cheating-homebuyers-case-latest-update-cbi-acts-on-supre-court-orders-registers-22-firs-in-loan-scams-raids-47-locations-2025-07-30-1001257)). UP-RERA has received about 60,000 complaints, roughly 40% of India's total, and has recovered ₹1,500–1,600 crore for buyers ([Realty N More](https://realtynmore.com/up-rera-transfer-recovers-1600-crore-homebuyers/)).

Resale buyers face a different set of traps. About **75,000 flats in Noida and Greater Noida** were still unregistered in December 2024, and such flats "cannot be sold or mortgaged" ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/the-fineprint/noida-greater-noida-home-buyers-struggle-for-registry-big-players-remain-shy/2406593/)). More than 8,000 buyers in nine Greater Noida West projects were blocked from registering because their developers owed the authority over ₹400 crore ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/explained-why-8-000-flat-registrations-are-stuck-in-ncr-s-greater-noida-125051400170_1.html)). Under the Supreme Court's *Suraj Lamp* ruling, a GPA, an agreement to sell and a will do not transfer title, so banks refuse to lend against homes held that way ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1565619/)).

In Delhi, DDA put leasehold-to-freehold conversion of residential property on hold in August 2026, with no date for resuming it ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/real-estate/story/dda-puts-leasehold-to-freehold-conversions-on-hold-which-delhi-homes-are-covered-under-the-policy-548432-2026-08-11)). Only about 40,000 PM-UDAY conveyance deeds or authorisation slips had been issued by 31 March 2026, for colonies housing about 45 lakh people ([PIB Tracker](https://pibtracker.com/article/2026-04-07/delhi-colony-regularisation-2249686)). Delhi's circle rates have not changed since 2014, while prime-colony prices run **50–70% above** them, which keeps pressure on buyers to pay part of the price in off-book cash ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/delhi-circle-rate-revision-impact-what-homebuyers-sellers-must-know-in-2025)).

In Gurugram, DTCP froze all fresh stilt-plus-four approvals statewide on 21 July 2026 ([Lawyers in Gurgaon](https://www.lawyersingurgaon.in/stilt-4-floors-stay-impact-on-property-buyers-gurgaon/)). Most single-plot builder floors sit outside RERA, and about 2,000 notices have been issued for illegally enclosed stilt parking ([RealBetter](https://gurgaon.realbetter.com/blog/locality-guide/builder-floor-in-gurgaon-buyers-guide)).

A credible NCR risk view therefore has to combine at least seven layers: the developer's distress history, the project's RERA status and quarterly progress, authority dues that block registry, land tenure, colony status, building type and OC status, and the type of title chain. No public portal joins these together.

### NRI buyers carry the same risks from a distance

NRIs can buy residential property freely but must pay through banking channels. Misuse of power of attorney is "one of the most common ways NRIs lose money or property in India." Tenants deduct 30% TDS on rent paid to NRIs, and property managers charge 8–10% of rent ([Belong](https://getbelong.com/blog/real-estate-investment-for-nri/)). High loading hits NRIs especially, who are "paying more… and getting less space" ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/your-money/saving-investment/indian-expats-in-uae-buying-property-back-home-why-youre-getting-less-than-you-pay-for-1.500157365)). NRIs are also becoming sellers: a July 2026 report headline, the only part that could be read, says **46% of NRI property owners want to sell now** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/46-of-nri-property-owners-want-to-sell-now-26-plan-exits-within-6-months-126072900685_1.html)). No reliable figure exists for NRIs' share of NCR purchases.

## Resale owners and developers fail at opposite ends of the funnel

### Resale sellers drown in noise, then in paperwork

A typical NCR resale is broker-led, and the seller carries most of the coordination. With a financed buyer, getting from signed agreement to registration takes **six to ten weeks** ([BookPropertyVisit](https://bookpropertyvisit.com/blog/real-estate/how-to-sell-your-home-in-gurgaon-fast-and-pay-only-after-it-sells)). In that time the seller manages roughly 13 hand-offs across six to ten parties.

**Pricing.** Sellers price from hearsay and portal asking prices, not registered deeds, and buyers exploit the gap. In one Square Yards example, a Noida flat listed at ₹92 lakh closed at ₹85 lakh, 7.6% below asking, because the buyer brought registered comparables. Square Yards puts negotiation room at **5–15% for motivated sellers and 2–7% for patient ones** ([Square Yards](https://www.squareyards.com/blog/how-to-negotiate-property-price)).

**Brokerage.** Brokers charge about **1% from each side, plus 18% GST**. Dual agency, where one dealer collects from both buyer and seller, is common enough that guides warn about it ([RealBetter](https://gurgaon.realbetter.com/blog/charges/brokerage-charges-in-gurgaon)).

**Paperwork.** Noida society NOCs take 7–15 working days and require all dues cleared ([Bhavishya Nirman](https://bhavishyanirman.com/blog/is-society-noc-required-for-sale-of-flat-in-noida/)). A seller with a home loan has to get the original documents back from the lender. RBI rules since December 2023 require release **within 30 days of repayment or ₹5,000 a day in compensation** ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/release-property-documents-within-30-days-on-repayment-or-settlement-of-loans-else-pay-rs5000-per-day-as-compensation-rbi/71983.html)). Noida's TM charge is paid by the buyer, but it lowers what the buyer will offer.

**Tax.** Sellers who bought before 23 July 2024 must compare two long-term capital-gains options: 12.5% flat, or 20% with indexation. They must also plan reinvestment exemptions under Sections 54 and 54EC, which have deadlines ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-ltcg-tax)). The buyer's 1% TDS is calculated on a base that includes parking and club charges. NRI sellers face withholding on the full consideration unless they obtain a lower-deduction certificate before registration ([TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/tds-on-property-purchase-section-194-ia-form-26qb-india)).

No credible data exists on NCR resale days-on-market or on the share of listings that never sell.

Taken together, the evidence ranks sellers' pains clearly. The biggest is noise: brokers and portal sales teams far outnumber real buyers among the people who call. Pricing confidence comes next. Then comes the coordination from token to registry, then tax (especially for NRIs), and finally safety during visits and payments.

The tools on offer address almost none of this. Portal plans sell visibility. NoBroker sells prepaid assisted plans with partial refunds ([NoBroker](https://www.nobroker.in/seller/plans)). Its NRI services publish no pricing and make no accountability commitments, according to a competitor's review ([66 MG Road](https://66mgroad.com/guides/nobroker-nri-services-review)). "Pay after sale" services do not disclose their fees. No one offers accountable, seller-side representation that combines verified buyers, transaction-based pricing, document orchestration and tax handling.

### Developers pay ₹50,000 to ₹6 lakh to acquire one booking

Developer selling in NCR runs on two tiers.

**Branded large developers** sell through expression-of-interest (EOI) queues in which demand exceeds supply. DLF's The Aureva sold all 172 residences, about ₹1,985 crore, after **406 confirmed EOIs in 48 hours** ([ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/research/equity/trending-news/dlf-sells-out-the-aureva-senior-living-project-in-gurugram-for-1-985-cr)). DLF booked ₹20,143 crore in FY26 ([Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/corporate-news/English/real-estate/DLF-Ltd-Reports-indian-rupee20143-Cr-Sales-Bookings-indian-rupee4408-Cr-Profit-in-FY26/6a7089f110bce1fd203a5844)). For these developers the bottleneck is allocation, KYC, collections and attributing each sale to the right channel partner. Collections, not bookings, drive their cash: in Q3 FY26, DLF's pre-sales were only ₹419 crore while collections hit a record ₹5,100 crore ([ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_dlf_q3fy26.pdf)).

**Everyone else** buys demand, and demand is softening even for some listed names. Signature Global's FY26 bookings fell 20% to ₹8,220 crore "on weak demand" ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/signature-global-reports-5-drop-in-q4-sales-bookings-on-weak-demand-126040900776_1.html)). ANAROCK finds that channel partners (CPs), the brokers who sell new projects for developers, account for **32% of bookings nationally** ([Business Standard/ANAROCK](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/india-s-housing-sweet-spot-anarock-ai-says-it-s-50-lakh-1-cr-homes-125080801155_1.html)). They earn **1–3% of unit value, up to 5% in luxury** ([Leadrat](https://leadrat.com/channel-partners-in-real-estate-a-complete-2026-guide/)). Commission is released against collection milestones, and "clawback on cancellation is standard" ([Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate)).

The digital funnel leaks badly. One CRM vendor's benchmarks put overall lead-to-booking conversion at **1–2.5% without a CRM**. They put average first response to portal leads at 4–6 hours, and claim leads contacted within five minutes convert "8–10x" better ([Realatic](https://realatic.com/blog/real-estate-lead-conversion-india/)). These are vendor claims, not audited data.

Leads are expensive. Meta leads cost ₹300–1,500 per qualified lead in metros, or about ₹8,000 per site visit all-in ([The Mediaverse](https://www.themediaverse.in/digital-marketing/blog/meta-ads-real-estate-leads-india-site-visit-cost)). Google leads cost ₹1,500–2,800 for premium projects and ₹2,000–6,000 for luxury Gurugram ([LeadPro](https://leadproio.com/real-estate-cost-per-lead-india/)). At these rates, paid acquisition costs roughly **₹50,000 per booking in the mid-segment and ₹3–6 lakh in premium and luxury Gurugram**. That is the same order as a CP commission of ₹1.5–4.5 lakh on a ₹1.5 crore unit. It explains why developers keep telecalling teams at ₹15,000–25,000 per person per month ([Qreo](https://dev.to/qreo-digital/how-ai-voice-agents-are-changing-real-estate-lead-follow-up-in-india-2026-3g6n)).

Advertising compliance is a growing burden. UP-RERA fined **91 developers ₹2.66 crore** in January 2025 for ads missing registration numbers or QR codes ([Best Media Info](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-marketing/up-rera-imposes-rs-266-crore-fine-on-91-developers-for-violating-ad-norms-8656562)). HARERA Gurugram opened suo motu proceedings in 2026 over ads missing RERA and DTCP licence numbers ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/harera-cracks-down-on-gurugram-developer-over-unregistered-housing-project/)).

After booking, the work shifts to demand letters, construction updates, RERA filings, possession and snagging, all handled for buyers whose constant worry, given NCR's history, is whether their project has quietly stalled. Developer websites still stop at "enquire" and "schedule a site visit" ([Godrej Properties](https://www.godrejproperties.com/)). Developers' tools are fragmented across CRMs such as Sell.Do, LeadSquared and Salesforce, CP portals, telephony and WhatsApp. Vendors name deduplication, attribution and leakage as the main gaps ([Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/blog/real-estate-crm-lead-management-guide)).

## Regulation dictates the product's shape as much as the market does

### Transaction rules differ by state and changed in 2025–26

Sources: [HomeFirst](https://homefirstindia.com/blog/article/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-in-delhi); [RupayWise](https://rupaywise.com/calculators/stamp-duty/haryana); [News On AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/up-cabinet-approves-1-stamp-duty-exemption-for-women-on-property-worth-up-to-1-crore-rupees); [99acres](https://www.99acres.com/articles/circle-rates-in-delhi.html); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/sudden-hike-in-haryana-circle-rates-is-2nd-since-dec-stamp-duty-as-high-as-145-in-parts-of-gurugram/2709912/); [Square Yards](https://www.squareyards.com/blog/circle-rate-in-ghaziabad-crc). Most of these rates come from secondary sources and must be checked against official calculators before they enter product logic.

**Circle rates.** Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the deal price and the government circle rate. Circle rates therefore have to be stored as versioned data with effective dates. Haryana's August 2025 revision alone raised rates in old-Gurugram residential sectors by about 77%.

**Registration.** The three states are modernising at different speeds. Haryana moved all 22 districts to paperless registration from 1 November 2025 ([Babushahi](https://www.babushahi.com/view-news.php?id=212496&headline=Haryana-to-go-fully-paperless-in-property-registration-from-November-1%3B-online-deeds-mandatory-across-22-districts)), and Gurugram tehsils will not register a property without a municipal no-dues certificate ([Realty+](https://www.rprealtyplus.com/news-views/no-registration-for-projects-with-pending-dues-mcg-78217.html)). In May 2026 Delhi announced a Passport-Seva-style overhaul of its sub-registrar offices to cut "reliance on agents" ([The News Mill](https://thenewsmill.com/2026/05/delhi-to-modernise-sub-registrar-offices-to-reduce-property-registration-delays-and-corruption/)).

**GST.** Unchanged by the September 2025 reform: **5% on under-construction homes, 1% on affordable homes, and nil on completed homes with an OC** ([TaxClue](https://taxclue.in/gst-on-flat-purchase)).

**Income tax.** The **Income-tax Act 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026** and renumbered every property-tax section. Section 194-IA became 393(1), 54 became 82, 54EC became 85, and 56(2)(x) became 92(2)(m) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income-tax_Act,_2025); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/income-tax-act-2025-section-numbers-old-vs-new)). Users, banks and CAs will mix old and new numbers through at least FY2026-27, so product copy should show both.

The substance of the rules carried over. The buyer still deducts 1% TDS when the price is ₹50 lakh or more, rising to 20% if the seller provides no PAN ([TDSGuru](https://tdsguru.in/sections/194IA.html)). The actual price is still accepted for tax purposes as long as the stamp-duty value is no more than 110% of it ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/stamp-duty-replaces-sale-price-sections-50c-43ca-110-percent-limit.html)). Cash receipts of ₹2 lakh or more, and cash advances of ₹20,000 or more for property, remain prohibited ([Coraa](https://coraa.ai/university/cash-transaction-compliance-checker)). The cash rule matters for product design: any platform feature that tolerates a "cash component" is a legal hazard, not a convenience.

RERA's definition of a real-estate agent is broad. MahaRERA used it in 2018 to serve notices on MagicBricks, 99acres, Makaan and Housing.com, which argued that they collected no commissions. The outcome was not found ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/maharera-issues-notices-to-magicbricks-99acres-makaan-and-housingcom/54960.html)), and no NCR regulator order aimed at portals was found either.

The business model sets the regulatory load. Pure classifieds, where owners list completed resale flats themselves, sit lightest. Commissions, success fees or builder-paid leads on RERA-registered projects sit heaviest. They almost certainly require agent registration in Delhi, Haryana and UP, and once revenue passes ₹20 lakh they trigger FIU-IND reporting.

Three further points need a lawyer's sign-off before launch. A "verified" badge creates consumer-law exposure unless the platform states exactly what was verified. Bulk scraping of registration portals risks IT Act offences for unauthorised access and exposure under the DPDP Act, and no Indian case law or guidance settles the question. Any in-app marketplace offering loans from several lenders brings the platform under RBI's digital lending rules.

## Valuation must be built from data nobody publishes in bulk

### Transaction prices exist, but only one record at a time

All three NCR jurisdictions run free deed-search portals: Delhi's DORIS/NGDRS ([Delhi Revenue Department](https://revenue.delhi.gov.in/revenue/important-information-regarding-registration-property)), Haryana's Web-HALRIS ([99acres](https://www.99acres.com/articles/land-records-online-in-haryana.html)) and UP's IGRSUP ([Kotak](https://www.kotak.bank.in/en/stories-in-focus/loans/home-loan/igrs-up-guide.html)). Each is built to look up one property or one party at a time. None offers bulk data or an API.

Deed data has three quality problems on top of that. Registered values are biased downward, because market prices run 50–70% above circle rates in prime Delhi colonies and above ₹50,000–60,000/sq ft on Gurugram's Golf Course Road, where circle rates lag ([Zee Business](https://www.zeebiz.com/real-estate/news-will-higher-circle-rates-make-home-buying-costlier-in-gurugram-experts-weigh-in-394078)). The Gurugram fake-PAN episode shows that party-level data cannot be trusted. And Noida's unregistered flats never appear in deed data at all, which biases the sample exactly where liquidity risk is highest.

The fastest legitimate route is to license data, and three vendors stand out.

| Vendor | Coverage | Price and terms |
|---|---|---|
| PropEquity | About 6 million registration records and 182,000+ projects | API delivery; pricing not public ([PropEquity](https://www.propequity.in/); [annual report](https://www.propequity.in/investorrelations/AnnualReport_2024-25.pdf)) |
| Liases Foras | About 5 million registration records, blended with listings and field surveys in an AVM covering 60 cities including Delhi | ₹499 per premium valuation, including a "Price Correction Risk" score ([Liases Foras](https://ps.liasesforas.com/)) |
| Square Yards | Claims 8.6 million transactions across the top 10 cities | Terms not stated ([Construction Week](https://www.constructionweekonline.in/business/square-yards-brings-price-transparency-in-real-estate-with-actual-recorded-history-of-transaction-prices)) |

Three other vendors fit less well. CRE Matrix's IndexTap covers Mumbai and Pune for consumers ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/indextap/)). Propstack focuses on commercial property ([Propstack](https://www.propstack.com/news/propstack-ready-to-seize-commercial-real-estate-opportunity-in-india/)). Zapkey has pivoted to a 90-day home-sale guarantee ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/zapkey/__VH8k93joR4tPE_XtvoXpr3OCfC2m_lIZCb-OLMSoq_I)). None of the vendors publishes NCR record counts, so years covered, fields and consumer-AVM usage rights all need negotiating.

Official indices serve calibration, not flat-level pricing. The RBI house price index is quarterly, registration-based, and covers 18 cities including Delhi ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=62833)). NHB RESIDEX uses bank and HFC valuation data ([NHB](https://www.nhb.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/WHITE-PAPER-NHB-RESIDEX-METHODOLOGY.pdf)).

Circle rates serve three purposes: a price floor, a signal of locality quality, and a predictor of under-declaration. They also carry a trap. Gurugram reportedly raised them 15–75% in 2026 ([BW Businessworld](https://www.businessworld.in/article/emerging-corridors-drive-up-to-75-circle-rate-hike-in-gurugram-report-602616)), so declared deed values will jump without any change in market prices. A model must not read that jump as appreciation.

Listings are the densest source of property attributes, but asking prices are not transaction prices, and no rigorous NCR study measures the gap. The platform should build a matched panel of listings and deeds for the same society, tower and size, and estimate the discount by micro-market itself.

### Expect AVM error well above Zillow's 7%

AVMs come in four families: comparable sales, hedonic regression, repeat-sales indices and machine-learning ensembles. All perform worst on new builds, on condition-sensitive stock and where data is sparse ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_valuation_model)). The best US benchmark, built on dense public data, misses by about 2% on listed homes and 7% on unlisted ones ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zillow)). Redfin's off-market error reaches **12.89% in data-thin Maine** ([Real Estate Witch](https://www.realestatewitch.com/redfin-home-value-estimator/)).

RICS has allowed AVMs in bank valuations since 1 January 2026, but only for "sufficiently uniform" property in active markets ([RICS](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/Bank-lending-valuations-and-MLV_2nd-edition_Dec2025.pdf)). Indian banks still require empanelled human valuers ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=3231&Mode=0)). An Indian AVM is therefore advisory, or a pre-screen for lenders. Claims like "98% accurate", or R² of 0.9 on scraped listings, are not comparable to median-error metrics.

A sound NCR design follows from this. Its core is a gradient-boosted or hedonic model with society and tower effects, because in NCR the project, tower and developer explain most of the variation in price. A same-society comparables layer makes each estimate explainable. A society-level repeat-sales index adjusts older comparables to today's prices, and features drawn from listing photos can capture condition. The output should be a range (from quantile or conformal methods) rather than a single number. Each estimate should carry a confidence grade based on how many recent comparables exist. The model should refuse to estimate builder floors, unauthorised colonies and homes without an OC. Publishing median absolute error, and the share of estimates within ±10% and ±20% of the sale price by segment, separates a credible tool from the unverifiable claims buyers already distrust.

### Rents, future value and liquidity each need their own model

**Rent.** Rent has no registered-transaction source in NCR. Delhi's 11-month agreements need not be registered ([NoBroker](https://www.nobroker.in/blog/how-to-register-rent-agreement-in-delhi/)), whereas Maharashtra requires every leave-and-licence agreement to be registered ([eDrafter](https://www.edrafter.in/maharashtra-rent-agreement/)). Rent models must instead use listing rents plus partnerships with property managers. They should estimate the gap between asking and achieved rent, and subtract maintenance, vacancy and brokerage explicitly to get net yield.

**Future value.** Model this as infrastructure events with dated milestones, not an extrapolation of 2019–2026 growth. Recent and pending milestones fall into three groups:

| Status | Projects |
|---|---|
| Open | Dwarka Expressway's Delhi section and UER-II Phase 1 (August 2025) ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/uer-ii-dwarka-expressway-open-today-what-changes-for-noida-dwarka-residents-homebuyers-489705-2025-08-17)); full Namo Bharat Delhi–Meerut line (February 2026) ([NewsOnAir](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/pm-modi-flags-off-meerut-metro-namo-bharat-train-in-uttar-pradesh)); Noida International Airport commercial flights (15 June 2026) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noida_International_Airport)) |
| In progress, with approvals pending | Gurugram Metro, targeting 2028 ([Square Yards](https://www.squareyards.com/blog/gurugram-metro-phase-2-gets-civil-works-clearance-linking-old-gurgaon-to-cyber-city)); Delhi–Gurugram–Bawal RRTS, cleared by the Public Investment Board but awaiting Cabinet approval ([Metro Rail Today](https://metrorailtoday.com/news/delhi-gurugram-bawal-namo-bharat-rrts-corridor-set-for-behror-extension-after-pib-clearance)); Gurugram–Faridabad–Jewar Namo Bharat corridor, DPR approved only at divisional level ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/gurugram/dpr-for-64-km-namo-bharat-corridor-linking-jewar-airport-gurugram-and-faridabad-approved/)) |
| Uncertain | Noida Film City, whose financial closure is reported inconsistently ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/noida-film-city-project-faces-fresh-delay-as-financial-closure-remains-pending-after-six-years)); Delhi Master Plan 2041, still not notified ([IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/master-plan-delhi-2041/)) |

The Gurugram metro study found that half the premium arrives before opening. Forecasts should therefore treat stage transitions such as Cabinet approval as price events, apply distance decay, weight projects by their probability of delivery, and mean-revert toward the RBI index's single-digit growth.

**Liquidity.** A liquidity score should weight registry and OC status most heavily. The developer's delivery record comes next, then society size and the number of past resales, then distance to an operating metro or RRTS station. Age, floor, facing and carpet efficiency matter less, followed by maintenance quality, waterlogging and AQI, and finally competing new supply nearby. Time-to-sell can be proxied from how long listings stay live. In Noida, registry status alone can decide liquidity: one rehabilitation framework had completed registries for only about 4,134 of 6,855 eligible units, around 60% ([Apartment Times](https://apartmenttimes.in/thousands-await-flat-registries-in-noida-nhrf-steps-in-with-city-wide-initiative/)).

**Neighbourhood data.** The free building blocks are CPCB's hourly AQI API ([data.gov.in](https://www.data.gov.in/catalog/real-time-air-quality-index)), NCRB district crime data ([NCRB](https://ncrb.gov.in/crime-in-india.html)) and OpenStreetMap, whose ODbL licence carries share-alike duties ([OSM](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)). Google Maps APIs cost $5–10 per 1,000 calls after free caps ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)). No official flood map was found. Strip names and PANs from deed data at ingestion so the platform does not create a DPDP problem.

### Virtual staging is nearly free and legally loaded

Google's current image models are cheap at list price. Nano Banana 2.1, generally available since 6 October 2026, costs **$0.0336 per 1K image and $0.0504 at 2K**. Nano Banana 2 Lite matches the 1K price, and Nano Banana Pro costs $0.134 at 1K or 2K. Batch processing halves these prices, and every output carries a SynthID watermark ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); [changelog](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/changelog)).

Staging a 3BHK listing takes about 48 images (8 rooms, 3 styles each, about 2 retries) and costs about **$1.61, or ₹150**. Free staging for every listing is therefore affordable, and building directly on the APIs is **3–10x cheaper** than buying a staging SaaS. The model line-up churns quickly, though: the original Nano Banana was deprecated about 13 months after launch. Some model names in this research were captured by an automated tool and should be checked on Google's pages before budgeting.

The failure modes are well documented. They include furniture drawn 20% too large, windows that do not exist, "melting" door frames and flooring silently replaced ([VirtuallyStage](https://www.virtuallystage.com/blog/why-ai-virtual-staging-looks-fake); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/weary-homebuyers-have-a-new-headache-misleading-ai-listing-photos-130020137.html)). The evidence of benefit is modest. NAR finds that 83% of buyers' agents say staging helps buyers visualise a home, but 38% rate virtual staging "of less importance", and offer uplifts of 1–5% appear for only a minority of sellers' agents ([NAR 2025](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-06/2025-profile-of-home-staging-report-06-26-2025.pdf)).

India's February 2026 IT Rules require a prominent label and permanent metadata on synthetic visuals, and California's AB 723 has set a "label it and link to the original" norm ([WAV Group](https://www.wavgroup.com/2025/11/19/californias-new-photo-disclosure-law-and-what-it-means-for-mlss-and-brokerages/)). Responsible staging therefore needs a "virtually staged" label on the image itself and the original photo one tap away. The structure must stay locked, so that walls, windows, floors and views never change. Furniture should be scaled to real room dimensions, and under-construction units should never be staged as if complete. The uplift is likely largest for empty, poorly photographed resale flats, but that is a hypothesis to A/B test, not a finding.

### Conversational and voice agents have the strongest evidence

Rightmove's numbers are the only published proof that conversational search lifts engagement and lead intent. Zillow's ChatGPT app, by contrast, produced engagement "pretty consistent with what we see on Zillow" ([The AI Innovator](https://theaiinnovator.com/zillows-svp-of-ai-on-launching-inside-chatgpt/)). And Rightmove gets under 0.5% of its traffic from LLM referrals. A ChatGPT app is therefore a distribution and brand play, not yet a lead channel.

Voice has the clearest Indian evidence. ConvoZen voice agents handle about **30–35% of NoBroker's 6–6.5 lakh daily conversations** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/how-convozen-is-emerging-as-nobrokers-new-growth-engine-517791-2026-02-24)). For NoBroker's builder business, they report **8% more site visits a month, and 12.5% of rejected customers won back** ([ConvoZen](https://convozen.ai/products/ai-voice-agent-for-real-estate)). In its interiors business, pre-sales conversion rose 9% ([ConvoZen](https://convozen.ai/success-stories/nobroker-interiors)).

The cost gap with human calling is large. A three-minute Hinglish qualification call costs about ₹12–17 on Bolna before telephony charges ([Bolna](https://www.bolna.ai/pricing)), against ₹15,000–25,000 a month for a human telecaller. Speed matters too: one vendor claims that answering a WhatsApp lead within 90 seconds lifts site-visit conversion to 25–30%, against 5–10% for slower replies ([Fix-it.ai](https://www.fix-it.ai/thesis/real-estate-lead-management-india)).

This research found no Hindi or Hinglish conversational or voice search at the leading portals. That is likely white space in a Hindi-dominant market, though demand for it is unmeasured.

The guardrails are well understood. Disclose at the start of a call that the caller is an AI. Record consent and scrub numbers against the DND registry. Keep personal data out of prompts. Ground every answer in the platform's own listings database.

### Document intelligence should flag, not certify

Indic OCR is now cheap and good. Sarvam charges ₹0.50 per page to digitise and ₹1.00 per page to extract ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/api-pricing)). It reports **93.52% Hindi accuracy** for Vision 2.1, measured on its own benchmark ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/sarvam-vision-2-1)). Landeed's Terra, launched in June 2026, answers natural-language questions on ownership, encumbrances and litigation. It draws on more than **773 million property documents** from 26 states and 4 union territories ([Realty N More](https://realtynmore.com/landeed-launches-terra-to-transform-fragmented/)).

Reading a 40-page property file costs under ₹100 in machine time. The real cost is the lawyer who signs off. Legal reasoning over stamps, handwriting and gaps in the title chain is assistive at best. The right output is "red flags to verify, with page citations", never a "clear title" verdict.

### Buy the models, build the trust layer

The platform should buy the model layer: foundation-model APIs plus Indic speech and OCR. At the start it should also buy voice orchestration from vendors such as Bolna or ConvoZen, because telephony, interruption handling and latency are hard engineering problems. It should build the parts that create trust and data advantage. Those are search grounded in its own inventory, staging that respects room dimensions and locks the structure, listing verification and deduplication, document workflows specific to Delhi, Haryana and UP, and the AVM.

Put every model behind an abstraction layer, because Google's image line-up alone changed four times in 12 months. Design WhatsApp-first. A WhatsApp utility message costs ₹0.115, against ₹0.86 for a marketing template ([MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026)), so the pricing rewards helpful service over broadcast spam.

The catch is acquisition cost. At ₹1,000 per lead and 2% conversion, a mid-segment booking costs about ₹50,000. In premium Gurugram, ₹3,000 leads converting at 1% cost ₹3 lakh per booking, and luxury leads cost ₹6 lakh. That is close to the whole commission.

This is the real AI thesis. AI's main value is not a nicer search box. It cuts the labour-heavy work that held full-stack brokerage to Square Yards' 8% EBITDA margin: instant response, qualification, follow-up, document chasing and closing coordination. The goal is a two- to fivefold cut in acquisition and servicing cost.

Home-loan referral should be bundled from day one. It accounts for about **half of Square Yards' revenue** ([OnlineMarketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/square-yards-raises-95m-with-more-to-come-as-ipo-edges-closer/)) and about 22% of NoBroker's ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/nobrokers-future-is-beyond-home-listings-but-will-profits-follow/)). In the US, Rocket–Redfin turned it into a buyer incentive: a one-point rate cut in year one ([Rocket](https://www.rocketcompanies.com/press-release/rocket-companies-completes-acquisition-of-redfin/)).

Two lines should stay out of the core business. Interiors, where Livspace ran at −13.4% EBITDA in FY25 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/livspace-posts-rs-1460-cr-revenue-in-fy25-losses-shrink-42-10559863)), works better as a referral partnership. iBuying should be avoided entirely. Zillow's FY2021 inventory write-downs totalled $407.9M ([Zillow 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764022000013/z-20211231.htm)), and Opendoor's contribution margin fell to 3.4% in FY2025 ([Opendoor 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801169/000180116926000010/open-20251231.htm)).

### History punishes cash burn, inventory and anti-broker purity

The cautionary tales are specific. Housing.com burned about ₹100 crore on a 2015 advertising blitz after SoftBank invested ([Quartz](https://qz.com/india/442399/timeline-the-rise-and-fall-of-rahul-yadav-the-bad-boy-of-indian-startups)) and was eventually sold for about $47.5M in stock. NestAway raised about ₹905 crore and sold for ₹90 crore, a 95% valuation cut ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/2023/06/aurum-acquires-tiger-global-backed-nestaway-at-95-valuation-cut/)). NoBroker is still loss-making after more than a decade and has drifted toward services. Purplebricks, the UK's low-fee anti-agent model, hit an "adoption ceiling", raised fees, began to resemble a traditional agency, and saw its UK business sold for £1 ([Mike DelPrete](https://www.mikedp.com/articles/2023/2/2/2-key-learnings-from-a-purplebricks-retrospective-4l4hk); [IdeaProof](https://ideaproof.io/failure/purplebricks)).

The winners control the transaction and its financing, or consolidate. Examples are Square Yards, Rocket–Redfin, Compass–Anywhere ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-closes-1-6b-anywhere-merger-forms-industry-giant/)) and Aurum's three acquisitions.

Funding is selective. Indian proptech raised **$552M across 32 deals in 2025**, dominated by construction tech and hospitality. Investors have shown "a clear shift away from classifieds-only platforms" toward full-stack, transaction-led models ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/report/after-2024-revival-indian-proptech-raises-550-mn-in-2025-amid-full-stack-shift-and-ipo-wave-11069442)). A seed or Series A raise will need early transaction revenue, not traffic. Consolidation also creates exit paths: Aurum, Info Edge, Square Yards and REA are all active buyers.

### Start in Gurugram's mid-premium corridors and Noida resale; leave Delhi for later

Gurugram is the obvious first market. It took **42% of NCR sales and 48% of launches in 2025**, and its capital values rose 10% YoY in Q2 2026 ([India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/business/news/is-gurugram-becoming-ncrs-ultimate-housing-growth-engine-heres-what-experts-say-2026-09-15-1054284)). It accounted for 91% of NCR's ₹5 crore-plus sales in H1 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/gurugram-accounts-for-91-of-delhi-ncr-s-5-cr-home-sales-in-2025-125080700082_1.html)), and it is where CP-paying developers and NRI buyers concentrate. It also holds **57% of NCR's unsold stock**. Its 2026 decline was concentrated in the ₹5–10 crore band, while the ₹2–5 crore band held steady ([BW Businessworld/Knight Frank](https://www.businessworld.in/article/ncr-housing-sales-slip-11-till-september-2026-gurugram-leads-decline-knight-frank-626888)). The entry point is therefore two or three corridors in the **₹1.5–5 crore band**: Dwarka Expressway, Golf Course Extension Road, and New Gurugram/SPR. It is not ultra-luxury, where relationship brokers dominate.

**Noida Expressway and Greater Noida West resale** come second. Supply is deep: 9,738+ resale listings in Greater Noida West on 99acres alone ([99acres](https://www.99acres.com/resale-flats-in-greater-noida-west-greater-noida-ffid)). The registry crisis makes verified registry and dues status the single most valuable trust signal there. The platform must still never certify title it cannot guarantee.

**Ghaziabad and Faridabad** are a plausible third wave. They are value-seeking end-user markets with the fastest recent appreciation (+15% and +18%), though the research says little else about them.

**Delhi resale** comes last. DDA's conversion freeze, GPA chains, colony regularisation and 2014 circle rates make it the most document-heavy and slowest-scaling segment.

For the cold start, the easiest supply to seed is developer inventory, through CP agreements with RERA-registered projects in the chosen corridors. Resale supply in dense gated societies can be built through RWA partnerships, as NoBrokerHood's 25,000+ society footprint shows ([PropNewsTime](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MzczOTI=/nobroker-targets-profitability-within-8-10-months-as-fy26-revenue-crosses-inr-1-000-crore)).

The evidence favours working with brokers, not against them. Brokers provide **61% of Housing.com's revenue** ([Investing.com](https://ph.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/aurum-proptech-q2-fy27-slides-profit-streak-continues-as-housingcom-deal-closes-93CH-2632633)). Purplebricks hit a ceiling, and Zillow faced agent backlash. A hybrid is the plausible design: AI tooling for NCR brokers and CPs, alongside the platform's own desk for direct and NRI buyers.

### Risks that could sink the model

The largest risk is the market cycle. NCR is cyclical and sensitive to investor sentiment, and it enters 2027 with a rate hike and heavy luxury supply. The second is unit economics: paid acquisition costs about as much as the commission on a deal, and a customer transacts perhaps once a decade.

The third is fast followers. Aurum and 99acres are now lean, AI-branded and close to generating cash ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/info-edge-expects-ai-tools-to-boost-recruitment-99acres-to-generate-cash-by-fy27-3162-PE1_COR)), and they can copy any AI interface within months. The fourth is regulatory load, with RERA, PMLA, the DPDP May 2027 deadline, IT Rules labelling and RBI lending rules all arriving at once.

Liability is the fifth risk. Wrong title, registry or valuation advice exposes the platform, and Zillow Offers shows how a pricing model can fail at scale. Off-book cash and registry fraud bring PMLA and reputational exposure. Smaller but real risks remain: transaction data may not be licensable on acceptable terms, brokers may resist price transparency as US agents resisted Zillow, and AI models are deprecated every 6–9 months.

## What the product-thinking phase should take forward

The evidence supports ranking problem and opportunity spaces, not features. The ranking below weighs severity of pain, strength of evidence, how much AI changes the economics, and how directly each space links to revenue and defensibility.

| 2 | Lead hygiene: consent-based, capped-share, qualified contact | Buyers, resale sellers, developers, brokers | Strong: 4–15 brokers per lead; "10 sales reps in 24 hours"; 4–6 hour response times | Voice/WhatsApp agents at ₹4–6/min replace telecallers and respond instantly | CP commissions; premium for capped or exclusive leads | Whether developers and brokers will pay more for fewer, better leads |

The open questions below should be closed before or during product thinking. Several can be answered quickly and cheaply.

| Open question | Why it matters | Fastest way to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Lead with resale or with developer inventory? | Developer CP revenue is faster but crowded; resale is where the mid-market buyer and the trust gap are | Model unit economics for both; 20–30 interviews each with Gurugram/GNW buyers, owners, CPs and mid-size developers |
| Partner with brokers or displace them? | Brokers control supply and fund the portals; anti-broker models hit ceilings | Test broker willingness to pay for AI tools and capped leads; map dealer gatekeeping in target societies |
| Which persona first: ₹60 lakh–1.5 cr end-user in GNW/Ghaziabad, ₹1.5–5 cr Gurugram buyer, or NRI? | Each needs different trust proof, channels and economics | Survey plus forum evidence (r/gurgaon, r/noida, r/delhi), which this research could not collect |
| What transaction data can be licensed for a consumer AVM, at what cost and coverage? | Price fairness and the AVM depend on it; scraping is legally risky | Requests to PropEquity, Liases Foras and Square Yards for NCR record counts, fields and licence terms |
| What exactly can "verified" mean, and who is liable when it is wrong? | Consumer-law exposure; it is the core of the brand | Legal opinion; pilot pricing for lawyer review; professional indemnity quotes |
| Is the platform a RERA agent, an FIU-IND reporting entity, a multi-lender LSP and/or an intermediary under each candidate model? | Determines compliance cost and launch timeline | One regulatory memo per business model, covering Delhi, Haryana and UP |
| How large is the off-book cash component in target resale segments, and what is the platform's policy? | PMLA exposure and deal fall-through | Broker and lawyer interviews; a policy decision before launch |
| What is real CAC through WhatsApp, YouTube corridor content, SEO and RWA partnerships? | Paid CAC is close to the commission per deal | Small-budget channel tests in one corridor |
| How much do NCR buyers want Hindi/Hinglish voice and chat? | Likely white space, but unmeasured | Instrumented pilot with language choice |
| What AVM error can be achieved and published per segment, and where should the model refuse to estimate? | Overconfidence destroys trust (the Zillow precedent) | Back-test on licensed deeds by micro-market before showing any estimate |
| How fast will Aurum, 99acres and MagicBricks copy AI features? | Sets how long an interface lead lasts | Competitive monitoring; plan for months, not years |

Beyond these questions, product thinking has to settle four cross-cutting choices that the evidence frames but does not decide. The first is the platform's role in the deal. It could act as an agent that earns a fee on closing, or as a neutral marketplace that sells verification and leads. That choice sets its RERA and PMLA exposure and shapes how buyers judge its conflicts of interest. The second is where a human must check a fact before the platform states it; title, valuation and loan eligibility are the obvious candidates. The third is channel. WhatsApp and voice, rather than an app, may be the main interface for NCR buyers and for its broker-heavy supply side. The fourth is how the platform proves delivery to sellers and developers. Prepaid plans with weak refunds are what users resent most about the incumbents.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the opportunity. What is scarce in NCR housing is not information about what is for sale. It is confidence that a specific flat is real, available, fairly priced and legally transferable, at a known total cost. AI's decisive contribution is economic rather than cosmetic. It collapses the cost of the trust work full-stack brokers do by hand: reading documents, chasing availability, qualifying leads in seconds and coordinating a dozen parties to registration. That makes a verified, transaction-led model viable at margins Square Yards reached only through mortgages. Two facts sharpen the timing. NCR's new supply has moved upmarket while its buyers have not, which pushes mid-market demand into resale, where verification matters most. And a 2027 shaped by a rate hike will favour homes buyers can inspect over promises they cannot.

The durable advantage will not come from the chat window, which incumbents can copy within a quarter. It will come from a per-society record of availability, registry and dues status, and real closing prices. That record accumulates only by handling transactions honestly, and the incumbents' shared-lead economics cannot easily produce it. Compliance is also an opening rather than only a cost. DPDP's May 2027 deadline and the 2026 IT Rules force incumbents to retrofit consent and AI labelling onto businesses built on reselling leads, while a new entrant can design them in from the start. The founder's next phase should therefore treat trust and closing as the product, and search, staging and chat as ways to reach users, not as the advantage itself.
