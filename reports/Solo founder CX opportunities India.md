# Solo founders should chase India's stuck money

India's complaint data ranks **cyber fraud, health-insurance claims, banking and credit access, retirement and succession paperwork, and housing** as the country's most painful consumer problems. The best openings for a solo, AI-first founder sit one step back from the very worst pain. They are disputes in which a known sum of the customer's own money is held by an institution that profits from delay, a free official escalation route exists that consumers fail to use, and the work is documents and deadlines rather than field operations. We scored every opportunity on one weighted 100-point rubric and put each through licence, liability, operations, data-access and revenue gates. Five clear the bar: a **health-insurance claim-and-bill advocate (87)**, a **"tenancy record" that protects tenants' deposits and handles small landlords' compliance (83)**, a **NEET/JEE counselling operations copilot (79)**, a **fee-only study-abroad copilot (78)**, and an **after-a-death "stuck money" desk (76)**. The last merges the succession, IEPF and unclaimed-asset ideas that three separate research tracks proposed. The evidence behind the five is uneven. Health claims rest on hard regulator data: health-claim complaints rose 35% in FY25, Ombudsman complaints about rejected claims nearly tripled between FY22 and FY26, and consumers already pay a 20% success fee to get money back. The tenancy and study-abroad scores lean on anecdote and on willingness to pay that nobody has tested. All five winners share one product shape. Each is a consumer-side back office (document AI, statutory clocks and escalation ladders, delivered over WhatsApp) working against insurers, banks, airlines, agents and landlords whose revenue depends on the problem continuing. Several markets look tempting but should be avoided because supply, capital, licensing or free alternatives limit them: rail waitlists, cab cancellations, instant home help, property listings, insurance distribution, lawyer marketplaces and AI document explainers. A six-week test costing roughly ₹1–1.5 lakh can confirm or kill the leaders. It uses free diagnosis tools, fulfilment done by hand, UPI pre-commitments and hard kill thresholds. Three legal questions must be settled first. The most important is whether a paid third party may represent a policyholder before the Insurance Ombudsman.

## Fraud and health claims top a complaint ledger that grew 56% in a year

India's formal complaint channels are filling up faster than they resolve anything. The National Consumer Helpline (NCH) registered **20.5 lakh complaints in FY26, up from 13.2 lakh in FY25**. E-commerce's share fell from 39.8% in FY23 to 28.2%, so most of the growth now comes from food, travel, electronics, telecom and other services ([Dataful](https://dataful.in/datasets/20716/)). By our arithmetic on those figures, complaints outside e-commerce grew about 65% in a single year. Around the NCH sit three other large streams. The RBI Ombudsman framework took **13.34 lakh complaints in FY25** ([AffairsCloud](https://affairscloud.com/rbis-annual-report-of-ombudsman-scheme-2024-25-complaints-up-13-55-loans-and-credit-cards-lead-grievances/)). CPGRAMS, the central public-grievance portal, received 18.34 lakh grievances for central ministries in FY26 ([LS USQ 4034](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU4034_rMyCth.pdf)). Complaints about unsolicited commercial calls and messages reached **31.1 lakh in 2025, up 60%** ([LS SQ 474](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AS474_kHdre1.pdf)).

Ranking sectors by volume gives a different order from ranking them by value. When consumers escalate disputes to the consumer commissions, which are quasi-judicial, insurance makes up about a quarter of district and state filings and **housing accounts for 44% of National Commission filings** ([IJR Consumer Justice Report 2026](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/IJR_Consumer%20Justice%20Report_2026_English.pdf)). These are the expensive disputes, and they wait. The National Commission averages **448 days** a case ([IJR press release](https://indiajusticereport.org/files/National%20Consumer%20Report%20Press%20Release_IJR%202026.pdf)). 5.74 lakh cases were pending across all tiers in January 2026 ([LS USQ 2069](https://fcainfoweb.nic.in/PMS/writereaddata/2026_LS_B_2069.pdf)), and 40% of bench posts were vacant ([GetNyay](https://getnyay.in/data/consumer-justice)). The NCH reports that 100% of complaints are "disposed". That is a closure count, not a resolution count. Official disposal times for the same years range from 13 to 66 days ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2100545); [LS USQ 3040](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU3040_MV7JFl.pdf)), so even the measure of success is defined inconsistently.

**Cyber fraud is the deepest pain on every measure, but it is the least winnable.** Banks put about 15% of reported losses under lien, but less than 2% of the liened money reaches victims. Only about 3% of complaints become FIRs. In a LocalCircles survey, 51% of UPI-fraud victims never filed a complaint at all ([LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/upi-fraud-complaint)). The root causes sit with banks, telecom operators and the police, though, not with a market that a product can serve. **Health insurance is the most acute pain that a product can actually move.** Ombudsman complaints about rejected claims rose from 11,942 in FY22 to **35,186 in FY26**. Courts and consumer forums decided **55% of FY26 insurance cases in the policyholder's favour** ([LS USQ 3556](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3556_EndnJB.pdf)). In LocalCircles' March 2026 survey of more than 54,000 people, 44% of claimants said their claim was rejected or partly paid for invalid reasons, and about half waited 6–48 hours to be discharged ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/health-insurance-premiums-rise-sharply-while-claim-experiences-remain-difficult-finds-localcircles-survey/79872.html)). That wait persists even though IRDAI has required a three-hour discharge decision since July 2024 ([Patient Square](https://patientsquare.com/in/blog/irdai-cashless-rules-doctors/)). Insurers' first decisions are reversed so often that the rejection itself looks like a pricing lever. On top of the claim, only 14% of private-hospital patients report no billing problems ([LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/private-hospital-loot)).

The banking row hides a navigation failure that a product could fix directly. Of 9.11 lakh complaints that reached the RBI's central processing centre in FY25, **7.76 lakh were closed as "non-maintainable"** ([AffairsCloud](https://affairscloud.com/rbis-annual-report-of-ombudsman-scheme-2024-25-complaints-up-13-55-loans-and-credit-cards-lead-grievances/)). That means roughly 7.8 lakh people a year file in the wrong place or the wrong way. Retirement and succession paperwork is barely visible in consumer-complaint data because it goes to EPFO's grievance system, CPGRAMS and bank branches instead. Its scale shows up elsewhere. EPFO rejected about one in five claims in FY25, mostly over name, date-of-birth and employer-record mismatches ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/epfos-instant-pf-withdrawal-promise-has-a-catch-one-in-five-claims-still-gets-rejected-541466-2026-07-07)). **₹1.84 lakh crore of financial assets lie unclaimed** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/banking/story/finance-minister-launches-rs-1-84-lakh-crore-unclaimed-assets-campaign-496827-2025-10-04)). The RBI's fund for unclaimed deposits, the DEA Fund, still grew to ₹86,917 crore by June 2026 despite a recovery campaign ([KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/sectors/unclaimed-bank-deposits-with-rbi-fund-rise-to-rs-86917-crore-govt)). Only 32% of families found it easy to transfer a dead relative's assets, and the share who paid bribes rose from 52% to 58% since 2022 ([LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/property-transfer-bribes)).

Housing is badly under-measured rather than mild. The Union government says it does not centrally maintain RERA complaint data, so the best signals are the share of litigation and surveys. One survey found **46% of residents or their families were stuck in a residential lift in the past year** ([LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/lift-maintenance-survey)). The others fall into a tier of high-frequency, lower-ticket pain. Education is measured mostly through surveys and is acute at specific moments. Travel and durables are growing fast. Vehicle ownership is huge in volume but low in severity per incident. E-commerce and telecom generate the most raw tickets but already have the most capacity to handle them. There are two caveats. LocalCircles panels are self-selected, about two-thirds male and roughly 40% from tier-1 cities. The researchers found no national NCH breakdown by sector for 2024–26, so a Karnataka table stands in for the national mix.

## Incumbents are paid by the side that causes the pain

Across sectors, the pain persists for the same structural reason: almost every scaled incumbent is paid by the counterparty, by the supply side, or by the float on money held back. The table summarises it, and the paragraphs that follow pick out the cases that matter most for opportunity selection.

| Sector | Who the incumbents are | Who pays them | Why the pain survives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health insurance | Insurers and their third-party administrators (TPAs); hospital billing desks; Policybazaar, Ditto and agents; Insurance Samadhan | Insurers (commissions), hospitals (bills), or a 20% success fee | Denials and deductions save insurers money; aggregators may not rate products; the one consumer-side specialist is small and runs on manual operations |
| Banking and credit | Banks, NBFCs, credit bureaus, fintech apps, debt-settlement firms | Interest, interchange and lender fees | Bureaus are paid by lenders; fintechs earn from credit and distribution, not one-off disputes; the free Ombudsman rejects most complaints on form |
| Retirement and succession | EPFO, banks, registrar and transfer agents (RTAs), mutual funds, insurers; government lookup portals; local agents | Float on unclaimed money; bribes; small fees | Whoever holds the money has no reason to hurry; government portals stop at finding the asset; agents are opaque and offline |
| Cyber fraud | Banks, police cyber cells, I4C (the Home Ministry's cybercrime centre); lawyers' blogs | Nobody is paid to restore money | Restoring money needs police and court sign-off; fake "recovery agents" poison trust |
| Housing and rentals | NoBroker, MagicBricks, Housing.com; MyGate; Livspace, HomeLane; JustDial | Transaction fees; advertising; project margins; leads sold to vendors | Everyone earns at the start of a tenancy or project, or from the society committee, not from fairness at the end |
| Education | School and college portals; study-abroad agents; coaching chains | Schools, colleges, universities and lenders | Advisers paid by the supply side steer families toward whoever pays them |
| Travel | Airlines, online travel agencies (OTAs), VFS and BLS (the visa outsourcing firms), Atlys | Ticket margins and float on refunds; governments | Airlines and OTAs are the other party in every refund dispute |
| Durables and vehicles | Brand service networks; Urban Company; GoMechanic (now Spinny); Cars24 | Service fees, parts, technician commissions, used-car margins | Brand service is expensive and hard to reach; the used-car trade has bought the neutral information layer |
| E-commerce and telecom | Platforms and operators | Consumers directly | Scaled in-house complaint handling plus 1,853 NCH partner companies leave little room for a third party |

**In health insurance, every adviser the patient meets is paid by someone else.** Insurers disallowed ₹15,100 crore of health claims in FY24 and settled 82% of claims by number but only 71.3% by value ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/health-insurers-reject-claims-worth-rs-15-100-crore-in-fy24-irdai-124123100615_1.html)). Third-party administrators work for insurers, and hospital billing desks work for hospitals. Licensed web aggregators earn insurers' commissions, and IRDAI bars them from publishing product ratings or reviews ([Taxhint](https://taxhint.in/insurance-web-aggregator/)). Policybazaar handled about 70,000 claims in the April–June 2026 quarter, but only for policies it sold ([InvestorStack](https://www.investorstack.in/earnings-calls/policybzr)). IRDAI does not even record why claims are denied ([LS USQ 1146](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1146_Rq9U22.pdf)). The only established consumer-side firm, Insurance Samadhan, charges ₹999 plus a 20% success fee ([Insurance Samadhan](https://www.insurancesamadhan.com/)), yet it earned only ₹6.2 crore in FY25 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/insurance-samadhan/)). Demand is proven; the bottleneck is the manual, human-heavy way it delivers the service.

**In money held by institutions, the holder earns float on the delay.** Discovery is now largely solved by government portals: RBI's UDGAM for unclaimed deposits, SEBI MITRA for mutual funds, IRDAI's Bima Bharosa for insurance, and a common Finance Ministry portal ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/rbi-irdai-and-sebi-step-up-efforts-to-help-citizens-reclaim-unclaimed-financial-assets/)). Payout still happens at the branch, the registrar or the EPFO office ([India Policy Hub](https://indiapolicyhub.in/2026/08/10/how-to-claim-unclaimed-bank-deposits-rbi-udgam/)), and none of them is paid to hurry. The consumer-side specialists that exist are real but small. Kustodian.life, which fixes stuck EPF claims, reached about **₹1.2 crore of annualised revenue in 2025** without outside funding ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/09/kustodianlife-unlock-money-stuck-in-insurance-epf-insurance)). Agents who recover shares from the Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF) charge ₹10,000–12,000 a case ([InvestorLink](https://investorlink.in/recovery-of-shares-from-iepf)). Yellow, a will-writing service, made only ₹46 lakh in FY24 because it sells before anyone has died ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/startups/how-yellow-plans-to-bring-order-to-estate-planning/)).

**In housing, education and mobility, the incumbents earn from the transaction, not from what comes after it.** NoBroker's ability to make money is "concentrated in a narrow window around the transaction" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/nobrokers-future-is-beyond-home-listings-but-will-profits-follow/)). MyGate earns 65–70% of its income from advertising ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/mygate-trims-fy25-loss-by-61-to-%E2%82%B915-4-cr/)). Livspace is the contractor whose work a homeowner would want checked ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/livspace-posts-rs-1460-cr-revenue-in-fy25-losses-shrink-42-10559863)). Leverage Edu, the biggest study-abroad counsellor, earns **70% of its revenue from university placement commissions** and lost ₹106 crore in FY25 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/leverage-edu-posts-rs-106-cr-loss-on-rs-173-cr-revenue-in-fy25-11105388)). Cars24 bought CarInfo, the vehicle-records app, and Spinny bought GoMechanic and Autocar India, so the neutral information layer for car buyers now belongs to used-car dealers ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/cars24-acquires-carinfo-to-expand-into-vehicle-information-services-10974050); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/13/indias-spinny-lines-up-160m-funding-to-acquire-gomechanic-sources-say)). The founder's separate Delhi NCR flat-platform research reached the same conclusion about property portals: they sell enquiries to brokers rather than standing with the buyer.

Two more failures make things worse. First, the free official channels close complaints rather than resolve them. The NCH's 100% "disposal", the 85% of central RBI complaints rejected on format, and the 448-day consumer commissions all leave the consumer to find the right path alone. Second, drafting is now free. GetNyay drafts consumer complaints and builds escalation paths at no charge ([GetNyay](https://getnyay.in/data/consumer-justice)). RTI Wiki offers more than 4,300 free guides and drafting tools ([RTI Wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/)). ChatGPT Go was made free for a year to every user in India ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/openai-offers-free-chatgpt-go-for-one-year-to-all-users-in-india)). The value has therefore moved from writing the letter to executing, following through and owning the outcome. That is the gap a new entrant can fill.

### Normalising the scores

The researchers used the same seven criteria but calibrated them differently. Each tended to rate the strongest ideas in their own sector at 29–31, and the same "after a death" idea scored anywhere from 27 to 29 with willingness-to-pay scores from 3 to 5. Every opportunity was therefore re-scored against fixed anchors.

**Pain severity** reaches 5 only when ₹50,000 or more, health or housing is at stake and official data confirms that redress fails. Pain supported only by anecdote is capped at 4.

**Market size** reflects annual episodes multiplied by a realistic ticket.

**Willingness to pay** reaches 5 only where consumers already pay intermediaries ₹5,000 or more per episode. It is 4 where payments of ₹1,000–5,000 are proven, 3 where payment is plausible but untested, 2 where free substitutes exist, and 1 for near zero.

**Solo feasibility** combines build effort, human minutes per order and access to organic distribution. Anything that needs a human to review high-stakes filings, or a physical step, is capped at 3. Anything that needs court work or a 24/7 emergency response is capped at 2.

The weights follow the solo-founder evidence. Every verified solo or creator success had distribution it already owned, from Striver's YouTube audience before TUF+ ([Striver on X](https://x.com/striver_79/status/1902989872161546338)) to SiteGPT's launch-platform and free-tool traffic ([superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/sitegpt)). Indian consumer apps earn only $0.03 per download ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/22/indias-app-market-is-booming-but-global-platforms-are-capturing-most-of-the-gains/)). Even Kuku FM, a profitable audio-subscription app, spends about 74% of revenue on marketing ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-kuku-ipo-revenue-surges/)). Service work runs at about 20% gross margin against 90%+ for software ([Albion VC](https://medium.com/thealbionvc/software-vs-services-what-every-saas-founder-should-know-6f3e103c289c)). AI itself is now cheap: document extraction costs about ₹1 a page ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/api-pricing)) and a Hindi voice agent ₹4–5.5 a minute ([Bolna](https://www.bolna.ai/pricing)). The weights are therefore **willingness to pay 20, solo feasibility 20, pain 15, product-led-ness 15, market size 10, incumbent weakness 10 and AI leverage 10**. AI leverage is weighted low because every candidate now has it.

Five gates sit on top of the score. The **licence** gate asks whether the core value needs an IRDAI, SEBI, RBI, Bar Council or medical licence. The **liability and trust** gate asks whether one AI error could cause irreversible harm, or whether the product could be mistaken for a scam. The **physical-operations** gate asks whether every order needs someone on the ground. The **data-rail and liquidity** gate asks whether the product depends on gated public infrastructure, such as Account Aggregator data (open only to regulated entities, per [Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/faq/)), Aadhaar authentication or vehicle databases, or on a two-sided marketplace. The **revenue floor** requires willingness to pay of at least 3 or a named business buyer.

A score of 75 or more marks a strong solo candidate. A score of 70–74 is viable with a specific fix. An idea that fails the revenue floor becomes a feature or a free funnel, whatever its score.

### What separates the top five

**The claim-and-bill advocate leads because it is the only idea that scores at the top on both evidence and willingness to pay.** Standalone health insurers alone rejected 5.68 lakh claims in FY25 ([LS USQ 3556](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU3556_EndnJB.pdf)). The free escalation ladder works: the insurer's grievance officer, then Bima Bharosa, then the Ombudsman. People already pay 20% to get it climbed for them. The work is reading the policy wording, bill, discharge summary and rejection letter, then matching them against IRDAI rules on moratoria, non-payable items and proportionate deductions. Language models handle that well. A flat fee of ₹999–2,999, or a 5–10% success fee, would undercut the incumbent while keeping software margins. Every upload also builds a database of denial reasons by insurer and TPA, which the regulator does not collect. That database is the moat, and it is also material for PR. Solo feasibility is held at 3 because every filing needs human review. The licence question is the single biggest unknown on the list.

**The tenancy record ranks second on product strength, not on evidence.** Bengaluru tenants commonly lock up 6–9 months of rent as a deposit ([NoBroker News](https://www.nobroker.in/news/karnataka/bangalore/bengaluru-rental-woes-security-deposit-refund-guide/)). One widely shared case returned **₹19,604 of a ₹1 lakh deposit** after "painting, polishing" deductions ([Goodreturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/news/bengaluru-tenant-gets-just-rs-19-604-back-from-rs-1-lakh-deposit-what-karnataka-law-says-about-re-1534645.html)). The Model Tenancy Act's two-month cap is adopted unevenly, and some sources say Karnataka has no cap at all ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/real-estate/story/model-tenancy-act-how-new-security-deposits-rent-agreement-entry-exit-rules-will-benefit-tenants-landlords-504839-2025-12-03); [RealtyHunting](https://realtyhunting.com/bangalore-rent-rules-2026-new-deposit-caps-registration-laws/)). The product therefore relies on a contract plus timestamped evidence, which works in any state. At move-in, an AI video condition report is co-signed by both parties and the agreement includes a deduction schedule. At move-out, the AI compares the two states and drafts a notice if needed. The landlord side covers registration reminders, a ₹100 court-record check on the tenant ([eCourtsIndia](https://blogs.ecourtsindia.com/2026/10/05/background-check-driver-maid-caregiver-india/)), rent reminders and maintenance triage. That side pays fastest, because NRI and out-of-town owners already pay property managers. It must charge a subscription, because RBI rules ended credit-card rent payments through fintech apps ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/phonepe-paytm-cred-end-rent-service-as-rbi-tightens-payment-rules-125091800422_1.html)). There is no survey or official statistic on how often deposits go unreturned, and no evidence on tenants' willingness to pay. It is also the natural post-transaction module for the founder's NCR flat platform, which matters if the two projects are meant to share a brand.

**The NEET/JEE copilot ranks third because families visibly pay, despite crowded incumbents.** NEET-UG 2026 had **22.8 lakh registrants and 11.2 lakh qualifiers** ([Telegraph Edugraph](https://www.telegraphindia.com/edugraph/news/over-11-21-lakh-qualify-as-nta-releases-neet-ug-2026-detailed-statistics-absentees-rise-sharply-aryan-gupta-tops/cid/2170469)). Paid counselling packages cost ₹19,999–49,999 ([CollegeForMe](https://www.collegeforme.in/neet-counselling)). JoSAA, the engineering-seat counselling, cancels a seat if the candidate completes only two of its three post-round steps ([Vidyamandir](https://vidyamandir.com/studyhub/josaa-counselling-dates/)). Rank predictors are already a commodity ([JEE Counsellor](https://jeecounsellor.me/)). The edge lies in execution across the parallel central (MCC) and state processes: document validity, deadlines, freeze/float/slide decisions and deposit refunds. Its weaknesses are a three-month season and a crowded field.

**The fee-only study-abroad copilot ranks fourth because the decision is the largest in the education sector and the adviser is the most conflicted.** Indian students spend about **₹6.3 trillion a year abroad** ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/inr-6-3tn-indian-student-spend-abroad-sharpens-focus-on-at-home-internationalisation/)). Agents earn 6–10% of first-year fees, roughly ₹2–3 lakh per student, according to 2022 reporting ([Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/international/study-abroad-regulating-education-counsellors-acting-as-agents-need-of-the-hour-news-122374)). Rising visa refusals, including about 80% of Indian student applications to Canada in 2025 ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/world/canada-tightens-visa-rules-rejects-80-of-indian-student-visas-in-2025-3720747)), make unbiased risk analysis more valuable. The risk lies in shrinking volumes, 1.33 lakh fewer Indians studying abroad ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/study-abroad-1-33-lakh-drop-indians-canada-australia-uk-us-student-visa-work-permit-immigration-germany-bangladesh-russia-gain/amp)), and in whether families will pay a fee when agents appear "free".

**The stuck-money desk scores fifth but has the highest ceiling.** It fails no gate outright and carries the strongest willingness-to-pay precedents in personal finance. About **86.5 lakh deaths a year** are registered (2022 data) ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/india/86-5-lakh-deaths-in-2022-15-dip-over-covid-hit-2021-crs-data/articleshow/121694145.cms)). RBI's 2025 directions on deceased customers set a 15-day settlement clock with compensation for delay ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/rbi/claims-settlement-deceased-customers-rbi-directions.html)), although one commenter there reports the circular now shows as "withdrawn", so its status needs checking. The IEPF received 1.33 lakh refund applications in two years and approved about 75,000 ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/personal-finance/unclaimed-funds-in-iepf-over-75000-claims-approved-in-two-years-rs-7782-crore-dividend-refunded)). It scores below the leaders only because notaries, stamp paper, branch visits and succession certificates keep it partly a service business. Folding the EPF fixer (#6) into it as the death-claim and pension module produces the strongest bundle in this research.

Below the top five, three groups stand out. Several ideas are strong but limited by operations or licences: the EPF fixer, the NRI desk, the cheque-bounce kit and fleet compliance. Two are strong but rest on thin evidence: the renovation copilot and the IVF navigator. Credit-report disputes and visa files are clean, but their willingness to pay is untested.

## Every strong idea is a consumer-side back office working against a counterparty

**The first and dominant pattern is an advocate or autopilot that acts for the consumer against institutions that profit from the problem.** Insurers keep disallowed amounts, banks and registrars hold unclaimed money, airlines push credit shells, agents collect commissions and landlords keep deductions. In each case a free official remedy exists but consumers fail to use it. 85% of central RBI complaints are rejected on form. Half of UPI-fraud victims never complain. Formal channels capture a tiny share of airline pain: DGCA logs about **1.76 complaints per 10,000 passengers** ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/business/domestic-airlines-lose-over-8-lakh-passengers-in-a-month-as-traffic-falls-over-6-percent-enn26092305079)), while IndiGo's December 2025 meltdown alone hit more than 10 lakh passengers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_IndiGo_scheduling_crisis)). The product converts free channels into outcomes. It is not a new channel.

**The second pattern is that willingness to pay follows stuck money, not information.** Indians pay 20% to recover insurance claims, ₹10,000–12,000 to recover IEPF shares and ₹20,000–50,000 for counselling at the moment a seat is at stake. They pay close to nothing for explanations. OpenAI launched ChatGPT Health to explain test results ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/07/openai-chatgpt-health-medical-records.html)), and free AI drafters have made notices and complaints a commodity. The working model is therefore a free, instant diagnosis followed by paid execution. Episodic outcomes are priced per outcome. UPI AutoPay subscriptions are kept for continuing value such as renewal watching or landlord compliance.

**The third pattern is that rules written in 2025–26 give agents something to enforce.** DGCA's refund rules took effect on 26 March 2026. They require credit-card refunds within seven days and make taxes refundable on every fare type ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/27/dgca-new-airline-ticket-refund-rules-2026/)). The Home Ministry's restoration and lien-grievance modules for cyber-fraud money have run since April 2026 ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2287039&reg=48&lang=2)). Credit-bureau errors must be fixed within 30 days, and the RBI Ombudsman can award ₹100 a day for delay ([Kenstone](https://kenstonecredit.com/library/rbi-ombudsman-credit-complaints)). Ministry of Education guidelines call for pro-rata coaching refunds within 10 days ([ET Education](https://education.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/government-policies/states-step-up-coaching-centre-regulation-after-centres-2024-guidelines/133187733)). Delhi now puts parents on the committees that set private-school fees ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/delhi/2025/Act4of2025DL.pdf)). Each clock becomes a deadline engine. Each missed deadline triggers an automatic escalation.

**The fourth pattern is that data regulators don't collect becomes the moat.** Nobody publishes denial reasons, hospital prices, deposit deductions, renovation rates or refusal reasons by consulate. Each case a consumer advocate handles adds to a dataset that general AI assistants cannot reproduce, and that dataset also drives SEO and press coverage.

Two further patterns decide which problems to pick. **Life events that span several institutions outlast single-institution fixes.** EPFO 3.0, DigiLocker's 72 crore users ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/digilocker-crosses-72-crore-users-umang-hits-11-crore)) and faceless transport-office services are shrinking simple pain. No government will build "a parent died", "we're moving house" or "my father is in hospital" as a single journey. **The NRI or remote child is the payer** in elder care, landlord compliance, tax notices, succession from abroad and study-abroad decisions. That makes "NRI tier" a premium layer across bundles rather than a separate company.

These patterns turn the shortlist into a handful of coherent bundles, all running on one shared engine.

| Bundle | What it combines | Compounding asset | Entry wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Your side of the health-insurance table" | Claim advocate, hospital-bill auditor and out-of-pocket estimator, policy X-ray and renewal watchdog | Denial-reason and bill database by insurer, TPA and hospital | Upload of a rejection letter or bill |

Under every bundle sits the same engine. It has five parts: document AI that reads policies, passbooks, certificates and letters; a library of statutory clocks; escalation ladders (insurer grievance officer to Bima Bharosa to Ombudsman; bank to RBI; airline to AirSewa to NCH to the e-Jagriti consumer court portal); WhatsApp intake with Hindi voice notes; and a case timeline that shows the consumer where the clock stands, the way a parcel tracker does. That timeline is where the experience can lead. Today's alternatives are an opaque agent, a lawyer's blog or a portal that marks the complaint "disposed". The engine is built once, and each new vertical is a playbook on top of it. The claim advocate is the best first vertical. If the legal check rules out paid representation before the Ombudsman, the stuck-money desk should go first instead, because success fees are already established there.

## A six-week, ₹1–1.5 lakh test can confirm or kill the leaders

**The test has one job: measure the two unknowns that sink solo consumer businesses, willingness to pay and human minutes per order, before any real build.** It runs in four stages.

**Week 0: legal checks.** Buy three short paid consultations. The first covers whether a paid third party may assist or represent policyholders before the Insurance Ombudsman under the 2017 rules, and whether a success fee is permitted. The second covers where the Advocates Act and Bar Council rules draw the line between self-help drafting software and legal practice. The third covers DPDP consent flows for health and identity documents. In the same week, confirm the current status of RBI's directions on deceased customers.

**Weeks 1–2: free diagnosis tools.** Build one free "front door" per opportunity, using WhatsApp, a web upload page, a language model and document extraction. At roughly ₹1 a page for extraction and ₹0.115 per WhatsApp utility message ([MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026)), the cost of serving a user stays small.

**Weeks 2–5: demand and price tests.** Run click-to-WhatsApp ads, which make the following 72 hours of messages free. Post in the relevant communities. Publish pages that target transactional queries such as "calculate", "check" and "file". These are less exposed than informational queries, where AI Overviews cut organic click-through by 61% ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-drive-drop-organic-paid-ctr-464212)). SiteGPT, an Indian AI product run by two brothers, gets 90% of its Google traffic from free tools ([superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/sitegpt)). Price is tested through UPI pre-commitments (refundable deposits), not survey answers. The founder fulfils the first 20–30 paid cases by hand behind AI-drafted outputs and times every minute.

**Week 6: decide** against the thresholds below. They are proposals, set against Meta's Indian cost-per-lead range of ₹150–2,000 ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/facebook-advertising-pricing/)) and the feasibility research's rule that first-order gross profit should be at least three times acquisition cost.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claim and bill advocate | People will pay to climb a free ladder; enough uploads contain money that a rule can recover | Upload rejection letter, policy and bill; get the IRDAI rule that applies, the recoverable amount and a draft grievance | ₹999 flat or a 10% success fee, pre-committed by UPI | Click-to-WhatsApp ads; pages such as "room rent proportionate deduction"; HR and RWA groups | 50+ qualified uploads at ≤₹300 each; ≥40% with a rule-backed recoverable amount; ≥20% accept the paid tier |
| Tenancy record | Tenants or landlords pay ₹499+; landlords co-sign | Free move-in condition report and deduction checker | ₹499 per tenancy for tenants; ₹1,500–5,000 a year for landlords | r/bangalorerentals; NCR and Bengaluru society groups; NRI owner groups | 200 reports created; ≥30% landlord co-signs; ≥5% pay |
| Stuck-money desk | Families pay ₹5,000–15,000 fixed fees; CAs and advisers refer clients | "Photo of an old share certificate: what it's worth today and its IEPF status"; "A parent died" checklist | Fixed packages of ₹4,999–14,999 plus an optional fee on found assets | 10 CA and wealth-adviser partners; Hindi YouTube Shorts; long-tail SEO | ≥5 paid cases; median recoverable amount ≥₹50,000 |
| Study-abroad copilot | Families pay a fee-only adviser when agents look free | Shortlist ROI and visa-risk audit; check of SOP and financial documents | ₹1,999 file review; ₹15,000–35,000 full package | r/Indians_StudyAbroad; YouTube; alumni referrals | ≥10 paid at ₹15,000 or more |
| NEET/JEE copilot | Self-serve beats ₹20,000+ human counsellors | Document-validity and deadline checker | ₹1,500–5,000 self-serve plan | Telegram and YouTube | Waitlist only now; run pre-sales in April–May 2027 |

The NEET/JEE test has to wait, because counselling runs from June (JoSAA registration opened on 2 June in 2026, per [Vedantu](https://www.vedantu.com/jee-main/josaa-counselling)). A waitlist now and pre-sales in spring is the cheap version. Across the other four tests, ad spend of about ₹15,000–25,000 each, inexpensive AI and messaging, and the legal consultations put the total at roughly ₹1–1.5 lakh. That figure is an estimate.

The decision rule is simple: build the opportunity with the best paid conversion multiplied by average ticket, divided by human minutes per case. Any case still taking more than about 30 minutes of founder time with no visible path below 10 points to a service business, however good the demand looks. The free tools from the losing tests should stay live, because they become search-driven funnels into the shared engine.

## Conclusion

The pain map and the opportunity map turn out to be inverted. The sector with the most pain, cyber fraud, gives a solo product the worst economics. The sector with the most complaints, e-commerce, already has the most capacity to handle them. The best ground lies in between: disputes where a known sum of money, a written rule and a free escalation path that people fail to use all exist together. The real shift that AI brings to India in 2026 is not drafting, which is now free. It is that the back office of a claims consultant, CA clerk or paralegal can run at a few rupees per case. That turns consumer advocacy from a manual 20%-commission service, which is how Insurance Samadhan reached only ₹6.2 crore, into a software business able to charge flat fees. The defensible part is the outcome data that regulators don't collect, not the model.

For this founder, that means one engine with many playbooks. Start where the evidence is hardest: health claims, or the stuck-money desk if the Ombudsman question goes the wrong way. Keep the tenancy record as the bridge to the NCR flat platform, since both sell trust at a moment when the counterparty holds the money. The biggest risk is not the build, which takes weeks. It is that no Indian solo founder has yet proven flat-fee advocacy with paid acquisition, and every verified solo success started with an audience it already owned. The free diagnosis tools are therefore more than a test. They are the first piece of the distribution this kind of business needs.
