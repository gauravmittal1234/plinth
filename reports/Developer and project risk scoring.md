# Follow the cash to rate NCR builders

A delivery-risk score for Delhi NCR should predict one observable outcome: whether a project gets its occupation or completion certificate (OC/CC) more than about 12 months after the date first promised to RERA, or stalls outright. Above brand, size or buyer sentiment, it should weight the gap between the buyer money a developer has collected and the construction it has completed. That gap, along with developer debt distress, unpaid authority land dues and a poor delivery record, showed up before almost every major NCR failure, usually one to three years before insolvency. India's credit rating agencies already use these ratios. But they publish no weights, and they answer a lender's question ("will debt be repaid?"), not a buyer's ("will my flat be delivered?"). The closest buyer-facing design is UP-RERA's 2020 grading framework, drafted with CRISIL; no evidence was found that it ever launched. MahaRERA halted its own grading in 2024 because the RERA Act gives it no power to grade. A credible v1 for Gurugram and Noida can run mostly on free official records: HARERA's quarterly cash, escrow and progress filings, UP RERA's default flags, DTCP's licence and OC register, IBBI insolvency notices, rating rationales and Sentinel-2 satellite imagery. On top of that it needs about ₹1–1.5 lakh a year of paid company and court data and six to ten weeks of engineering. The design that best balances accuracy, explainability and legal safety has three layers. The first is a set of hard "weak-link" gates for disqualifying facts. The second is a developer track-record index. The third is a project model fitted on HARERA and DTCP outcomes. The result should be shown as five plain-language bands, each with a "how many similar projects were late" frequency, a confidence state and up to four reasons, every one linked to a public record. The main legal exposure is defamation, not SEBI. It is manageable if every negative signal traces to a document, the methodology is published, developers get a right of reply, and the product is never called a "credit rating". Four other buyer risks merit v1 scores: ownership and paperwork, waterlogging, society health, and fair price. They should be kept as separate, named assessments. The hardest unresolved issue is commercial. The developer channel-partner commissions recommended in the earlier platform research put the platform on the payroll of the companies it would be scoring.

## Rating agencies measure lender safety; nobody yet scores delivery for buyers

### Indian credit raters publish the formulas but never the weights

India's four large credit rating agencies (CRAs) score developers on the same core factors. CRISIL's July 2024 criteria assess business, financial, project and management risk at the level of the micro-market. The criteria publish explicit ratios, of which the most useful for buyers is **adequacy of committed receivables: receivables from units already sold ÷ (remaining construction cost + debt servicing)**. CRISIL notes that the corollary is "the velocity of new sales required" to finish the job ([CRISIL criteria, Jul 2024](https://www.crisilratings.com/mnt/winshare/Ratings/SectorMethodology/MethodologyDocs/criteria/CRISILs%20Rating%20criteria%20for%20Real%20Estate%20Developers.pdf)). ICRA adds four more measures. **Collection efficiency** is the amount received divided by the amount demanded. **Years of unsold inventory** is unsold area divided by the past year's average sales. **Leverage** is debt ÷ (work in progress − customer advances), where a ratio above 100% can signal diversion of funds. Finally, ICRA compares physical and financial progress with the RERA completion date ([ICRA methodology](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRatingMethodologyFile/646~Real%20Estate)). CARE tracks construction progress "as per the RERA website", booking ratios and the share of bookings with registered agreements ([CARE methodology, Mar 2023](https://www.careratings.com/upload/pdf/get-rated/Rating%20Methodology%20-%20Real%20Estate%20Sector%20March%202023.pdf)). India Ratings warns that RERA's ring-fencing of project cash makes "the consolidated liquidity analysis alone… incomplete or misleading" ([India Ratings, Dec 2023](https://www.indiaratings.co.in/data//Uploads/CriteriaReport/2023/Rating%20Approach%20of%20Residential%20Real%20Estate%20Entities-Dec.pdf)).

**None of them publishes numeric weights.** CARE calls rating determination "a matter of experienced and holistic judgment by the Rating Committee". The only public glimpse of a CRA's internal weights is a warning. In November 2025 Infomerics settled with SEBI over alleged "manipulation and tampering with weights in the Rating Models thus inflating the final model ratings" ([Moneycontrol, Nov 2025](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/infomerics-valuation-and-rating-pays-rs-65-25-lakh-to-settle-case-with-sebi-13674757.html)). A buyer-facing score therefore has to set its own weights and calibrate them against outcomes. Its weight history should be version-controlled and auditable, because undocumented weight changes are a known failure mode.

These ratings also answer the wrong question for a buyer. CareEdge reaffirmed Signature Global at A+ in September 2026 mainly because homebuyer receivables of "over ₹19,600 crore" cover both construction and loan repayment ([The Financial World, Sep 2026](https://www.thefinancialworld.com/signature-global-retains-a-rating-plans-170-lakh-sq-ft-of-new-projects-in-gurugram-belt/)). That is the same cash that pays for the buyer's tower. Debt safety and delivery safety usually move together, but they split apart when a developer services debt from new launches while an older project stalls. Coverage is thin where risk is highest, because only developers that borrow from the debt markets carry ratings. Ratings also lag. CARE cut Jaiprakash Associates to D in July 2015, more than a year after construction at Jaypee's Wish Town had stalled around 2013–14 ([Livemint, Jul 2015](https://www.livemint.com/Money/7sqngwbKFhkYYigdP51swJ/Jaiprakash-Associates-debt-rating-cut-to-default.html); [Wikipedia: Jaypee Infratech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaypee_Infratech)). In the IL&FS case, SEBI fined ICRA, CARE and India Ratings for "excessive reliance on the submissions made by the IL&FS management". It later raised the penalties to ₹1 crore each, saying earlier downgrades would have "forewarned the investors" ([The Hindu, Dec 2019](https://www.thehindu.com/business/ilfs-sebi-fines-3-rating-agencies-25-lakh-each/article30405219.ece); [Free Press Journal, Sep 2020](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/ilfs-case-sebi-raises-penalty-to-rs-1-crore-each-on-icra-care)).

### CREST, UP-RERA and MahaRERA show the score can be built, but not by a regulator

India has had buyer-facing gradings since 2010. CRISIL's CREST grades a project from 0 to 7 stars on the "likelihood" of delivery "with the least delay". The scale is benchmarked against other projects in the same city, ignores amenities, and follows a 4–5 week assessment with a site visit. The developer pays an annual surveillance fee until the OC is issued, cannot withdraw the grade before then, and may advertise it ([Crisil CREST FAQs](https://www.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/sme_ratings/star-rating-brochure/crest-faqs.pdf)). CRISIL's developer grading runs from DA1+ to DA5. It explicitly covers "likelihood of time and cost overruns" and the "ability to transfer a clear title", and it stays under surveillance only until the developer asks for it to be withdrawn ([Crisil developer grading criteria, Jul 2018](https://intelligence.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil/our-businesses/crest/national-developer-rating/crest-criteria-developer-grading.pdf)). Both are opt-in products paid for by the developer.

**The UP-RERA × CRISIL framework is the most directly reusable precedent for NCR.** UP-RERA invited comments on it on 22 April 2020 ([TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/8778/uttar-pradesh-rera-issues-grading-framework-for-real-estate-projects-a/)). It grades promoters from I to V and projects from 1 to 5 stars. Its blocks cover RERA compliance (QPR uploads, escrow adherence, end-date extensions), legal record (OC/CC as a share of completed projects, NCLT history, land issues), delivery record ("average delay in months in physical possession w.r.t. … RERA end date"), financial quality (share of units sold, debt-to-equity, advances received ÷ total cost) and customer complaints. Its most useful design choice is a hygiene layer on top of the additive score. **"Deflators"** pull scores down for unpaid authority dues, defaulter-list status, or many court cases and recovery certificates (RCs). **"Weak-link" caps** hold a project down when it is a defaulter, its title is unclear or its land is encumbered. The framework is also candid about what public data cannot supply. Its data column marks expected time overrun, OC delay, title clarity, area delivered and past delays as "Not available". It marks RERA compliance, share of units sold, gearing, advances and complaints as available. CRISIL warned of "significant reputational and legal risk". It recommended that grades come from "an independent agency rather than by UP-RERA under its own brand", backed by manual validation, site visits triggered by red flags, and a seven-working-day appeal window ([UP-RERA CRISIL Grading Framework](https://up-rera.in/frm_Ifram_for_PDf.aspx?Param=111UP_RERA_CRISIL_Grading_Framework.pdf)). No evidence was found that UP-RERA ever published grades under it.

MahaRERA went further and then retreated. Its Circular 46/2023 created a "Grading Matrix" with four sections (project overview, technical, financial and legal), generated automatically from developer filings every six months, with no published weights or letter scale ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/understanding-mahareras-new-grading-matrix-for-real-estate.html)). It was halted in late 2024 on the ground that "the Act does not authorize a regulatory body to grade projects", with CREDAI-MCHI opposed. Sources date the halt to October or November ([PropNewsTime, Nov 2024](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTIxNDc=/maharera-halts-real-estate-project-grading-initiative-due-to-legal-constraints); [PropNewsTime, Oct 2024](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTE0NTU=/maharera-halts-real-estate-project-grading-initiative-due-to-regulatory-constraints)). Section 32(f) of the RERA Act only asks authorities to "encourage grading of projects… including grading of promoters" ([TaxGuru on s.32](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/grading-promoters-rera-2016.html)). Homebuyers still complain that "there is no mechanism to verify a promoter's track record before a new project registration" ([Hindustan Times, Oct 2025](https://www.hindustantimes.com/real-estate/homebuyers-body-seeks-changes-in-rera-law-calls-for-rules-to-verify-developers-track-record-before-project-approvals-101761492232887.html)). The gap is real, and the regulators' own adviser pointed to an independent issuer to fill it. The flip side is that a private score can never claim regulatory endorsement.

### Abroad, developer-paid stars inflate while bright-line balance-sheet tests travel well

The closest foreign analogue is New South Wales' **iCIRT**, an Equifax-run rating of 0 to 5 stars across six "Cs": capability, conduct, character, capacity, capital and counterparties. Ratings are valid for 12 months, and only firms with three stars or more appear on the public register ([NSW Building Commission](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/building-commission/icirt)). The rated firm pays A$3,050 for a standard report or A$8,250 for a detailed one ([iCIRT order page](https://www.icirt.com/order.html)). Its weaknesses are instructive. It rates "the business, not the individual building… It does not inspect the apartment" ([Canberra Times, Sep 2026](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9356255/deicorp-icirt-rating-how-nsw-checks-reveal-developer-record/)). Its opt-in design has let weak firms stay out, and sales staff have claimed ratings that did not exist ([Flat Chat, Nov 2023](https://www.flatchat.com.au/developers-fake-icrt-ratings/)).

The UK's HBF customer-satisfaction stars show how survey ratings inflate. Advocacy data from 2019 recorded 42 of 48 rated builders on five stars while 99% of buyers reported defects ([new-home-blog, 2019](http://www.new-home-blog.co.uk/hbf-satisfaction-survey-2019-shows-number-of-defects-still-high/)). HBF has since moved to a composite of surveys taken eight weeks and nine months after move-in, with five stars requiring a score of at least 4.15 ([HBF rules 2025](https://www.hbf.co.uk/documents/14404/HBF_-_CSS_and_HBF_Star_Rating_rules_2025.pdf)). Singapore's CONQUAS does the opposite. An independent assessor scores structural, architectural and M&E work against fixed weights (20/70/10 for private housing) and does not re-score rectified work ([CONQUAS factsheet](https://www.nas.gov.sg/archivesonline/data/pdfdoc/20130523003/factsheet_-_conquas.pdf)). Dubai has no official developer rating. It protects buyers through structure instead: 100% escrow, with withdrawals certified by an engineer at each milestone ([Kayrouz & Associates, Mar 2026](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/rera-developer-obligations-off-plan-dubai)).

China supplies the bright lines. The "three red lines" require liabilities excluding advances below 70% of assets, net gearing below 100% and cash at least equal to short-term debt. Breaching more lines meant tighter caps on debt growth, ranging from 15% a year for firms meeting all three down to 0% for firms breaching all three ([CKGSB](https://english.ckgsb.edu.cn/knowledge/professor_analysis/series-chinas-real-estate-problem-1-the-three-red-lines/); [SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/101253537-three-red-lines-and-the-red-orange-yellow-and-green-in-the-performance-report)). China also supplies a warning. In a study of 192 listed developers, entering a top sales ranking was followed by **debt-to-capital ratios 1.88–3.66 points higher**, because rank worked as a credit signal that fuelled over-borrowing. Country Garden, a perennial top-ranked developer, later defaulted ([Humanities & Social Sciences Communications, Jun 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05154-7)). S&P, unlike Indian CRAs, publishes sub-factor weights for homebuilders and debt/(debt+equity) bands, from under 25% ("minimal") to over 60% ("highly leveraged") ([S&P Key Credit Factors via Maalot](https://www.maalot.co.il/Publications/MT20190514121552.PDF)).

### Consumer credit scoring supplies the explanation and validation playbook

Consumer scores pair a simple public story with a fitted model. FICO runs from 300 to 850 and publishes category weights of 35/30/15/10/10, adding that importance "may vary from one person to another" ([myFICO](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score)). CIBIL runs from 300 to 900 ([Axis Bank](https://www.axis.bank.in/blogs/credit-score/cibil-score-ranges)). Its factor weights circulate only on third-party blogs.

US Regulation B sets the reason-code standard. Giving more than four reasons is "not likely to be helpful", and the reasons must "relate to and accurately describe the factors actually considered or scored". One accepted way to pick them is to rank the factors where the applicant fell furthest below the average of applicants who only just passed ([CFPB Reg B interpretation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/interp-9/)). Complex algorithms get no exemption from naming specific reasons ([Federal Register, CFPB Circular 2022-03](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/06/14/2022-12729/consumer-financial-protection-circular-2022-03-adverse-action-notification-requirements-in)).

The standard build groups each factor into weight-of-evidence bins, fits a logistic regression and scales the output to points, with a fixed number of points doubling the odds ([MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/blogs/credit-scoring-logistic-regression/)). The Basel Committee's validation guidance covers two tests. Discrimination asks whether the score ranks good and bad cases correctly (measured by AUC, and the accuracy ratio = 2·AUC − 1). Calibration asks whether predicted probabilities match outcomes (binomial, Hosmer–Lemeshow and traffic-light tests). The guidance also warns that **portfolios with few defaults are hard to validate** ([BIS BCBS Working Paper 14](https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs_wp14.pdf)). Stalled NCR projects are exactly that kind of portfolio.

## NCR stalls follow one script, visible one to three years ahead

### NCR holds two-thirds of India's fully stalled flats, even after RERA

NCR is the national outlier. ANAROCK counted about **1.74 lakh completely stalled units across India's top seven cities in 2021, of which NCR held 1,13,860 (66%)** ([Business Standard, Aug 2021](https://www.business-standard.com/article/current-affairs/174-000-flats-completely-stalled-in-7-cities-ncr-s-share-at-66-report-121080100437_1.html)). It also held about 52% of all stalled-or-delayed stock ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/construction-of-6-lakh-housing-units-valued-at-over-rs-5-lakh-cr-facing-delay-in-india-anarock-report)). Before RERA, only 23% of NCR projects launched before 2008 had been completed by January 2012, against 61% in Mumbai ([PropEquity via PRLog](https://www.prlog.org/11893301-45-per-cent-of-all-projects-delayed-in-ncr-mmr-and-bmr-propequity-research.html)).

RERA helped without closing the gap. Of RERA-registered projects launched between July 2017 and December 2018, **86% were complete across the top seven cities but only 74% in NCR**, tied with Hyderabad for the lowest rate ([Moneylife/ANAROCK, Sep 2023](https://www.moneylife.in/article/86-percentage-projects-launched-postrera-completed-in-top-7-cities-report/71921.html)). NCR projects still take 4.7 years from launch to completion for small projects and 5.4 years for large ones ([The Realty Today/ANAROCK, Jul 2024](https://therealtytoday.com/news/builders-cut-project-completion-time-by-20-in-top-7-cities-anarock-report/)).

The most rigorous estimate comes from Agarwal, Fan, Ghosh and co-authors, using 13,357 projects in 12 cities from 2010 to 2020. They found a **mean delay of 14.41 months**. RERA registration cut delays by about 5.09 months, while delays at unregistered projects rose by about 3.67 months. Delays fell in affordable housing and rose in luxury ([ABFER 2024](https://abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2024/annual-conference/papers-household/AC24P7045-Does-Regulating-Developers-Democratize-Credit-and-Homeownership.pdf)). The legacy stock remains. PropEquity counted about 2,000 stalled projects with 5.08 lakh units nationally in August 2024 ([Business Standard, Aug 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/nearly-2-000-housing-projects-stalled-across-42-cities-propequity-124081500404_1.html)). A month later it counted 167 stalled projects (74,645 units) in Greater Noida and 103 (41,438 units) in Noida ([Business Standard, Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/housing-crisis-stalled-projects-surge-in-up-greater-noida-and-noida-lead-124090900283_1.html)).

Two things follow for model design. First, the base rate must be calibrated to NCR, not to national averages, or every NCR project will look riskier than its band claims. Second, blanket regulator relief has to be removed from the delay labels. UP granted a COVID "zero period" from 1 April 2020 to 31 March 2022 ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/uttar-pradesh/up-govt-approves-amitabh-kant-committee-proposals-likely-to-provide-relief-to-noida-homebuyers-2817386)). In 2026, MoHUA advised a four-month force-majeure extension because of the West Asia conflict, which UP RERA applied to projects due between 28 February and 31 October 2026 ([Realty n More](https://realtynmore.com/up-rera-grants-four-month-extension-to-real-estate/); [Moneycontrol, Aug 2026](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/personal-finance/what-rera-s-4-month-extension-for-real-estate-projects-means-for-homebuyers-and-developers-13992019.html)). A project-specific Section 6 extension is a risk signal. A blanket extension is not.

### Cash collected ahead of concrete predicts stalls better than anything else

Almost every NCR stall runs the same way. The developer collects most of the price early through construction-linked, flexi or subvention plans, then sends the money to new land, new launches, sister companies or other debt. Construction falls behind collections, a bank, NBFC or NCD trustee goes unpaid, and RERA recovery certificates and an NCLT petition follow. The gap is visible in the cases. Gurugram's Ocean Seven Buildtech had **completed about 62% of the work after collecting about 95% of the money** ([ThePrint, Sep 2025](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/gurugram-affordable-housing-isnt-affordable-for-builders-or-buyers/2746570/)). HARERA revoked all five of Mahira Homes' affordable projects in March 2024 after finding that buyer deposits had been "unlawfully diverted" ([The Tribune, Mar 2024](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/rera-revokes-all-5-affordable-housing-projects-of-mahira-homes-in-gurugram-602242)). Ajnara Ambrosia stopped in 2017 after most of its roughly 1,100 buyers had paid nearly 90% ([99acres](https://www.99acres.com/articles/ajnara-india-dragged-into-insolvency-by-homebuyers-nid.html)). The Amitabh Kant committee named "lack of financial viability" as the primary cause of stress ([Vajiram summary](https://vajiramandravi.com/upsc-daily-current-affairs/mains-articles/amitabh-kant-committee-report-on-real-estate-projects/)). PropEquity blames diversion "to buy new land or retire other loans" ([Business Standard, Aug 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/nearly-2-000-housing-projects-stalled-across-42-cities-propequity-124081500404_1.html)). The subvention schemes made it worse: banks released 60–70% of loan amounts to builders without matching construction. By July 2025 the CBI had registered 22 cases and searched 47 locations across NCR ([ETV Bharat, Apr 2025](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/sc-orders-cbi-probe-on-claims-of-fraud-on-homebuyers-enn25042905923); [News on AIR, Jul 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/cbi-crackdowns-on-builders-in-delhi-ncr-for-defrauding-homebuyers)).

Debt distress is the next-strongest signal, and it comes with ready-made probabilities. CRISIL's FY16–FY26 default study gives **three-year cumulative default rates of 1.97% for BBB, 9.70% for BB, 27.28% for B and 56.05% for C**, against 0.14% for AA. Average time to default is 21 months for B-rated issuers. Real estate development is one of six industries that together account for almost half of CRISIL's 3,842 defaults ([CRISIL default study FY2026](https://www.crisilratings.com/content/dam/crisil/our-analysis/publications/default-study/crisil-ratings-annual-default-and-ratings-transition-study-fy-2026.pdf)). Non-bank creditors often start the process. Vatika was admitted to insolvency over an NCD default of about ₹274 crore ([ghar.tv](https://www.ghar.tv/blog/gurugram-developer-vatika-faces-bankruptcy-proceedings-after-failing-to-repay-274-crore-in-dues/artid5328)). Mahagun faced a Section 7 petition over about ₹256 crore before settling in February 2026 ([Business Standard, Feb 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/nclt-clears-withdrawal-of-insolvency-proceedings-against-mahagun-126022100512_1.html)).

Noida's leasehold model adds a predictor peculiar to NCR: **unpaid land dues to the development authority**. These block both completion and registry. Developers in Gautam Buddh Nagar owed about **₹44,700 crore** in March 2024 ([Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/infrastructure/noida-greater-noida-builders-clear-dues-to-revive-stalled-housing-projects-under-up-governments-relief-package)). Compliance with the Kant relief package stayed patchy. A secondary report put Noida in April 2026 at 57 projects owing about ₹28,000 crore, with only 22 builders having paid the required 25% and eight having paid nothing ([the420.in](https://the420.in/noida-builders-dues-auction-unsold-properties-amitabh-kant-panel/)). Noida sealed Lotus Panache towers over ₹703 crore of dues ([PropTrust](https://proptrust.co/noida-seals-lotus-panache-towers-over-%E2%82%B9703-crore-dues/)).

Several weaker signals amplify the risk. **Running too many projects at once** is one: in 2020 HARERA stopped licensing builders with delayed projects, saying "a builder cannot leave one project midway and start another one to siphon off or divert funds" ([The Tribune, Oct 2020](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/no-new-licence-till-builder-finishes-earlier-projects-harera-160174)). A **2010–2014 launch year** is another, since that boom cohort was followed by a 76.8% fall in Gurugram's active developer count between 2011-12 and 2017-18 ([Business Standard, Jul 2019](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/number-of-real-estate-developers-in-top-9-cities-halved-after-note-ban-119070900010_1.html)). **Fixed-price affordable housing** is a third: about 35 of roughly 60 Gurugram projects under the Haryana affordable housing policy are delayed ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/gurugram-affordable-housing-isnt-affordable-for-builders-or-buyers/2746570/)). **Distress nearby** matters too. In China, a developer default cut sell-through at neighbouring projects within 5 km by about 5 percentage points ([Zhang, JUE Insight, 2026](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6312634)). Litigation and environmental orders cause long delays that projects usually survive. The NGT's 2013 Okhla orders froze 49 projects and about 70,000 buyers until the eco-sensitive zone was notified ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/okhla-bird-sanctuary-case-respite-likely-for-noida-homebuyers-developers-45767)).

| 2 | 2010–2014 or pre-RERA launch; not RERA-registered | Strong: ANAROCK cohorts; Agarwal et al. | Delay or stall |
| 2 | Very large multi-tower projects; many concurrent projects | Moderate | Delay |
| 2 | Fixed-price affordable or luxury segment | Moderate | Delay |
| 2 | Title, acquisition or environmental litigation | Moderate | Long delay; total loss rare |
| 3 | GRAP construction bans, COVID, 2026 supply shocks | Moderate; affects everyone | Months of delay, usually extended for all |

Positive signals are mostly the mirror image. Investment-grade ratings carry near-zero default rates. Listed and leading developers doubled their share of top-seven-city sales from 17% to 34% ([ANAROCK/NAREDCO 2024](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/Real_Estate_Unboxed_The_Modi_Effect_5ef38a29b7.pdf)). National developers' share of NCR supply rose from 3% to 13% between 2022 and 2025 ([ConstructionXperts/ANAROCK](https://constructionxperts.co.in/from-3-to-13-supply-share-national-developers-quadruple-down-on-ncr/)). Signature Global handed over its Sunrise affordable project six months early ([Realty n More](https://realtynmore.com/signature-global-delivers-sunrise-before-time/)). Listing on a stock exchange protects but does not suffice: Unitech and Jaypee Infratech were both listed.

**No systematic, independent on-time league table for NCR developers exists.** The score will have to build one from OC dates and original RERA completion dates. The machine-learning literature does not fill the gap either. Delay classifiers reporting 90%+ accuracy were trained on questionnaires or a few dozen projects. Examples are a random-forest model at 91.67% ([Yaseen et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/su12041514)) and a deep-learning hybrid trained on 68 Indian projects ([Sahu et al., 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15623599.2026.2619678)). None uses the financial and regulatory signals that drive NCR stalls. No published model predicts Indian project delay from RERA quarterly progress reports (QPRs), MCA filings or RERA orders. That gap is the opportunity.

The signals tend to appear in a consistent order. Losses or a downgrade to BB, D or "issuer not cooperating" come first. Missed payments to fixed-deposit holders, NCD trustees, banks or LIC follow. Then construction falls behind collections, buyers protest and RERA complaints pile up. RERA refund orders are ignored and recovery certificates stay unexecuted. Authorities seal towers or refuse to revalidate building plans, and petitions spread to other projects in the same group. Enforcement agencies (ED, CBI, EOW) arrive late. Their action confirms a stall rather than predicting it. A practical watchlist rule is **any two Tier-1 signals within 24 months**, which would have given one to three years of warning in most of these cases. Because Raheja, Ansal and Unitech stayed distressed for a decade or more, developer history should fade slowly in the score. Gurugram's current list of unexecuted RCs is a ready-made developer-level flag. It covers **₹446 crore across 71 firms**, led by Ansal (₹91 crore), Raheja (₹90 crore) and Vatika (₹80 crore), with many orders pending for more than four years ([The Financial World, Sep 2026](https://www.thefinancialworld.com/ansal-raheja-vatika-top-%E2%82%B9446-cr-gurugram-hrera-defaulters/)).

### Rescue arrives 5–15 years late and needs its own model

Buyer remedies are slow and partial. UP RERA had recovered only about **20% of the value of its recovery certificates** by September 2023, ₹630.70 crore out of ₹3,105 crore ([PropNewsTime, Sep 2023](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MjY2MQ==/up-rera-provides-relief-to-homebuyers-with-rs-3-105-crore-in-recovery-certificates)). Resolved insolvencies (CIRPs) took 597 days on average to March 2025 ([IBBI newsletter](https://ibbi.gov.in/uploads/whatsnew/912e97d4d9f96651386541fb7059203b.pdf)). Homebuyers do relatively well in real-estate resolution plans: Jaypee's allottees received about 75% of their claims, largely as flats rather than cash ([ANAROCK–Khaitan, Sep 2023](https://websitemedia.anarock.com/media/Khaitan_ANAROCK_IBC_Report_Sep_2023_ab5b7e2fcc.pdf)). Rescue funds and state completion have helped a minority. SWAMIH has delivered about 61,000 homes ([The Tribune, Jan 2026](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/swamih-fund-completes-corpus-commitment-delivers-over-61000-homes-to-residents/)), and NBCC has completed about 25,000 Amrapali flats ([ThePrint, Feb 2025](https://theprint.in/india/nbcc-completes-25000-flats-of-amrapali-group-sells-6686-units-of-defaulter-homebuyers-sc-told/2517917/)). Government-managed Unitech, by contrast, handed over only 1,007 homes between January 2020 and July 2026 ([insolvencytracker, Aug 2026](https://insolvencytracker.in/2026/08/16/unitech-homebuyers-seek-urgent-appointment-of-cmd-as-projects-remain-stalled/)). The Supreme Court restored Earth Infrastructures' resolution plans in May 2026, about 12 years after the developer abandoned its sites ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/litigation/over-4200-homebuyers-get-relief-as-supreme-court-restores-insolvency-plans-for-earth-infras-projects)).

For a buyer, "rescued" still means a severe delay. Rescue probability therefore belongs in a separate module shown only for projects already stalled. It should never be blended into the main band in a way that makes a stalled project look less risky.

## Free public records can feed a v1 for about ₹1.5 lakh a year

### HARERA Gurugram publishes quarterly cash, escrow and progress for every project

HARERA Gurugram is the richest open source in NCR, and arguably in India. On 18 October 2026 its registered-projects page (parsed by the researcher) listed **1,083 registrations**. Of these, 520 were valid on or after that date, 200 were flagged "Lapsed Project" and 158 were flagged "QPR not Uploaded". The list contained 644 distinct builder-name strings, with variants such as "VATIKA LIMITED" and "VATIKA LTD" ([HARERA registered projects](https://haryanarera.gov.in/admincontrol/registered_projects/2)).

Each project's registration form (Form A-H) gives the promoter's corporate ID (CIN) and directors, land and licence details, a cost split into land, infrastructure and other, units booked and unsold, the amount received, loans raised, the escrow bank account and approvals with dates. It also lists the promoter's projects from the previous five years, each with initial and likely completion dates and any pending litigation ([HARERA Form A-H](https://haryanarera.gov.in/view_project/project_preview_open/1444)). That list is a ready-made track-record input. The **quarterly progress report** covers every quarter from Q1-2021 to Q3-2026. Each one discloses collections from buyers, bank funding and promoter contribution, along with land, construction and infrastructure outflows. It also shows escrow deposits, withdrawals and balances, units sold in the quarter, and EDC (external development charges) due versus paid. Finally, it records when the architect's, engineer's and chartered accountant's certificates were uploaded. The CA certificate covers money withdrawn from escrow, including withdrawals for non-project purposes ([HARERA QPR endpoint](https://haryanarera.gov.in/view_project/quaterly_schedule_preview_open)). The regulations add tower-by-tower physical progress and legal cases, and declare the QPR "open for public viewing" ([HARERA QPR Regulations 2018](https://haryanarera.gov.in/regulations/Quarterly_Progress_ReportGURUGRAMRegulations2018.pdf)). A JSON endpoint lists every extension, OC, part-completion and completion certificate, cancellation order and sale ban, each with a date ([HARERA corrigendum endpoint](https://haryanarera.gov.in/assistancecontrol/view_corrigendums_popup)). These are the outcome labels a model needs, and they can be cross-checked against DTCP's register. The judgments index holds **17,938 orders** from April 2018 to October 2026, including 4,647 decided in 2025 alone. Emaar MGF (841), Vatika (553 plus 406 under a variant name) and BPTP (360 plus 226) top the respondent list ([HARERA judgements](https://haryanarera.gov.in/admincontrol/judgements/2)).

The data is self-reported and messy. The same Q2-2026 QPR showed **negative collections of −572.98 lakh** and a quarter "ending on 31/06/2026" ([HARERA QPR endpoint](https://haryanarera.gov.in/view_project/quaterly_schedule_preview_open)). A missing or impossible filing should be treated as a signal in its own right.

### UP RERA and the Noida authorities leave the biggest gaps where risk is highest

UP RERA lists **3,822 projects** statewide: 971 in Gautam Buddh Nagar and 482 in Ghaziabad. Of these, 237 NCR projects carry a "(Default on QPR)" tag, and 233 NCR residential projects (142 in GB Nagar) have end dates on or after 18 October 2026 ([UP RERA registered projects](https://www.up-rera.in/projects), parsed by the researcher). Project pages show complaint counts per project and per promoter, the promoter's project count, revised end dates with the extension order number, all three RERA bank accounts, and the sanctioning authority: Noida, Greater Noida, YEIDA or GDA ([UP RERA project view](https://www.up-rera.in/viewprojects)). The de-registered list has 72 projects, 63 of them in GB Nagar and dominated by Amrapali entities ([UP RERA de-registered list](https://www.up-rera.in/DefaulterList)). The gaps are serious, though. The "Completed Projects" list returned only 11 rows statewide. Complaint and RC detail sit behind image CAPTCHAs ([UP RERA complaint status](https://www.up-rera.in/Frm_All_Complaint_Status_Page.aspx)). QPR content loads dynamically and was not parsed. Data quality should improve: from the quarter ending 30 September 2026, architects, engineers and CAs must upload digitally signed certificates themselves ([Grihik](https://www.grihik.com/news/real-estate-news/up-rera-new-rules)).

Noida Authority's group-housing dashboard still reads "Updated as on 16/06/2023" ([Noida PIMS](https://pims.mynoida.org.in/GroupHousing/GroupHousingProject)). No official, machine-readable list of builder dues was found for Noida, Greater Noida or YEIDA. That is a serious gap, because authority dues are a Tier-1 predictor in exactly these markets. In v1 they will have to be compiled by hand from press releases and RTI requests.

Delhi RERA could not be reached and covers few projects ([HomeFirst guide](https://homefirstindia.com/blog/article/delhi-rera)). MoHUA's Unified RERA Portal claimed 1,51,113 projects at launch in September 2025 ([News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/union-minister-manohar-lal-launches-unified-rera-portal-to-boost-transparency-in-real-estate)), but its certificate had expired when checked. Gurugram's DTCP licence register fills Haryana's remaining gaps. It has about 3,163 rows with no CAPTCHA and exports to Excel. It shows licence validity, cancellation status, and links to building-plan approval (BR-III), OC permission (BR-VII) and completion certificate (LC-IX) ([DTCP licence details](https://tcpharyana.gov.in/webadmin/license/licensedetails)).

### Company, insolvency, court and rating data are cheap to add

**Company data (MCA).** Basic company records are free behind a CAPTCHA, and filed documents cost ₹100 per company ([LegalSuvidha, Jul 2026](https://legalsuvidha.com/blog/mca-company-search-understanding-the-process)). Tofler sells structured access at **₹15,000 or ₹28,000 plus GST a year** for 40 or 100 company unlocks, plus ₹349 per extra credit ([Tofler pricing](https://www.tofler.in/price-and-plans)). By the researcher's arithmetic, about 300 promoter entities and SPVs would cost roughly ₹30,000 directly from MCA or about ₹98,000 through Tofler. The filings yield charges (secured loans) against project land, net worth and debt, customer advances, related-party loans, auditor qualifications, and director links to failed companies.

**Insolvency (IBBI).** IBBI's 14,952 public announcements export freely ([IBBI public announcements](https://ibbi.gov.in/en/public-announcement)). Its newsletter counts 571 CIRPs admitted in the "real estate activities" sub-sector, with 176 still ongoing ([IBBI newsletter Excel, Apr–Jun 2026](https://ibbi.gov.in/uploads/publication/18054eb85bc0e7388537b05326192072.xlsx)).

**Courts.** NCLT, eCourts and consumer-commission searches all require CAPTCHAs ([NCLT party search](https://nclt.gov.in/party-name-wise); [eCourts](https://services.ecourts.gov.in/ecourtindia_v6/?p=casestatus/index)). The licensed route is eCourtsIndia's API, at ₹0.20–0.60 per party search and ₹0.50–1.50 per case detail. That works out to roughly ₹6,000–10,000 per full refresh for 300 entities ([eCourtsIndia API pricing](https://ecourtsindia.com/api/pricing)).

**Ratings.** ICRA's rationale feed is free and flags "issuer non-cooperating" in its titles. Nine of 23 rationales in one snapshot carried the flag ([ICRA rating rationales](https://www.icra.in/Rating/AllRatingRationales)).

**Paid project data.** PropEquity tracks more than 182,000 projects ([PropEquity](https://www.propequity.in/)). Liases Foras sells "risk rating" for projects and developers ([Liases Foras](https://www.liasesforas.com/)). Neither publishes prices. Liases Foras is the closest thing to a buy-instead-of-build option and deserves a quote and a sample of its output before engineering starts.

### Satellites confirm activity, not floor counts

Sentinel-2 imagery is free at 10 m resolution, with a five-day revisit ([Copernicus SentiWiki](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s2-mission)). It is enough to detect site clearing and new building mass, but not floors. The best published height models err by about **3.7 m** ([arXiv 2307.01378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01378)). Sub-metre archive images start at about $15 and new captures at about $200 ([SkyFi pricing](https://skyfi.com/en/pricing)). India's own Cartosat imagery can be ordered through Bhoonidhi ([NRSC Bhoonidhi](https://bhoonidhi.nrsc.gov.in/bhoonidhi/home.html)). Google Earth imagery cannot be used commercially or to extract data ([Google Geo Guidelines](https://about.google/brand-resource-center/products-and-services/geo-guidelines/)). The right v1 feature is a monthly check that flags "no visible activity for N months while the QPR claims progress". Paid sub-metre images should be bought only to spot-check high-risk or disputed projects.

### Portal terms, not technology, set the legal limits on collection

Government data in India is not automatically open-licensed. **HARERA** allows reproduction "after taking proper permission by sending a mail", on condition that the material is not used "in a derogatory manner or in a misleading context" ([HARERA Terms of Use](https://haryanarera.gov.in/login/terms_of_use)). That condition is awkward for a risk product. **UP RERA** allows downloads, but "any other proposed use" needs its approval, and its policy even requires prior permission to link to its pages ([UP RERA policies](https://www.up-rera.in/DisclaimerandPolicies)). The government's open-data licence (GODL) covers only data released under the national data-sharing policy, and it excludes personal information ([GODL Gazette](https://data.gov.in/sites/default/files/Gazette_Notification_OGDL.pdf)).

Three statutes cut in different directions. Section 52(1)(q)(iv) of the Copyright Act exempts reproduction of any "judgment or order of a court, Tribunal or other judicial authority", which plausibly covers RERA orders ([Indian Kanoon, s.52](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1013176/)). IT Act s.43 penalises downloading or extracting data "without permission" ([Indian Kanoon, s.43](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/39800/)). The Delhi High Court has enjoined scraping of a private database in *OLX v. Padawan* ([IKIGAI Law](https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/263/legality-of-data-scraping-in-india)). The DPDP Act's carve-out for data published under a legal obligation is s.3(c)(ii) ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). It plausibly covers MCA and RERA disclosures, but commentators read it narrowly where data is scraped or aggregated ([India Briefing, Sep 2026](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html)). HARERA forms also show directors' residential addresses and mobile numbers.

The safe posture follows from this. Write to HARERA and UP RERA for permission before launch. Never bypass a CAPTCHA; use licensed aggregators instead. Limit request rates and record the source (URL, timestamp, hash) of every data point. Keep raw documents internal and publish only derived scores, each linked to the official page. Discard personal phone numbers and addresses as soon as data is collected.

## Four other buyer risks deserve v1 scores; the rest should stay as facts

The developer score answers only one buyer question. Four other risks combine severe buyer pain with enough data for a v1 in Gurugram and Noida.

**Transferability and clean ownership** is the most severe. About 75,000 Noida and Greater Noida flats were unregistered in December 2024, and such flats "cannot be sold or mortgaged" ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/the-fineprint/noida-greater-noida-home-buyers-struggle-for-registry-big-players-remain-shy/2406593/)). A residents' federation was still citing the same figure in 2026 ([Apartment Times](https://apartmenttimes.in/thousands-await-flat-registries-in-noida-nhrf-steps-in-with-city-wide-initiative/)). Gurugram has broken the assumption that an OC is a green light. Since November 2022, DTCP has let registered architects self-certify OCs. In April 2026 a flying squad inspected 22 buildings holding OCs and found **14 still under construction**. About 2,000 OCs are now under audit (another outlet says 1,500) ([ThePrint, Jul 2026](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/gurugrams-oc-scam-how-an-official-certificate-leaves-buyers-stuck-in-unfinished-homes/3001654/); [Ghar.tv](https://www.ghar.tv/blog/gurugrams-oc-scam-exposed-1500-property-clearances-under-scanner-for-flouting-basic-structural-norms/artid6187)). Much of this is knowable at project level: authority dues and registry permission, OC/CC dates and audit status, lease type and Noida's transfer charge ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/real-estate/transfer-memorandum-the-hidden-tax-on-resale-flats-in-noida)). The right format is not a number. It is a UK-style checklist of "checks done / issues found / not checked", with a "what to do next" action for each.

**Waterlogging** is the strongest location risk, and also the one where official data misleads most. Gurugram's development authority (GMDA) says its vulnerable road spots fell from about 90 to six by July 2026 ([The Tribune, Jul 2026](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/gurugram/narsinghpur-beats-decade-long-waterlogging-gurugram-hotspots-drop-from-7-to-6/)). Yet on 1 September 2025 basement switchgear flooded in societies around Golf Course Road and Cyber City. A single non-official source reports a 51-hour outage at Signature Global's Solera ([FloodWatch Gurgaon](https://floodwatchgurgaon.in/blog/gurgaon-sept-1-2025-flood/)). The score should rest on observed evidence first: dated, geocoded incidents and resident reports. Modelled exposure from terrain and drain status should come second.

**Society health and running costs** matters for ready and resale flats. Maintenance charges at The Westerlies in Sector 108 rose about 90% in two years ([Business Standard, Oct 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/maintenance-fee-rises-90-in-gurugram-township-what-to-check-before-buying-126100500295_1.html)). Gurugram owners are fighting builders for control of their resident associations ([ThePrint, Aug 2026](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/own-a-flat-but-have-no-say-gurugram-residents-are-fighting-for-rwa-membership/3017820/)). Noida had about 80,000 unregistered lifts when UP's lift law took effect ([99acres](https://www.99acres.com/articles/lift-registration-under-up-lift-act.html)). This score also builds a moat: its best inputs (bills, outage logs, association status) come from residents, so coverage grows with users.

**Fair price** uses the AVM design from the earlier research: a range, not a point estimate, built on licensed registered deeds, with a confidence grade and refusal to estimate where comparables are thin.

An **affordability and total-cost stress test** should sit alongside these four as a calculator rather than a property score. It should be presented as arithmetic, not advice. Published indices are unstable: Knight Frank's NCR EMI-to-income ratio of 67% for H1 2026 cannot be compared with its earlier 28% figure ([Business Standard, Jul 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/ahmedabad-tops-affordability-chart-mmr-ncr-stay-beyond-50-emi-threshold-126070300550_1.html)).

Global experience shows both the power of such scores and the backlash they provoke. In Redfin's randomised trial with 17.5 million users, buyers shown flood scores **bid on homes with 54% less flood risk**, with no effect at the low-risk end ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/redfin-users-interact-with-flood-risk-data/)). When every viewer was treated, high-risk homes' prices fell about 1.7% ([NBER w33119](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33119/w33119.pdf)). Zillow removed First Street's climate scores from listings in late 2025. Agents and the California Regional MLS (CRMLS) objected that predictions showing a 50% chance of flooding this year had gone unchanged for five years in places that had not flooded in decades ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-removes-climate-risk-data/)). Redfin kept the scores and let sellers request removal of information they believed was inaccurate ([HousingWire, Dec 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/redfin-climate-risk-transparency/)). Realtor.com and Redfin dropped or declined crime data in 2021 over bias concerns ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2021/12/13/redfin-calls-on-real-estate-websites-to-drop-crime-data/)). That is reason enough for an NCR platform to avoid area-level safety scores, which in India could also proxy caste or religion. The UK route is mandatory "material information" disclosure plus pass or "further action" searches ([National Trading Standards](https://www.nationaltradingstandards.uk/news/full-material-information-guidance-published/); [Groundsure via Severn Trent](https://www.severntrentsearches.com/residential-conveyancing-searches/flood-risk-searches/groundsure-flood-report/)). That format suits legal risk better than a score does.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership and paperwork (registry, OC integrity, dues, transfer charges) | Deal-breaker | Good at project level; unit level needs documents plus a lawyer | High | **v1**: project flags plus document checklist |
| Flood and waterlogging (society level) | Recurring and severe | Medium: incidents, terrain, drains, resident reports | High | **v1**: evidence-led bands |
| Society and running costs | Recurring cost and quality of life | Medium; improves as users contribute | Medium | **v1** for ready and resale |
| Price fairness | Largest rupee decision | Medium to good with licensed deeds | Medium | **v1**: range with confidence |
| Affordability stress test | High in NCR | Very good (user inputs) | Low | **v1** as calculator |
| Resale liquidity, rental yield | Medium | Poor until listing history accumulates | Medium | v2; proxies in v1 |
| Predictive construction quality | High | Poor (sporadic audits) | High (defamation) | Known-issue flags only, e.g. Chintels Paradiso's unsafe towers ([The Realty Today](https://therealtytoday.com/news/gurugrams-chintels-paradiso-declared-unsafe-demolition-of-all-towers-announced/)) and the 65+ Gurugram high-rises reporting structural faults ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/over-65-gurugram-high-rises-report-structural-anomalies-584072)) |
| AQI, noise, landfill, power lines, heat, seismic zone | Moderate; barely varies within NCR | Mixed | Medium | Distance-based facts, not a composite |
| Area crime or "safety" | Contested | Thin and biased | High | Do not build |

These should be separate named assessments: "Delivery risk", "Ownership and paperwork", "Flood and waterlogging", "Society and running costs" and "Price fairness". They should not be folded into one composite "credit score". Each has a different evidence base, horizon and failure mode. A single number would hide which risk drives it and would be harder to defend.

## Defamation, not SEBI, is the legal risk that matters

### Courts protect factual, temperate criticism but can grant takedowns fast

Criminal defamation survives in India as BNS s.356. It carries up to two years' simple imprisonment. Its exceptions include true statements for the public good and cautions given in good faith ([Urban Rights Legal, s.356](https://urbanrightslegal.com/laws/bns/section-356)). A Supreme Court judge remarked in September 2025 that it may be "time… to decriminalise". The remark has no legal effect while the 2016 *Subramanian Swamy* judgment stands ([Medianama, Sep 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-supreme-court-observation-decriminalising-defamation-journalism-india/)). In civil defamation, truth alone is a defence, and "statements based on factual analysis cannot be categorized as defamatory" ([Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2021/07/corporate-defamation-a-perspective-on-analyst-reports/)).

Recent rulings favour the platform's intended posture. In *Jindal Power v. ICRA* (2020) the Delhi High Court treated a credit rating as an expert opinion, open to challenge only if "perverse, arbitrary and mala fide" ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/54995907/)). In *Bloomberg v. Zee* (March 2024) the Supreme Court set aside an ex parte takedown order. It held that pre-trial injunctions against published content are for exceptional cases, and warned that they can be misused in SLAPP suits ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/106783348/)). In *Shahed Kamal v. A. Surti Developers* (April 2025) the Supreme Court quashed a builder's criminal defamation complaint against homebuyers whose banner listed grievances in "mild and temperate language" and avoided words like "fraud". The Court held that consumers' right to protest matches the seller's right to commercial speech ([Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/peaceful-protest-by-consumer-a-corresponding-right-to-commercial-speech-by-seller-2025-insc-502-shahed-kamal-v-a-surti-developers-pvt-ltd-1574491); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/libel-defamation/1615158/homebuyers-victory-supreme-court-quashes-builders-defamation-claim)). And in *Lodha v. Krishnaraj Rao* (2019) the Bombay High Court refused Lodha an injunction in its ₹100 crore suit over YouTube videos questioning its construction quality ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/lodha-defamation-case-judge-gautam-patel-strikes-down-injunction-request/57039.html)).

The risk still runs the other way too. Another Lodha company obtained an interim order in a ₹15 crore suit stopping a Goan activist's statements from being "further… disseminated" ([The Goan](https://www.thegoan.net/goa-news/%C3%AF%C2%BB%C2%BFrealty-firm-files-rs-15-cr-defamation-suit-against-activist/147924.html)). The Delhi High Court ordered 44 more videos removed in the Isha Foundation suit in August 2026 ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/08/03/delhi-high-court-directs-google-nakkheeran-remove-44-additional-videos-isha-foundation-defamation-suit/)). MouthShut collected 790+ takedown notices and 11 court cases by 2013 ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2013/08/223-mouthshut-coms-tally-over-790-takedown-notices-240-legal-notices-11-court-cases/)). NCR developers will most likely sue in Delhi, and criminal complaints can name founders personally. No Indian case on an algorithmic developer or project score was found, so this would be a novel fact pattern.

### Calling it a "credit rating" invites regulators; calling it a "risk score" does not

SEBI regulates CRAs that rate securities "listed or proposed to be listed". A credit rating is an opinion on timely repayment of debt ([SEBI CRA FAQs, Mar 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/mar-2026/1772445490055.pdf)). A homebuyer-facing project or developer score is not that, so SEBI registration does not appear to be triggered. Three boundaries need watching. SEBI reserves its standard rating symbols (AAA to D) and bars even CRAs' own non-rating units from using its scales ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/sebi-directs-credit-rating-agencies-to-up-firewall-with-non-rating-entities-122092101169_1.html)). The perimeter can also widen, as it did when SEBI brought ESG ratings under registration in 2023 ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/sebi-introduces-framework-for-esg-rating-providers/)). And selling developer analytics to NBFCs or funds, or scoring listed developers in investment terms, could bring the product close to the CRA or research-analyst rules. Incumbents call their products "grading", not "rating". The safe vocabulary is "Delivery Risk Score", "Project Risk Grade" or "Developer Track-Record Index", with a plain disclaimer: "not a credit rating under SEBI regulations; not issued or endorsed by any RERA". "Credit score" is fine as a marketing analogy. Whether the Credit Information Companies Act limits its use as a product name was not researched and needs a lawyer.

Consumer law cuts both ways. If the platform trades with developers, a developer could argue that a wrong negative score gives "false or misleading facts disparaging the goods, services or trade of another person", an unfair trade practice under CPA 2019 s.2(47)(j) ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/117738049/)). Toward buyers, assurance language such as "safe", "verified" or "guaranteed on time" invites complaints under the CCPA's misleading-advertisement guidelines. Penalties run up to ₹10 lakh, or ₹50 lakh for repeat contraventions, and the guidelines bar disclaimers that hide material information ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1832906&reg=48&lang=2)). There is no safe harbour to fall back on. The score, its labels and AI-written summaries are the platform's own publications, so the intermediary protection in IT Act s.79 does not apply ([Indian Kanoon, s.79](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/844026/)).

On data protection, the DPDP Rules notified in November 2025 phase in fully by May 2027 ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014&reg=3&lang=2)). Section 8(3) requires data used for decisions to be complete, accurate and consistent ([DPDPA s.8](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html)). In June 2026 the Delhi High Court ordered search engines to de-index name-based results for cases that ended in acquittal, discharge or quashing ([SCC Online, Jun 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/06/03/del-hc-verdict-on-de-indexing-masking-and-right-to-be-forgotten/)). Score companies and projects, not individuals, and never surface such cases against named promoters.

### Taking developer money turns a credibility problem into a legal one

Issuer-pays is the recurring cause of rating failure, from the 2008 crisis to IL&FS. In the IL&FS case, IL&FS group entities held about 5–9% of CARE between 2007 and 2013 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pti-stories/il-fs-case-sebi-widens-probe-into-role-of-rating-agencies-119072100157_1.html)). Developer revenue is also direct defamation exposure. If competing developers who pay the platform score better, a downgraded developer can argue malice, which defeats the good-faith and fair-comment defences. SEBI's rules for research analysts provide a voluntary benchmark: disclose financial interests and compensation from the companies covered ([TaxGuru, Dec 2024](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-research-analysts-amendment-regulations-2024.html)).

This collides with the earlier platform research, which recommended developer channel-partner commissions and RERA agent registration as early revenue. An agent is paid by the developer and is legally bound not to give false information about projects ([IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/rera-agent-registration)). Those duties sit uncomfortably beside publishing a low score on the same developer's other project.

Each mitigation below maps onto a legal defence or a regulator's expectation. Wording for all of them needs an Indian advocate's review before launch.

| Mitigation | What it protects |
|---|---|
| Publish the methodology, data sources, refresh schedule and version number | Shows the score is not "perverse, arbitrary or mala fide" (*Jindal Power*); supports fair comment |
| Separate "facts from public records (linked)" from "our assessment" | Fact/opinion defence; RERA's "not misleading" condition |
| Seven days' notice and right of reply before a move into the bottom two bands; developer response shown beside the score | Good faith; mirrors CRA and iCIRT practice |
| Dispute process: acknowledge in 48 hours, decide in 15 working days, publish dispute statistics, as Trustpilot does ([Trustpilot Trust Report 2025](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/trust/trust-report-2025)) | Accuracy duties under DPDP s.8(3); good faith |
| "Insufficient data" or "developer not cooperating" labels instead of silence or guesses, following SEBI's "issuer not cooperating" practice | Avoids misleading middle scores |
| Temperate language: never "fraud", "scam" or "blacklist" | *Shahed Kamal* standard |
| Media-liability insurance covering defamation and product disparagement ([BimaKavach](https://www.bimakavach.com/blog/media-liability-insurance-guide/)) | Defence costs |
| Evidence vault for every score; counsel template ready that cites *Bloomberg v. Zee* | Resisting ex parte takedowns |
| Scoring walled off from sales; no developer can pay to change, suppress or preview a score | Defeats malice and bias claims |

## Show five bands, a natural frequency and four sourced reasons, not a 712

### Format: bands with published thresholds, framed as "out of 100 similar projects"

A three-digit number on the CIBIL model invites false precision ("why is mine 712 and theirs 718?") that a model trained on a few thousand projects with few failures cannot support. AAA-style letters look regulated. Five stars carry the "reviews" mental model and its skewed distribution ([CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/research/overcoming-the-j-shaped-distribution-of-product-reviews/)). The better pattern is a short ordinal scale with named bands and published thresholds. Walk Score publishes its band boundaries and method ([Walk Score methodology](https://www.walkscore.com/methodology.shtml)), and Redfin's flood scale pairs numbers with words from "minimal" to "extreme" ([Redfin methodology](https://www.redfin.com/guides/climate-change-housing-impact/methodology)). For delivery, the scale would be "Low / Moderate / Elevated / High / Very high delivery risk".

Each band should carry a reference-class **natural frequency**, for example "of 100 comparable NCR projects since 2017, about 30 got their OC more than 12 months late", shown as a 10×10 icon array. Natural frequencies are easier to reason with than conditional probabilities, and icon arrays improve accuracy for low- and high-numeracy readers alike ([Bodemer & Gaissmaier](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/18c766fd-ed55-4bb0-8405-27b54a15bf08/download); [Galesic et al.](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2099767_4/component/file_2562291/content); [NCBI evidence review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK572436/)). The frequency framing also defends better in court than a label, because it states a fact about a class of projects rather than an accusation about one developer. Words should never appear without numbers: in one study, "unlikely" was read as anything from 5% to 50% ([Budescu et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275118595_The_Interpretation_of_IPCC_Probabilistic_Statements_Around_the_World)). Uncertainty should be explicit. Weather forecasts that included uncertainty produced better decisions and *preserved* trust when they missed ([Joslyn & LeClerc, 2012](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/xap-18-1-126.pdf)). Traffic-light colours can work as a secondary cue ([PMC8491916](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8491916/)), but never alone, and never red for "insufficient data".

### Explanation: four reasons, each tied to a document, plus what would change the band

Following the Regulation B norm, show the top three or four drivers. Each should be specific, should be a factor the model actually scored, and should link to its source. Good examples read "QPR shows 41% construction vs 78% of time elapsed (HARERA QPR, Jun 2026)", "₹90 crore of unexecuted RERA recovery certificates against the group" or "2 group projects more than 24 months past their RERA date". Add counterfactuals, "the smallest change to the world" that would flip the outcome ([Wachter, Mittelstadt & Russell](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3063289)). For developers this means public-record actions, such as "file QPRs on time for two consecutive quarters" or "obtain OC for Tower B". That turns the score into an incentive and lowers the temperature of disputes. Confidence needs three explicit states: scored with high confidence, scored with low confidence (with a wide range shown), and not scored. A developer with no completed RERA projects gets "not scored — no delivery record yet", not a middling band. Credit Karma's long struggle to explain why its score differs from lenders' FICO scores ([Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/credit/i/is-credit-karma-accurate)) argues for printing the model name and version on every score.

### Governance: no stale scores, logged changes, published accuracy

The CRMLS attack on Zillow worked because predictions "had been in place for almost five years, with no updates" ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-removes-climate-risk-data/)). Zillow, by contrast, publishes the Zestimate's error rates ([The Close](https://theclose.com/how-accurate-is-zillow-zestimate/)). RBI's FREE-AI framework asks that "every automated decision can be understood and justified" and calls for change control and independent validation ([RBI FREE-AI](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/PublicationReport/Pdfs/FREEAIR130820250A24FF2D4578453F824C72ED9F5D5851.PDF)). The spec below adapts SEBI CRA practice, iCIRT, First Street's quarterly-data/annual-model cadence and FREE-AI.

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Primary display | Five named bands with published numeric thresholds; internal 1–100 index for sorting only |
| Probability framing | Reference-class natural frequency plus icon array; rounded to the nearest 5–10 points |
| Reasons | At most four, ranked by contribution relative to well-scored peers; each linked to a dated public record |
| Confidence | High, low (range shown) or not scored, with a stated reason |
| Facts vs opinion | "Public records" panel separate from "Our assessment (Methodology vX.Y)" |
| Freshness | "RERA data synced N days ago" badge, as with Bayut's TruCheck ([MyBayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/expert-advice-trucheck-bayut/)); scores marked stale after a set period without refresh |
| Event-driven updates | Recalculate within 24–72 hours of a new QPR, RERA/NCLT order, OC or petition; log what changed and why |
| Methodology changes | At most quarterly; public changelog with before/after score distribution |
| Watch state | "On watch" when a material event is pending, borrowed from CRA watch practice, to avoid sudden top-to-bottom jumps |
| Alerts | Saved-project users and the developer notified together when a band changes |
| Validation | Quarterly calibration table ("of projects rated High in 2027, X% were >12 months late"); annual independent review |
| Oversight | Small methodology committee with minutes; external statistician; founders recused where conflicted |

## Recommended v1: hard gates, two linked models and a published calibration table

**Predict an outcome a buyer would recognise.** The primary target should be *material delay*: OC or CC granted more than 12 months after the completion date in the project's first RERA registration, after removing blanket regulator extensions. The secondary target should be *stall*: registration revoked, de-registered or lapsed with construction incomplete; CIRP admitted; or no reported physical progress for four consecutive quarters. The 12-month threshold is a judgement. It sits just below the 14-month pre-RERA mean delay, so it separates ordinary slippage from delay that costs buyers rent plus EMI. It should be tested against 6- and 24-month alternatives in the backtest.

**Start in Gurugram, then add Noida with low-confidence badges.** Gurugram has the outcome labels: HARERA's OC/CC records cross-checked against DTCP. Noida's labels depend on authority completion-certificate data that is stale or manual. Noida projects should launch in the "low confidence" state until those labels improve.

The architecture has six layers, with the explanation categories mapped to developer, finances, timeline, legal and site:

| Layer | Purpose | Key inputs | Method | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Entity graph | Tie each project to its promoter, SPVs and group | HARERA corporate IDs, UP RERA promoter names, MCA director links, name variants | Rules, fuzzy matching, manual review | Group ID per project |
| 1. Hard gates (weak links) | Stop a good average hiding a disqualifying fact | Insolvency admitted against promoter or group SPV; registration revoked, de-registered, abeyance or sale ban; rating D or "issuer not cooperating"; authority sealing or plan refusal; large unexecuted RCs; two or more quarters of QPR default | Deterministic rules, as in UP-RERA × CRISIL deflators and caps | Band floor at High or Very high; "On watch" |
| 2. Developer Track-Record Index | Score the sponsor | Past OC vs original RERA date across projects; live delayed projects; complaints and execution cases per 100 units; unexecuted RCs; insolvency petitions; rating and rating moves; MCA charges, net worth, auditor remarks; pipeline vs area delivered | Monotonic scorecard; slow decay of history | Developer band, reasons, confidence |
| 3. Project Delivery-Risk model | Estimate P(material delay) and P(stall) | Cash vs concrete (share collected or sold minus share built); escrow withdrawals vs cost incurred; non-project withdrawals; cost revisions; EDC dues; project-specific extensions; QPR gaps; progress vs time elapsed; satellite inactivity; land tenure and authority dues; licence validity; litigation; size, launch year, segment; developer index | Public model: weight-of-evidence logistic scorecard with monotonic bins. Internal challenger: gradient boosting or time-to-OC survival model | Probability, band, top four reasons |
| 4. Calibration and display | Turn probabilities into honest bands | Backtest frequencies by band | Band thresholds set on realised rates; isotonic recalibration | Five bands, icon array, confidence state |
| 5. Rescue module (stalled projects only) | Estimate the chance of possession within N years | Stage of completion, buyer organisation, land and dues status, resolution applicant, SWAMIH or NBCC involvement | Separate model or expert rules | Separate panel, never blended into the band |

**Use an interpretable scorecard for the public score.** A boosted model will likely rank projects a little better, but the public score should still be the logistic scorecard, for three reasons. The data is a low-default portfolio, where complex models overfit. The scorecard yields Regulation B-style reason codes that are exact rather than approximations. And a court weighing whether a score is "arbitrary" will understand a published scorecard more easily. The boosted or survival model runs alongside as a challenger. If it consistently disagrees with the scorecard on a project, that project gets human review.

**Starting category weights**, before fitting, are a judgement drawn from the evidence tiers above. They should be published at category level, FICO-style, and replaced by fitted weights once labels allow.

| Explanation category | Starting weight | Strongest signals |
|---|---|---|
| Finances (cash vs concrete) | ~30% | Collections or sales ahead of construction; non-project escrow withdrawals; EDC and authority dues; subvention or flexi plans |
| Developer | ~30% | Delivery record; distress (ratings, defaults, petitions, unexecuted RCs); concurrent pipeline |
| Timeline and progress | ~20% | Progress vs time elapsed; project-specific extensions; missed QPRs; satellite inactivity; launch year; size |
| Legal, land and approvals | ~15% | Authority lease vs freehold; dues and sealing; licence lapse; title, acquisition or environmental litigation |
| Site and environment | ~5% | Stalled-project density nearby; distress within 5 km; GRAP exposure (near-uniform across NCR) |

**Validate before showing bands, and publish the result.** Train on projects whose original completion dates fall in or before 2022 and test on outcomes from 2023 to 2026. Report AUC and accuracy ratio with bootstrap confidence intervals, which will be wide, and realised delay rates for each band. Check whether the model would have flagged Supertech, Jaypee, Ansal, Raheja, Vatika, 3C and Earth one to three years ahead, and compare it with CRA ratings where they exist. Band thresholds should then be set on realised rates. As an illustration, Low could mean fewer than 10% of comparable projects materially delayed, Moderate 10–25%, Elevated 25–40%, High 40–60%, and Very high above 60% or any hard gate. Those cut-offs are placeholders until the backtest fixes them. One limitation stands out. HARERA's QPR history starts only in 2021, so the deepest cash-flow features have the shortest outcome history. Early weights will lean on registration-form data, complaints and developer history more than on QPR dynamics.

**Roll out in three steps.** First, publish facts-first project pages: the public-records panel, linked documents and freshness badges, with no band. This is closest to MahaRERA's disclosure matrix and carries the least legal risk. It also starts the permission conversations with HARERA and UP RERA. Second, after the backtest and a lawyer's review, add the developer index and project bands for Gurugram, with the right-of-reply process live. Third, extend to Noida and Greater Noida under low-confidence badges, together with the ownership-and-paperwork checklist, where registry risk dominates.

**Score every in-scope project.** Do not adopt iCIRT's "publish only passes" design. Steelmanned, gating negatives does reduce defamation exposure, and iCIRT uses it for exactly that reason. But it destroys public value where buyers need it most, invites developers to misrepresent their ratings, and contradicts SEBI's practice of rating issuers that do not cooperate. The better compromise is to score everything and to lead with facts in the bottom bands, behind the seven-day reply window.

**Keep the sibling assessments separate and cheap.** "Ownership and paperwork" is a green/amber/red checklist built from the same authority, RERA and DTCP records. "Flood and waterlogging" starts as evidence-led bands from dated incidents. "Society and running costs" covers ready and resale societies, seeded from public audit and compliance lists. "Price fairness" reuses the AVM range from the earlier research. The affordability calculator sits beside them. None of these feeds the delivery band, and each gets its own page, sources and dispute route. The whole v1 costs about ₹1–1.5 lakh a year in paid data plus six to ten weeks of solo engineering, before legal fees and insurance.

## Open questions the v1 leaves

| Open question | Why it matters | Fastest way to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| How many HARERA projects have both an original RERA date and an OC/CC date to train on? | Determines whether weights can be fitted or must stay expert-set | Collect corrigenda and DTCP BR-VII/LC-IX data; count labels by launch year |
| Can Noida outcome labels and authority dues be obtained reliably? | Tier-1 predictor and label source in the riskiest market | RTI requests to Noida, GNIDA and YEIDA; test UP RERA QPR access in a headless browser without CAPTCHAs |
| Will HARERA and UP RERA grant reuse permission, and on what terms? | The "derogatory manner or misleading context" clause and UP RERA's linking rule | Write to both authorities now; get a lawyer's view on s.52(1)(q)(iv) for RERA orders |
| Does the scorecard beat simple rules out of sample? | Few failures can make any model look good in-sample | Out-of-time backtest; compare with a "two Tier-1 signals" rule and with CRA ratings |
| Should the platform take developer channel-partner commissions at all? | Malice arguments, credibility and RERA agent duties all hinge on it | Model unit economics with and without CP revenue; consider buyer-paid reports, bank referral and resale brokerage instead |
| Build or buy the developer layer? | Liases Foras already sells developer and project risk ratings | Request a quote, sample output and methodology before building |
| Do MCA charge filings and auditor remarks add early warning? | Plausible but untested in Indian real estate | Add to the backtest as a feature family; measure lift |
| How do NCR buyers read bands, frequencies and Hindi labels? | No Indian evidence on score formats | A/B test band vs number vs frequency framing in the product |
| What exact wording, insurance and terminology clear legal review? | Defamation, CPA s.2(47)(j), the Credit Information Companies Act on "credit score", RERA agent duties | One legal memo before the second rollout step; media-liability quotes |
| How should the score treat 2026 blanket extensions and GRAP stoppages? | Mislabelled delays distort both training and display | Encode regulator extensions as dated events; test sensitivity of labels |
| Will developers organise against it, as agents did against Zillow? | The Zillow/CRMLS episode shows rated parties fight back | Publish methodology and backtest before bands go live; brief CREDAI chapters and homebuyer groups in advance |

## Conclusion

The research changes what the score is for. NCR buyers already pay for a proxy: 54% will pay up to a 20% premium for an established brand ([Constro Facilitator, citing Housing.com](https://constrofacilitator.com/how-organised-trust-led-developers-will-shape-indias-housing-growth-in-the-next-decade/)). Brand is roughly right for the top listed names and silent about everyone else. China's sales-rank evidence and the listed collapses of Unitech and Jaypee show it can be dangerously wrong. The score's real value lies in the large middle tier of NCR developers that have no rating and no national brand. For them, the gap between cash collected and concrete built is visible in public QPRs years before insolvency, and nobody is reading it. That shifts the competitive frame. The score does not compete with rating agencies. It competes with the brand premium, and it can lower the cost of trusting a smaller developer whose filings are clean.

The second shift is that accuracy and legal defensibility turn out to be the same engineering problem. Every design choice that makes the score more predictive also makes it easier to defend: tying each reason to a dated public record, calibrating bands to observed frequencies, refreshing on every filing, and publishing backtests. The two threats to the score are not technical. One is commercial dependence on the developers being scored. The other is going stale. A platform that solves both turns NCR's fragmented RERA disclosures into the track-record check that homebuyer groups have asked regulators for and regulators have twice failed to deliver.
